Japan: APPI Amendment Bill — Cabinet Approved, Diet Pending

Jurisdiction:     Japan
Instrument:       Proposed amendment to the Act on the Protection of Personal Information (APPI)
Issuing body:     Japanese Cabinet (approved 2026-04-07); now before the Diet (bicameral legislature)
Published:        2026-04-07 (Cabinet approval)
Expected enactment: 2026 Q3–Q4 (estimated, subject to Diet schedule)
Operative date for businesses: approximately 2028 (18-month grace period post-enactment is standard for APPI cycles)
Primary sources:  https://www.morihamada.com/en/insights/newsletters/138006 (Mori Hamada — SECONDARY)
                  https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/05/japan-appi-reform-key-changes (Baker McKenzie — SECONDARY)
                  https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/japanese-cabinet-approves-appi-amendments (Fisher Phillips — SECONDARY)
                  PENDING PRIMARY CONFIRMATION from PPC (ppc.go.jp) — WebFetch blocked; confirm via counsel.

What changed:
The 2026 APPI amendment introduces a risk-based framework that moves away from a uniform consent-driven
model. The engineering-relevant changes are: (a) a new consent exemption for statistical processing and
AI-training on publicly available and third-party-sourced data (subject to transparency safeguards);
(b) mandatory parental consent for children under 16; (c) enhanced rights for minors (deletion/
suspension of data use); (d) higher fines aligned with turnover. For cookies and tracking, the existing
Telecommunications Business Act External Data Transmission Rule (third-party cookie notification /
opt-out, in force since June 2023) is NOT amended by this bill. Third-party cookies transmitted to
parties that link them to identified individuals continue to require notification or consent.

ID-PRIVACY relevance:
No immediate platform change required. Monitor for: (a) enactment date (Diet vote expected Q3 2026);
(b) whether any implementing PPC rules narrow or expand the External Data Transmission consent-or-
notification obligation for third-party cookies; (c) children-data consent gate for tenants operating
apps targeting Japanese under-16 users (will require an age-verification / parental-consent flow
once operative ~2028).

Promote to MEDIUM if: Diet enacts the bill and PPC publishes implementing rules touching the
External Data Transmission Rule or CMP consent flows.

Effort if promoted: est. 8–13 pts (children consent gate for JP locale; parental-consent sub-flow).

South Korea: PIPA Amendment — Signed 2026-03-10, Effective 2026-09-11

Jurisdiction:     South Korea
Instrument:       Amendment to the Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA)
Issuing body:     National Assembly (passed 2026-02-12); signed 2026-03-10
Published:        2026-03-10 (signing)
Effective:        2026-09-11 (T-minus 119 days from today)
Enforcement:      Immediate from 2026-09-11
Primary sources:  https://iapp.org/news/a/south-korea-overhauls-pipa-and-ties-fines-to-ceo-accountability (IAPP — SECONDARY)
                  https://korea.acclime.com/news/data-protection-law-fines-accountability/ (Acclime — SECONDARY)
                  PENDING PRIMARY CONFIRMATION from PIPC (pipc.go.kr) — WebFetch blocked; confirm via counsel.

What changed:
The 2026 PIPA amendment makes four governance changes: (a) raises the maximum administrative fine to
10% of total turnover (from KRW 300M or 3% thresholds); (b) designates the CEO as personally
responsible for data protection compliance; (c) requires board approval and PIPC reporting for the
appointment, reassignment, or dismissal of the CPO at large organisations; (d) introduces a mandatory
ISMS-P certification obligation for large-scale data controllers from 1 July 2027. The amendment does
NOT introduce new cookie-consent mechanisms, new opt-out signal obligations, or new banner requirements.
Existing PIPA consent obligations for third-party cookie tracking remain unchanged.

ID-PRIVACY relevance:
No platform code or configuration change required. Tenants operating in South Korea should be advised
of the increased fine exposure and CEO accountability obligations — these are organisational / legal
matters, not CMP matters. The ISMS-P certification requirement (2027-07-01) may create a documentation
demand that touches the consent-record and audit-log exports the platform generates; flag for the
Documentation / RoPA team to ensure Korean audit-export templates are ISMS-P-compatible.

Promote to MEDIUM if: PIPC publishes implementing guidance that introduces new technical consent or
tracking obligations tied to the amendment (none signalled to date).

Effort if promoted: est. 3 pts (KR audit-log export template update for ISMS-P compatibility).

                  https://www.oaic.gov.au/news/media-centre/oaic-releases-exposure-draft-of-the-childrens-online-privacy-code

                  https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2026/04/australia-exposure-draft-of-childrens-online-privacy-code-signals-tougher-standards/
                  https://www.corrs.com.au/insights/oaics-childrens-online-privacy-exposure-draft-from-consultation-to-code

What changed:
The OAIC published an exposure draft of the Children's Online Privacy Code, crystallising how the
Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) — as amended by the Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024 — applies
to services likely to be accessed by children. Key provisions: (a) prohibition on using children's
personal data for targeted advertising without express consent; (b) children under 15 cannot provide
consent — must be obtained from a parent/guardian with "reasonably confirmed" parental responsibility;
(c) mandatory Privacy Impact Assessments (PIAs) for high-risk processing involving children, plus a PIA
register; (d) data-minimisation and children's-best-interests obligations. Scope: providers of social
media services, relevant electronic services, and designated internet services (Online Safety Act 2021)
likely accessed by children — excludes health services.

ID-PRIVACY relevance:
Monitor closely. If the Code is registered by December 2026 and commences in 2027, it will require:
(a) a parental-consent sub-flow for Australian services with child audiences; (b) age-signal / child-
audience flag in the Geo/Jurisdiction routing module for AU; (c) prohibition on routing AU child-
audience traffic through advertising vendor SDKs without parental consent; (d) PIA documentation
template for AU child-data processing. Consult close: 2026-06-05 — Ajit to decide whether
DataSafeguard should submit a response (has product implications for the platform).

Promote to HIGH once the Code is registered (expected 2026-12-10) and commencement date is set.

Effort if promoted: est. 13–21 pts (parental-consent flow, AU child audience routing, PIA template,
vendor ad-SDK gating for AU child-audience tenants).

EU: Digital Omnibus — Proposed GDPR Articles 88a / 88b (Cookie and Consent-Signal Rules)

Jurisdiction:     European Union / EEA
Instrument:       European Commission proposal — Digital Omnibus package; proposed GDPR Articles 88a and 88b
Issuing body:     European Commission (published 2025-11-19); Council and Parliament in early-stage review
Published:        2025-11-19 (Commission proposal)
Council general approach (GDPR/ePrivacy portions): Not yet adopted as of 2026-05-15
Operative date (if adopted as proposed): Article 88a — 6 months after entry into force;
                                          Article 88b — 24 months after entry into force
Earliest realistic operative date:        Late 2027 (Art. 88a) / Late 2028 (Art. 88b)
Primary sources:  https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/digital-package (Commission — SECONDARY pending primary EUR-Lex confirmation)
Secondary refs:   https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/global-data-hub/2026/the-digital-omnibus-proposal/gdh---the-digital-omnibus---cookies
                  https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/digital-omnibus-reshapes-eu-cookie-rules-leaves-banner-fatigue-largely-intact
                  https://www.loyensloeff.com/insights/news--events/news/digital-omnibus-what-the-proposed-changes-mean-for-gdpr-privacy-and-cookies/
                  https://noyb.eu/en/digital-omnibus-report-v3-analysis-select-gdpr-and-eprivacy-proposals-commission

Note: The AI Omnibus portion of the Digital Omnibus package reached provisional trilogue agreement
on 2026-05-07; the GDPR/ePrivacy cookie provisions (Art. 88a/88b) have NOT yet progressed to that
stage and remain at proposal level.

What changed:
Article 88a would integrate cookie-consent rules directly into the GDPR, replacing the ePrivacy
Directive for personal-data-touching tracking. Key provisions as proposed: (a) granular, prior,
withdrawable consent required before cookies are set; (b) rejection of consent must be possible "with
a single-click button or equivalent means"; (c) once a user has refused, the same purpose consent
cannot be requested again for at least 6 months; (d) Article 88b would mandate legal recognition of
browser-level consent signals (GPC-type signals get statutory standing). If adopted as proposed, this
would codify the 6-month re-consent restriction (currently a CNIL/Garante soft requirement) across all
EU/EEA DPAs and make GPC-style signals legally binding across the EU.

ID-PRIVACY relevance (horizon planning):
If Art. 88a/88b are adopted, the following ID-PRIVACY changes will be required: (a) enforce 6-month
re-consent lockout in the EU/EEA ruleset (already optionally configurable; would become mandatory);
(b) browser consent signal (GPC / Art. 88b signal) must be honoured as a valid EU consent refusal —
this is architecturally significant as it would make GPC a consent-layer signal, not just a CCPA opt-
out; (c) single-click rejection on the first layer is already required in FR/DE/IT — would become
universal across EU/EEA; (d) ePrivacy Directive would no longer be the governing instrument for
personal-data cookies, simplifying the dual-basis analysis.

No engineering action required now. Promote to MEDIUM when Council adopts a general approach on the
GDPR/ePrivacy provisions. Promote to HIGH when Parliament and Council agree a trilogue text.

Effort if promoted to MEDIUM: est. 5 pts (ruleset review, lockout enforcement, GPC-as-consent mapping).
Effort if promoted to HIGH: est. 21–34 pts (cross-jurisdiction GPC consent signal integration, 6-month
lockout enforcement across all EU tenants, legal review of consent-without-banner flows).

Brazil: ANPD Becomes Formally Independent (Law 15.352/2026) — Advertising Enforcement Priority

Jurisdiction:     Brazil
Instrument:       Law 15.352/2026 — ANPD formal independence; ANPD 2026–2027 enforcement priorities
Issuing body:     Brazilian federal government / ANPD
Published:        February 2026 (Law 15.352/2026 enacted)
Effective:        Immediately upon enactment
Primary sources:  https://www.gov.br/anpd/ (ANPD — SECONDARY pending primary Diario Oficial confirmation)
Secondary refs:   https://www.montaury.com.br/en/brazil-s-data-protection-authority-becomes-a-regulatory-agency-what-changes-now
                  https://iclg.com/practice-areas/data-protection-laws-and-regulations/brazil

What changed:
Law 15.352/2026 formally transformed the ANPD from a government-adjacent body into a fully independent
regulatory agency with functional, technical, decision-making, administrative, and financial autonomy —
similar to the transformation of other Brazilian independent agencies (ANATEL, ANAC, etc.). This does
NOT change the substantive obligations of the LGPD or the ANPD's October 2022 cookie guidance; it
changes the ANPD's enforcement capacity: independent budget, ability to hire specialised staff (200 new
positions announced), and political insulation from executive pressure. The ANPD has announced that
advertising and profiling are explicit 2026–2027 enforcement priorities.

ID-PRIVACY relevance:
No platform change required. The existing BR LGPD ruleset in ID-PRIVACY (consent required for non-
essential cookies; consent = opt-in; legitimate interest = opt-out) remains correct. The risk for
tenants is that the ANPD now has both the will (stated priority) and the means (independence, budget)
to pursue enforcement actions against advertising-cookie non-compliance more aggressively. Tenants
operating in Brazil should be reminded that the ANPD cookie guidance is in force and that the ANPD
has graduated from a toothless advisory body to an active enforcement authority.

Action: Include in the next tenant newsletter / compliance update. No engineering action.

What changed:

disclosure of cookie categories and purposes) is architecturally correct. This item confirms the

deployed a PDPL-compliant banner should be flagged for outreach. No engineering action.

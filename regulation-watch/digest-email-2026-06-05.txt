Jurisdiction: United Kingdom

Effective: 2026-04-29 (DUAA analytics exception already in force 2026-02-05)
Primary source: https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-the-use-of-storage-and-access-technologies/
===============================================================

WHAT CHANGED

The ICO published its final Storage and Access Technologies (SAT) guidance on
29 April 2026, replacing all prior cookies guidance. It finalises three new
consent exemptions introduced by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (DUAA),
which came into force on 5 February 2026:

  (1) Analytics/statistical cookies (aggregate site-improvement stats only)
  (2) Appearance preference cookies
  (3) Emergency-assistance location cookies

For analytics and appearance exemptions, organisations must provide a "simple
and free means of objecting" -- the ICO confirms this requires a visible,
one-step opt-out toggle in the banner itself (not a link to browser settings).
Dual-purpose analytics + advertising cookies do NOT qualify for the exemption.
Guidance scope is also broadened from "cookies" to all storage and access
technologies (pixels, fingerprinting, web storage, tag scripts). PECR maximum
fine is now GBP 17.5 M / 4% global turnover (DUAA-aligned since Feb 2026).

WHAT CHANGED

On 29 May 2026, IAB Europe and IAB Tech Lab jointly announced TCF Policy v5.0.b
and TCF v2.4, advancing from v5.0.a / TCF v2.3. Three substantive changes:

  (1) Multi-device consent handling: New requirements for how consent signals
      propagate when an authenticated user moves across devices, aligned with
      CNIL multi-device guidance. CMPs must propagate TC string across device
      sessions in a disclosed, user-facing manner.

  (2) CMP UI requirements: Chapter V, Appendix B(B)(f) now mandates that CMPs
      display standard explanation text + illustration alongside each Feature
      in the consent layer.

  (3) Special Feature 2 renamed: Previously "Use precise geolocation data and
      actively scan device characteristics for identification," now renamed
      "Identify devices based on information actively requested."

WHAT CHANGED

South Korea's National Assembly passed a PIPA amendment on 12 February 2026,
taking effect six months after promulgation (~12 August 2026). The headline
change is a new maximum administrative fine of 10% of total annual revenue for
the most severe violations (previously 3% of relevant turnover). The amendment
also designates the CEO as the ultimate responsible person for data protection
and expands "data breach" to include forgery, alteration, and destruction
(previously only loss/theft/disclosure). Core consent requirements for cookies
and behavioral tracking are NOT substantively changed.

===============================================================

WHAT CHANGED

A new Information Privacy Principle 3A (IPP 3A) came into force on 1 May 2026
under the Privacy Amendment Act 2025. It requires agencies to notify individuals
when their personal data is collected indirectly (from a third party), disclosing
what was collected, why, who holds it, and the individual's access/correction
rights. Applies only to information collected on or after 1 May 2026. Relevant
where customer websites use third-party data enrichment, audience segmentation,
or data broker integrations feeding into their consent or targeting stack.

===============================================================

WHAT CHANGED

OAIC released an exposure draft of the Privacy (Children's Online Privacy)
Code 2026 on 31 March 2026. Once finalised (must be in force by 10 December
2026), the Code will impose:

  - Scope: Broad -- covers apps, games, websites, streaming, educational tools
    likely accessed by under-18s (not only services targeting children)
  - Age consent threshold: Children under 15 cannot provide valid consent;
    parental/guardian consent required (from someone with "reasonably
    confirmed" parental responsibility)
  - Best-interests principle: Processing must be consistent with child's
    best interests
  - Prohibited: Behavioral profiling of children for marketing
  - Mandatory PIAs for high-risk processing involving children

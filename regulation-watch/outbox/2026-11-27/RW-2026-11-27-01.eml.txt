WHAT CHANGED (≤4 sentences, plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Connecticut's third CTDPA update bars controllers and third parties from selling a consumer's precise geolocation data, regardless of consent. Before, precise geolocation was sensitive data that could be sold with opt-in consent. The same package adds a data-broker registry (registration by 2027-01-01, 45-day deletion-platform checks, central deletion mechanism by 2028-07-01) plus surveillance-pricing and genetic-data provisions. For a CMP, "consent" can no longer legitimise geolocation-sale vendors for CT residents.

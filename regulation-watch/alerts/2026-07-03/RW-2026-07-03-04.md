WHAT CHANGED (≤4 sentences, plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Section 702 of FISA — one of the surveillance authorities the European Commission relied on (alongside Executive Order 14086 and the PCLOB oversight mechanism) when it found the U.S. "adequate" for the EU-US Data Privacy Framework — lapsed in mid-June 2026 after Congress failed to agree on reauthorization terms. In practice, surveillance under existing FISA Court certifications continues unaffected until March 2027 (grandfathering), so there is no immediate operational change to how U.S. companies process EU personal data. However, this compounds an existing, already-flagged concern: the Privacy and Civil Liberties Oversight Board (PCLOB), cited 31 times in the Commission's DPF adequacy decision, has been non-functional since early 2025, and the EDPB has already recommended the Commission closely monitor Section 702 developments in its first periodic DPF review. A pending CJEU appeal (Latombe, Case C-703/25 P) challenging the DPF's adequacy is still awaiting a hearing date. No EU institution has, as of this scan, announced suspension of the DPF adequacy decision.

WHAT CHANGED (≤4 sentences, plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PIPL cross-border transfers can use one of three lawful mechanisms: CAC security assessment, filed Standard Contract, or (new) certification. The CAC's Certification Measures took effect 2026-01-01, formally activating certification as a distinct, auditable pathway. A second wave of supplementary national standards — covering technical controls, encryption benchmarks, access-control specs, audit-logging requirements, and certification-body accreditation criteria — became mandatory on 2026-07-01, 16 days before this scan. Any organization already certified, or planning to certify, cross-border transfers of PRC personal information (including cookie/device identifiers routed to non-PRC vendors/SDKs) must now meet the tightened technical and audit-logging bar.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHO IS AFFECTED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Applicability test: Any data controller/processor transferring personal information (incl. online/device identifiers) collected in mainland China to recipients outside mainland China, where the certification route (rather than security assessment or Standard Contract) is or will be used.
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: Customers with mainland-China-facing properties whose vendor/SDK stack routes analytics, ad-tech, or CDP data outside China. Customers using the Standard Contract filing route (the more common mechanism for mid-tier transfers) are not directly bound by these certification-specific technical standards, but should confirm which mechanism each China-touching vendor actually uses — this is not visible in ID-PRIVACY® today.
- Customer notice required: YES — for any tenant flagged with China-origin traffic and third-party vendor transfers, by 2026-08-15 (allow ~30 days for customers to audit their own transfer-mechanism choice).

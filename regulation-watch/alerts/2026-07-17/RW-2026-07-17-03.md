WHAT CHANGED (≤4 sentences, plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Chile's prior data protection law (19.628, 1999) had no GDPR-style consent standard and no dedicated regulator. Law 21.719 replaces it with a GDPR-aligned regime: consent must be free, informed, specific, prior, and unambiguous, with no implied/pre-ticked consent, and reject must be as easy as accept. It creates Chile's first dedicated data protection authority (Agencia de Protección de Datos Personales), which begins enforcement and sanctions powers on the law's full-effect date. This is the last stretch of a 24-month transition window that started at publication in August 2024.

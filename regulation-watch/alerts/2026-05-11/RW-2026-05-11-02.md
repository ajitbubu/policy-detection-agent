WHAT CHANGED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Prior rule (pre-DUAA PECR): consent was required for all cookies and similar
technologies except those "strictly necessary" for a service the user has
expressly requested (the original two-category exemption). The fine ceiling
for PECR breaches was £500,000. The ICO's previous detailed cookie guidance
predated the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025.

New rule (DUAA commencement 5 February 2026; ICO guidance finalised 29 April
2026): five categories of storage and access technologies are now exempt from
PECR consent under UK law — (1) purely statistical/analytics cookies used
solely to improve the service and not feeding into advertising targeting,
(2) appearance/functionality customisation cookies, (3) security and fraud-
prevention cookies, (4) software update delivery cookies, and (5) emergency
assistance identification cookies. The PECR fine ceiling rose to £17.5M or
4% of global annual turnover (whichever is higher), aligned with UK GDPR.
The ICO also finalized two new guidance chapters: "what does a simple means
of objecting mean?" and "can we use the same storage and access technology
for multiple purposes?" — the latter directly addresses mixed-purpose cookies
that straddle an exempt and a non-exempt use. Scope of PECR is now confirmed
to cover all storage and access technologies (pixels, fingerprinting, web
storage, tag scripts) not just cookies by name.

---------------------------------------------------------------
WHO IS AFFECTED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Applicability test:  Any organisation that sets cookies or uses storage/access
                     technologies on devices of UK-based users, regardless of
                     the organisation's establishment country.
DataSafeguard customers likely affected:  All tenants serving UK traffic —
                     estimated to be the majority of EU/global enterprise
                     tenants plus any UK-domiciled tenant. The change is
                     permissive (reduces consent requirements for some
                     categories) but requires correct classification to
                     unlock the exemptions.
Customer notice required:  YES — by 2026-06-12 (within ~6 weeks). Customers
                     need to know: (a) the new exemptions let them deploy
                     analytics without a consent gate in the UK, and (b) the
                     fine ceiling has risen, making previous risk calculations
                     obsolete.

ID-PRIVACY® IMPACT
---------------------------------------------------------------
Affected subsystems:
  [X] Consent Banner               UK ruleset must reflect the five new exempt
                                   categories. Banners served under the UK
                                   jurisdiction route should not present a
                                   consent gate for cookies that qualify for
                                   an exemption — but only when the cookie is
                                   used solely for the exempted purpose.
                                   The "simple means of objecting" guidance
                                   requires that a non-consent alternative
                                   (e.g., an opt-out link or settings panel)
                                   be available even for exempt categories
                                   that are not strictly necessary. Banners
                                   must not rely on closing/dismissal as
                                   consent (consistent with GDPR but now
                                   explicitly confirmed for PECR too).
  [X] Cookie Classification ML     Classifier must be able to tag cookies/
                                   technologies as falling into one of the
                                   five DUAA exemption categories for the UK
                                   route. A cookie classified as "analytics"
                                   must be further assessed for whether it
                                   also feeds advertising targeting — if so,
                                   it loses the exemption. This mixed-purpose
                                   test requires a new sub-classification or
                                   a purpose-combination flag in the schema.
  [ ] Consent Storage & Proof      No structural change; exempt-category
                                   cookies do not require stored consent, but
                                   the classification decision (including the
                                   sole-purpose determination) should be logged
                                   for audit against future ICO inquiry.
  [ ] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP) Not directly affected; UK TCF/PECR
                                   exemptions are orthogonal to signal handling.
  [X] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing   UK routing rule must be updated to apply
                                   the five new exempt categories separately
                                   from EU rules. A UK visitor to a site that
                                   also serves EU traffic must receive the
                                   DUAA-aware ruleset, not the ePrivacy-only
                                   ruleset (which has no analytics exemption).
  [X] Vendor / SDK Governance      Third-party vendors whose SDKs are purely
                                   analytics may be gated from consent in EU
                                   but can be loaded without consent in UK
                                   (if sole-purpose test passes). Vendor
                                   metadata must capture "exempt under DUAA"
                                   as a distinct attribute. Mixed-purpose
                                   vendor SDKs (analytics + ad targeting) must
                                   remain consent-gated.
  [ ] DSR Engine                   Not directly affected.
  [X] Audit / Logging              Logs should capture which classification
                                   basis was applied (exempt category vs.
                                   consent) and for which jurisdiction rule.
                                   The ICO may ask for evidence of the
                                   sole-purpose determination.
  [ ] Cross-border transfer        Not directly affected by this guidance
                                   item specifically.
  [X] Tenant Config / Admin UI     New toggles needed: per-cookie "DUAA exempt
                                   category" selection; "sole-purpose confirmed"
                                   flag; warning when a cookie marked DUAA-
                                   exempt is also mapped to an advertising
                                   purpose in the vendor list.
  [X] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA  Update UK cookie compliance guide for
                                   tenants. Update DPIA template to reflect
                                   reduced consent scope for exempt categories
                                   and the elevated fine exposure under PECR.

RECOMMENDED ACTIONS
---------------------------------------------------------------
ENGINEERING (Abhishek's track):
  1. Geo/jurisdiction routing: add UK-DUAA ruleset as a distinct layer that
     applies the five exemption categories — separate from the EU/ePrivacy
     ruleset. UK consent banner should not show a consent prompt for cookies
     that satisfy an exemption AND pass the sole-purpose test. Estimated:
     5 pts — by 2026-06-15.
  2. Cookie Classification schema: add a "duaaExemptCategory" field
     (enum: analytics_only | appearance | security | software_update |
     emergency | none) and a "mixedPurpose" boolean to the cookie record.
     Estimated: 3 pts — by 2026-06-08.
  3. Banner logic: for UK route, suppress consent gate for cookies where
     duaaExemptCategory != none AND mixedPurpose == false. Retain an
     opt-out / preference link ("simple means of objecting") in the footer
     or settings panel for all technologies, including exempt ones.
     Estimated: 5 pts — by 2026-06-22.
  4. Vendor governance: update vendor metadata schema to carry the DUAA
     exempt flag. Add a warning in Admin UI when a vendor is flagged
     exempt but also has an "advertising" purpose mapped.
     Estimated: 3 pts — by 2026-06-22.
  5. Logging: extend consent event log to record the classification basis
     (exempt-category name or "user-consent") per cookie/technology.
     Estimated: 2 pts — by 2026-06-22.

ML (Sumeet's track):
  1. Train / fine-tune the cookie classifier to output the five DUAA exempt
     categories as additional labels (beyond the existing IAB taxonomy).
     Focus on distinguishing pure-analytics from analytics-plus-advertising
     mixed use cases — this is the highest-risk classification boundary.
     Estimated: 8 pts — by 2026-07-06.
  2. Create a curated labeled dataset of common analytics cookies/SDKs
     (GA4, Plausible, Fathom, Matomo, Heap, Mixpanel) with sole-purpose
     annotations for training. Estimated: 5 pts (data curation) — by
     2026-06-22.

PRODUCT / LEGAL:
  1. Update UK-tenant onboarding docs and cookie audit workflow to reflect
     the DUAA exemptions and the sole-purpose test requirement.
     By 2026-06-15.
  2. Customer advisory: publish a one-page "UK Cookie Compliance Update —
     May 2026" explaining the new exemptions and how tenants can enable
     the DUAA ruleset in ID-PRIVACY. By 2026-06-12.
  3. Legal review: confirm with UK counsel whether the ICO's "simple means
     of objecting" sub-chapter creates an implied right of opt-out even
     for exempt categories, and how that should be surfaced in the banner.
     By 2026-06-08.
  4. Update PECR fine exposure figure in all risk documents and DPA
     templates from £500,000 to £17.5M / 4% global turnover. By 2026-06-08.

DEPENDENCIES / BLOCKERS:
  - ICO has signalled it may consult further on advertising-cookie relaxation
    in the future; monitor for a second phase of guidance that could expand
    exempt categories. Do not build the analytics exemption in a way that
    requires full re-architecture when advertising cookies are addressed.
  - Primary source (ico.org.uk) is WebFetch-blocked; engineering should
    download and review the full finalized guidance PDF directly before
    implementing classification rules.

---------------------------------------------------------------
RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Two-sided risk: (1) Tenants who are entitled to the analytics exemption are
being forced through unnecessary consent gates, reducing data quality and
creating banner fatigue — competitive disadvantage relative to CMPs that
have implemented DUAA. (2) The PECR fine ceiling is now £17.5M / 4% global
turnover. Any ICO action against a DataSafeguard tenant for improper cookie
use — including failure to classify mixed-purpose cookies correctly — now
carries existential financial risk. The ICO has an active cookie sweep
programme and is auditing major UK websites; updated enforcement notices
citing the 2026-04-29 guidance can be expected within months.

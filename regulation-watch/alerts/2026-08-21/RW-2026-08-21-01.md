WHAT CHANGED (≤4 sentences, plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Korea's PIPA is amended to raise the maximum administrative fine to 10% of a company's total worldwide revenue for willful/grossly-negligent repeat violations, large-scale breaches (≥10M individuals), or non-compliance with a PIPC corrective order — the highest revenue-linked ceiling of any major privacy regime. The statutory definition of a reportable breach is expanded from "loss, theft, or leakage" to also include "forgery, alteration, or damage," and the notification trigger moves earlier: controllers must now notify on "reasonable likelihood" of a breach rather than waiting for confirmation. The amendment also creates personal CEO liability and requires board-level approval/PIPC notice for Chief Privacy Officer appointment, change, or removal. PIPA already applies extraterritorially to any controller processing personal information (including cookie/device identifiers) of individuals in Korea, so this raises real fine and governance exposure for any ID-PRIVACY tenant with Korean site traffic.

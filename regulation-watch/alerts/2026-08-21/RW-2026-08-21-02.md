WHAT CHANGED (≤4 sentences, plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Chile replaces its 1999-era data protection law (Law 19.628, the first in Latin America) with a GDPR-aligned regime, reaching full effect roughly 24 months after its December 2024 publication. The new law adds legal bases beyond consent (contract performance, legitimate interest), tightens rules for sensitive-data processing, and — per multiple compliance trackers — expects cookie consent banners to give accept/reject options with equal visibility, present clear information before any tracking decision, and support withdrawal as easily as consent was given. A new sanctioning authority, the Agencia de Protección de Datos Personales (APDP), is created with real enforcement power, replacing Chile's prior fragmented, largely unenforced regime. This is a new-market compliance requirement rather than an amendment to a rule ID-PRIVACY already serves in Chile today (Chile has not previously required an EU-style cookie banner).

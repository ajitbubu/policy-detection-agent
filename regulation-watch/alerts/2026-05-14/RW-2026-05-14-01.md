WHAT CHANGED (<=4 sentences, plain English)
---------------------------------------------------------------
On April 29, 2026, the ICO published its final, consolidated guidance on storage and access technologies (SATs) --
the UK's definitive interpretation of how PECR and UK GDPR apply to cookies, tracking pixels, device fingerprinting,
and similar technologies. The guidance follows two consultations (December 2024 and July 2025) and incorporates
provisions introduced by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (DUAA), which took effect February 5, 2026 and raised
the PECR fine ceiling from GBP 500,000 to GBP 17.5 million / 4% of global annual turnover. The most operationally
significant changes are: (1) five new consent-exempt categories added by the DUAA (communication, strictly necessary,
statistical purposes, appearance/functionality, and emergency assistance); (2) a new sub-chapter clarifying that a
"simple means of objecting" must be provided on-banner -- not just via browser settings -- for the statistical and
appearance exceptions; and (3) a new sub-chapter clarifying that an SAT used for multiple purposes cannot rely on
a single-purpose exemption and must obtain consent for all purposes that are not independently exempt. Additionally,
the ICO expressly states that affiliate marketing tracking pixels require consent and confirms that "strictly necessary"
is assessed from the user's perspective (not the operator's business need).

---------------------------------------------------------------
WHO IS AFFECTED
---------------------------------------------------------------
- Applicability test:  Any operator deploying a website, app, or service accessible to UK users -- whether
  established in the UK or targeting UK users (PECR has an establishment/equipment test; UK GDPR adds a
  targeting test). This covers the majority of DataSafeguard's customer base.
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: All customers who have enabled the UK jurisdiction ruleset in
  ID-PRIVACY; all customers whose websites receive UK traffic; specific impact on customers who rely on
  analytics/statistical exemptions without providing an objection mechanism.
- Customer notice required: YES -- customers operating under UK-PECR configurations need to be told that:
  (a) the ICO has finalised guidance incorporating DUAA changes; (b) the new exemption categories may allow
  reclassification of certain analytics cookies away from consent-required; (c) the "simple means of objecting"
  requirement must be implemented on-banner; (d) multi-purpose SAT use-cases now require consent. Recommended
  notice window: within 30 days of this alert.

ID-PRIVACY IMPACT
---------------------------------------------------------------
Affected subsystems:

  [X] Consent Banner               -- New "simple means of objecting" requirement: the ICO's new sub-chapter
                                       confirms that for cookies/SATs relying on the statistical-purposes or
                                       appearance/functionality exemption, a visible, accessible objection
                                       mechanism must be presented on-banner (or equivalently prominent
                                       location) -- browser-settings alone do not satisfy this. ID-PRIVACY's
                                       UK banner template must add an objection toggle/link for these exempt
                                       categories. Additionally, the multi-purpose SAT rule means that any
                                       cookie currently classified as exempt but also serving an advertising
                                       purpose must be moved into the consent-required bucket in the UK ruleset.

  [X] Cookie Classification ML     -- Five new exemption categories need to be reflected in the UK
                                       classification schema: (1) communication; (2) strictly necessary
                                       (user-perspective assessment); (3) statistical/analytics (now exempt
                                       but requires objection mechanism); (4) appearance/functionality
                                       (exempt but requires objection mechanism); (5) emergency assistance.
                                       The classifier must be updated to label cookies against these new
                                       categories for UK jurisdictions. Affiliate-marketing pixels must be
                                       tagged as consent-required (ICO express statement). Multi-purpose
                                       cookies must be flagged where a secondary purpose falls outside all
                                       exemptions.

  [X] Consent Storage & Proof      -- The proof-of-consent record for UK must now capture: which exemption
                                       category (if any) was applied per cookie, whether an objection
                                       mechanism was presented for statistical/appearance-exempt cookies, and
                                       whether the user objected. Existing UK consent records do not include
                                       an exemption-basis field; schema update required.

  [ ] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP) -- No change from this guidance; existing GPC/TCF handling unaffected.

  [X] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing   -- UK ruleset must be updated to activate the five new exemption
                                       categories as a new consent-flow tier: "exempt-with-objection"
                                       (statistical, appearance) and "exempt-no-objection" (communication,
                                       strictly-necessary, emergency). The routing engine must distinguish
                                       these two new tiers from the existing binary consent/no-consent split.

  [X] Vendor / SDK Governance      -- Affiliate marketing pixels must be flagged as consent-required in the
                                       vendor library for UK. Any vendor SDK currently tagged as "analytics
                                       exempt" must be re-evaluated against the multi-purpose rule: if the
                                       same SDK also serves retargeting or other non-exempt purposes, it must
                                       move to consent-required for UK.

  [ ] DSR Engine                   -- No new rights created by this guidance.

  [X] Audit / Logging              -- Objection events for statistical/appearance-exempt SATs must be
                                       logged as a new event type (e.g., "exemption_objection_received"),
                                       and the applied exemption category must be logged per cookie/vendor
                                       for UK sessions.

  [ ] Cross-border transfer        -- No change from this guidance.

  [X] Tenant Config / Admin UI     -- New tenant toggles for UK ruleset: "Enable statistical-purposes
                                       exemption (requires objection mechanism)"; "Enable
                                       appearance/functionality exemption (requires objection mechanism)";
                                       per-cookie exemption-category selector. Tenants must be able to
                                       declare which cookies rely on which DUAA exemption.

  [X] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA  -- Update sample privacy notices and DPIA templates to reflect:
                                       (a) DUAA exemption categories as a lawful basis alternative to
                                       consent; (b) objection-mechanism requirement for statistical/
                                       appearance cookies; (c) ICO's affiliate-pixel consent position.
                                       Update the UK-specific compliance guide in the knowledge base.

RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Fine exposure: The DUAA has already raised the PECR fine ceiling from GBP 500,000 to up to
GBP 17.5 million or 4% of global annual turnover (whichever is higher) for certain PECR
contraventions from February 5, 2026. Any UK tenant whose ID-PRIVACY configuration relies on
the statistical or appearance exemption without an objection mechanism is already technically
non-compliant with the finalised guidance and exposed to this elevated fine ceiling. The ICO
conducted a compliance review of the UK's 1,000 most-visited websites in 2025, achieving >95%
compliance via engagement rather than fines -- but the review methodology will now apply the new
guidance standards. Competitor CMPs (OneTrust, Didomi, Usercentrics, Cookiebot) are actively
updating UK templates for the DUAA exemptions; delay creates a customer-retention risk as
customers may migrate to compliant alternatives. Customer contractual exposure: most MSAs include
a "comply with applicable law" warranty that now requires compliance with the finalised guidance.
Reputational: ID-PRIVACY's value proposition is proactive compliance -- being behind the ICO's
published final guidance by more than 60 days would be an unacceptable gap.

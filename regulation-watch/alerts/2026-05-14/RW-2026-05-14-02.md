WHAT CHANGED (<=4 sentences, plain English)
---------------------------------------------------------------
On April 17, 2026, Italy's Garante adopted binding guidelines (Provision No. 284) classifying the
insertion of tracking pixels into emails as "access to the user's terminal equipment" under Article
122 of the Italian Privacy Code (implementing Article 5(3) of the ePrivacy Directive), making them
subject to the same consent rules as website cookies. The prior regulatory position treated email
tracking pixels as a grey area -- some operators relied on legitimate interest or the existing
commercial-communications consent to justify per-recipient pixel tracking without separate
disclosure. Under the new guidelines, individual-level open-tracking pixels require prior, free,
specific, and informed consent from the email recipient; consent may be bundled with the general
consent to receive commercial communications provided the request is neutrally worded, but the
recipient must be able to withdraw pixel consent selectively (i.e., to stop being tracked without
stopping all email receipt). Three categories of pixels are exempt from consent: (1) anonymised
aggregate-statistics pixels where the pixel is identical for all recipients and IP addresses are
rigorously anonymised; (2) security/authentication pixels (e.g., OTP delivery confirmation);
(3) institutional/mandatory communications (e.g., obligatory banking notifications). The six-month
transition period (to October 29, 2026) applies to email addresses collected before the Official
Gazette publication date of April 29, 2026; new addresses are subject to the rules immediately.

---------------------------------------------------------------
WHO IS AFFECTED
---------------------------------------------------------------
- Applicability test: Any organisation (regardless of where established) that sends emails
  containing tracking pixels to recipients in Italy or whose email communications are governed by
  Italian law. The territorial reach follows the ePrivacy Directive implementation: the pixel
  accesses the recipient's terminal equipment, so Italian law applies when the recipient is in Italy.
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: Any tenant that (a) uses an email-integrated consent
  flow in which ID-PRIVACY gates or records consent for email tracking pixels; (b) discloses
  email-tracking-pixel SDKs in their ID-PRIVACY vendor library (e.g., Mailchimp, SendGrid,
  Brevo/Sendinblue, Klaviyo, HubSpot, Campaign Monitor -- all use per-recipient open-tracking
  pixels by default); (c) has Italian users in their subscriber lists. This is a broad category:
  most e-commerce, SaaS, and media tenants with EU operations use email marketing tools with
  per-recipient pixel tracking. Customer segment estimate: moderate-to-high proportion of EU/IT
  tenants.
- Customer notice required: YES -- by 2026-07-31 at the latest. Customers with Italian email
  subscriber lists need to understand the new consent requirement for tracking pixels and the
  transition-period deadline. Notice should explain what consent must be captured, how to configure
  ID-PRIVACY's vendor library for email pixels, and what to do about pre-existing subscriber lists
  (re-consent or switch to aggregate-anonymised tracking).

ID-PRIVACY IMPACT
---------------------------------------------------------------
Affected subsystems:

  [X] Consent Banner               -- If ID-PRIVACY is the point of capture for email-tracking-pixel
                                       consent (e.g., an email sign-up form overlays with a cookie/
                                       consent banner, or a preference centre is embedded in the
                                       subscription flow), the IT-jurisdiction consent capture flow
                                       must present a specific, separate consent signal for tracking
                                       pixels. The Garante permits bundling with commercial-comms
                                       consent IF the request is neutrally worded -- meaning the
                                       banner/preference-centre must not pre-tick or frame the pixel-
                                       consent positively (no "We'd love to improve your experience
                                       with personalised insights" framing that nudges acceptance).

  [X] Cookie Classification ML     -- Email tracking pixels used by vendor SDKs in the vendor library
                                       must be tagged with a new Italian-jurisdiction label:
                                       `emailTrackingPixel_IT`. The classifier must distinguish between:
                                       (a) aggregate-anonymous pixels (IT-exempt); (b) individual-level
                                       open-tracking pixels (IT-consent-required); (c) security/auth
                                       pixels (IT-exempt). Most commercial email platform SDKs use
                                       individual-level open-tracking by default and should be tagged
                                       consent-required for IT. This is a new sub-category not
                                       previously captured in the cookie/tracker taxonomy.

  [ ] Consent Storage & Proof      -- If ID-PRIVACY captures email-pixel consent directly, proof-of-
                                       consent records must include email-pixel consent alongside the
                                       existing web-cookie consent fields. However, if the email
                                       marketing platform captures and stores this consent independently,
                                       ID-PRIVACY's obligation is limited to vendor-library disclosure
                                       and classification. Clarify scope with product/legal.

  [ ] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP) -- No change; GPC/TCF are web-only signals; email tracking is
                                        out of scope for TCF and GPP.

  [X] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing   -- Italian jurisdiction ruleset must include a new policy:
                                       email-tracking-pixel vendors flagged `emailTrackingPixel_IT`
                                       require consent for Italy-resident email recipients. This is a
                                       separate jurisdiction from the EU/GDPR web-cookie ruleset and
                                       requires its own routing rule.

  [X] Vendor / SDK Governance      -- All major email platform SDKs in the vendor library (Mailchimp,
                                       SendGrid, Klaviyo, HubSpot, Brevo, Salesforce Marketing Cloud,
                                       etc.) must be reviewed for per-recipient pixel tracking and
                                       flagged `emailTrackingPixel_IT = true` where applicable. Vendors
                                       that offer aggregate-only tracking modes should be flagged
                                       `emailTrackingPixel_IT_aggregateExemptAvailable = true` so
                                       tenants can choose the exempt mode. This review must be complete
                                       before the transition period ends (October 29, 2026), but should
                                       be completed by August 29, 2026 to allow tenant remediation time.

  [ ] DSR Engine                   -- The guidelines mention that recipients must be able to selectively
                                       withdraw pixel consent while continuing to receive emails --
                                       this is a preference-centre capability. If ID-PRIVACY's
                                       preference centre governs email-pixel consent, a "stop pixel
                                       tracking / keep receiving emails" granular option is required
                                       for IT. If the tenant's email platform manages this separately,
                                       no DSR-engine change is needed. Clarify scope.

  [ ] Audit / Logging              -- If ID-PRIVACY captures email-pixel consent, log events are needed.
                                       If the email platform captures consent independently, no
                                       ID-PRIVACY audit-log change is required. Scope to be confirmed.

  [ ] Cross-border transfer        -- No change from this guidance.

  [X] Tenant Config / Admin UI     -- New vendor-library field: `emailTrackingPixelType` (values:
                                       individual-level, aggregate-anonymous, security-auth, not-
                                       applicable). New IT-jurisdiction policy toggle: "Require consent
                                       for individual-level email tracking pixels (Italy)". Tenants
                                       should be able to see which SDKs in their stack have been tagged
                                       as IT-email-pixel-consent-required.

  [X] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA  -- Update the Italian jurisdiction compliance guide: explain the

RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Fine exposure: The Garante can impose GDPR-level fines (up to EUR 20 million or 4% of global
annual turnover, whichever is higher) for violations of the Italian Privacy Code, which implements
the ePrivacy Directive. Precedent: in April 2026, the Garante fined Poste Italiane EUR 12.5 million
for invasive app tracking and email consent violations -- demonstrating that the Garante is
actively enforcing in the email-tracking space concurrent with these guidelines. Customers (tenants)
who continue per-recipient open-tracking of Italian email subscribers without compliant consent
after October 29, 2026 are directly exposed. ID-PRIVACY's vendor-library failing to flag this risk
creates a contractual exposure for DataSafeguard (most MSAs include a "comply with applicable law"
provision). The Garante has a track record of coordinated enforcement with the CNIL and EDPB;
a cross-border investigation is possible if the pixel-consent gap is identified at scale. This
is a MEDIUM (not HIGH) alert because the six-month transition provides adequate run-time for
engineering delivery, and the guideline does not change the web-cookie consent flow or require
an immediate banner change.

---------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT CHANGED (plain English)
---------------------------------------------------------------
Prior rule: There were no Italy-specific guidelines on tracking pixels in
email. Controllers relied on general GDPR consent principles and the
Garante's existing cookie guidance (which addressed website-embedded
technologies but did not address email-embedded pixels explicitly).

New rule: Garante Provision 284 now establishes binding national guidelines
that classify email tracking pixels as a form of access to users' terminal
devices, placing them on the same legal footing as website cookies under
Italian law and GDPR. As a result, any email tracking pixel that can be
attributed to an individual recipient — including open-rate pixels, device-
type pixels, and location/IP-derived pixels — requires prior, free,
specific, and informed consent before firing, with three narrow exceptions
(see below). Controllers have exactly six months from Gazzetta Ufficiale
publication (i.e., until 28 October 2026) to bring existing email programs
into compliance.

Three narrow no-consent exceptions:
  1. Anonymised aggregate statistics only — a single shared pixel counting
     total open percentage, with all identifying technical data (IP, device,
     timestamp) anonymised at the point of collection, serving no other
     purpose. A per-recipient pixel does NOT qualify.
  2. Pure service/transactional messages with legal obligation — mandatory
     banking notifications, healthcare alerts, password-reset confirmation
     emails; the pixel must serve only the transactional purpose.
  3. Emergency assistance — location pixels used solely to identify a user
     requiring emergency services.

Consent can be bundled into a broader promotional-communication consent if
presented neutrally and non-coercively (not bundled silently). Consent
withdrawal must be granular: the user must be able to stop pixel tracking
while continuing to receive the email, or stop both.

WHO IS AFFECTED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Applicability test:  All controllers sending email to recipients in Italy
  (or established in Italy) and using unique/per-recipient tracking pixels
  for open rates, click rates, device type, read-time, location inference,
  or any individually attributable metric. Applies regardless of whether the
  controller is an Italian company or a non-Italian company targeting
  Italian recipients. Scope includes marketing platforms, CRM/ESP vendors,
  transactional email services, and the CMP vendors that govern consent for
  those channels.

DataSafeguard customers likely affected: All ID-PRIVACY tenants operating
  email marketing programs targeting Italian users. Given Italy is one of
  the five largest EU markets, this is expected to affect the majority of
  EU-tenants and likely many global/multi-jurisdictional tenants. Estimated
  impact: HIGH (broad tenant base, Italy is a major digital-advertising
  market with active DPA enforcement history).

Customer notice required: YES — by 2026-06-17 (30 days from today). Tenants
  need to understand that: (a) email tracking pixels used for per-recipient
  analytics now require explicit consent under Italian law; (b) ID-PRIVACY
  is adding consent management support for this channel; and (c) they should
  audit their ESP/email vendor configurations immediately.

---------------------------------------------------------------
ID-PRIVACY IMPACT
---------------------------------------------------------------
Affected subsystems:

  [X] Consent Banner
      Email consent capture is a distinct user-interaction surface that
      currently sits outside the cookie-banner flow. ID-PRIVACY must add
      a consent capture mechanism for email tracking pixels, either:
      (a) as an additional toggle in the existing web consent banner (e.g.,
          "Email open-rate tracking" purpose, gated by jurisdiction == IT),
      (b) as a preference-centre module that can be surfaced in emails
          (via preference-link / email unsubscribe-style page), or
      (c) as an API endpoint that ESPs can call to confirm pixel-consent
          status before rendering a tracked email.
      The banner/preference-centre must present email pixel consent as a
      separate, named purpose — it cannot be merged silently into a generic
      "marketing" or "analytics" toggle. The withdrawal UX must support
      "stop tracking but keep receiving emails" and "stop both" as distinct
      choices.

  [X] Cookie Classification ML
      The classifier must be extended to recognise email tracking pixel
      patterns (1x1 GIF/PNG remote-hosted images in email HTML, known ESP
      tracking domains such as mailchimp.com, sendgrid.net, klaviyo.com,
      sparkpost.com, etc.) as a distinct technology category: "email-
      tracking-pixel." The category needs three sub-labels: (1) per-
      recipient-attributable (consent required in IT), (2) aggregate-
      anonymised (no consent required under exception 1), (3) service-
      transactional (no consent required under exception 2). The distinction
      between sub-labels (1) and (2) requires inspection of whether a pixel
      is unique per email send or shared.

  [X] Consent Storage & Proof
      Proof-of-consent records must now capture email-pixel consent as a
      distinct record: purpose = "email-tracking-pixel," channel = "email,"
      disclosure-text hash, timestamp of consent capture, method of capture
      (banner toggle / preference centre / in-email link), withdrawal events.
      The existing web-cookie consent schema may need a new `channel` field
      or a parallel `emailConsentRecord` entity to avoid conflating web and
      email consent proofs. Retention: >= 3 years (Garante investigation
      window precedent).

  [X] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP)
      GPC and IAB TCF strings do not currently carry an email-tracking-pixel
      signal. Until a standard signal exists (none planned as of this scan),
      email-pixel consent status must be propagated out-of-band: ID-PRIVACY
      must expose an API that ESPs can query with a user identifier to
      confirm Italian-law consent status before sending a tracked email.
      Document this gap for IAB Tech Lab and ESP partners.

  [X] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing
      The Italy ruleset (`jurisdiction == IT`) must be extended to include
      the email-tracking-pixel rule. Routing logic: if a user's country of
      residence = IT (or if the tenant's target-audience includes IT), the
      email-pixel consent flag must be honoured regardless of whether a web-
      cookie consent string exists. This is a distinct gate.

  [X] Vendor / SDK Governance
      ESP vendors (Mailchimp, Klaviyo, Salesforce Marketing Cloud, HubSpot,
      Brevo, SendGrid, etc.) used by tenants must be catalogued as "email
      tracking pixel" vendors in the ID-PRIVACY vendor library. Each must
      carry a flag: `usesPerRecipientTrackingPixel: true/false`. Tenant
      vendor management UI must surface this flag and block pixel firing for
      IT-resident recipients unless `emailPixelConsent.granted = true`.

  [ ] DSR Engine — No new consumer right, but consent-withdrawal for email
      pixel tracking must feed into the existing preference-revocation flow.

  [X] Audit / Logging
      New audit-log events required: `email_pixel_consent_prompt_shown`
      (with IT jurisdiction flag), `email_pixel_consent_granted`,
      `email_pixel_consent_withdrawn`, `esp_vendor_pixel_gated` (fired when
      ID-PRIVACY blocks ESP pixel call due to missing consent).

  [ ] Cross-border transfer — No new cross-border transfer obligation created.

  [X] Tenant Config / Admin UI
      New tenant toggles: "Enable email tracking pixel consent flow (IT),"
      per-ESP-vendor pixel flag, exception-type selector (aggregate-anon /
      transactional), email consent withdrawal UX selector (banner toggle /
      preference centre / in-email link).

  [X] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA
      Add Garante Provision 284 as the controlling Italian standard for
      email tracking. Update sample DPIA template: add "email tracking
      pixels" as a processing activity row with legal basis = consent
      (Italy), retention of consent proof = 3 years. Update RoPA template
      with new processing purpose "email-open-rate tracking (IT)."

RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Direct exposure: Any tenant using per-recipient email tracking pixels for
Italian recipients after 28 October 2026 without consent faces GDPR
enforcement by the Garante. The Garante is an active enforcer: it levied
EUR 12.5M against Poste Italiane in the same April 2026 enforcement cycle,
EUR 79.1M against Enel Energia (largest Italian GDPR fine to date), and
EUR 15M against OpenAI. The email-pixel guidelines are binding, not
advisory. GDPR fine ceiling: EUR 20M or 4% of global annual turnover,
whichever is higher. Civil litigation risk is secondary but real in Italy
given the country's active privacy bar.

Platform risk: Email marketing is a standard feature used by the majority
of DataSafeguard's mid-market and enterprise tenants. A competitor CMP that
ships a "Garante email pixel compliance" feature first creates a meaningful
sales wedge. OneTrust and Didomi have already referenced the Garante action
in their product blogs; expect product announcements within 60 days.

Customer contractual: Most tenant MSAs include a "comply with applicable
law" warranty; Garante enforcement on a tenant's email program may trigger
indemnification claims if ID-PRIVACY failed to flag and enable compliance.

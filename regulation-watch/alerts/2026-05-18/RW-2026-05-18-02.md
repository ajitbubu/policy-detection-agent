WHAT CHANGED (plain English)
---------------------------------------------------------------
Prior rule: PECR required consent for all cookies/storage technologies
  except two legacy exemptions (strictly necessary; communication
  transmission). PECR maximum fine was GBP 500,000. The ICO's last
  substantive cookie guidance predated the DUAA.

New rule (three layers):

  Layer 1 — Five new PECR consent exemptions (effective 5 February 2026,
  enacted by DUAA):
    (1) Communication transmission — for carrying out transmission over an
        electronic communications network (unchanged from legacy strictly
        necessary transmission exemption; now codified more explicitly).
    (2) Strictly necessary service — where storage/access is essential to
        provide the information society service the user explicitly requested
        (unchanged in substance; now codified).
    (3) Statistical / analytics — for collecting information about visitors
        to improve the service (NEW). An organisation providing an
        information society service may use analytics cookies without
        consent, BUT must still provide a simple, free means to object.
    (4) Appearance / functionality customisation — for adapting the
        appearance or functionality of the service to user preference (NEW).
        Same "simple means of objecting" requirement applies.
    (5) Emergency assistance — for identifying the subscriber or user to
        provide emergency assistance (NEW; narrow).
  For exemptions (3) and (4), if a user objects the organisation must stop
  immediately. Browser settings alone do NOT satisfy the "simple means of
  objecting" requirement; a discoverable in-product control is required.

  Layer 2 — Two new guidance sub-chapters (effective 29 April 2026):
    "What does a simple means of objecting mean?" — requires that for the
    analytics and appearance exceptions, the opt-out be clearly discoverable
    (not buried), free to use, and immediate in effect. CMPs must provide
    a visible toggle (not just a link to browser settings or a deeply
    nested privacy page).
    "Can we use the same storage and access technology for multiple
    purposes?" — if a cookie/technology serves both an exempt purpose and
    a non-exempt purpose simultaneously, the exempt purpose cannot be used
    as a basis to avoid consent for the non-exempt purpose; the controller
    must either separate the technologies or obtain consent for the non-
    exempt purpose alongside the exempt use. This directly targets the
    common practice of routing analytics and advertising through the same
    cookie.

  Layer 3 — Penalty increase (effective 5 February 2026):
    PECR fines now align with UK GDPR: maximum of GBP 17.5M or 4% of
    global annual turnover, whichever is higher. The prior cap was GBP
    500,000. This is a 35-fold increase in maximum exposure for PECR
    violations (which include cookie consent failures).

WHO IS AFFECTED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Applicability test: Any organisation using storage and access technologies
  (cookies, pixels, fingerprinting, local storage, etc.) on devices of
  users in the UK, or established in the UK. UK-targeting test applies;
  no need for UK establishment.

DataSafeguard customers likely affected: All ID-PRIVACY tenants operating
  websites, apps, or digital services targeting UK users. Given the UK is
  the second-largest digital-advertising market DataSafeguard serves, this
  covers the substantial majority of the tenant base. The analytics exemption
  and multi-purpose technology clarification will require active CMP
  reconfiguration for most tenants who currently serve analytics cookies
  on a pure consent basis or who combine analytics and ad-targeting in the
  same cookie.

Customer notice required: YES — by 2026-06-17 (30 days). Tenants need to
  understand: (a) they can now deploy analytics cookies in the UK without
  a consent gate (exemption 3), but must provide a discoverable opt-out;
  (b) multi-purpose cookies must be disclosed separately for non-exempt
  purposes; (c) PECR fine ceiling has increased 35x, making non-compliance
  materially more expensive.

ID-PRIVACY IMPACT
---------------------------------------------------------------
Affected subsystems:

  [X] Consent Banner
      Two changes required:
      (a) Analytics/appearance exemption handling: ID-PRIVACY must add a
          UK-specific ruleset that permits analytics cookies to fire without
          an initial consent gate (jurisdiction == UK, exemption == analytics),
          but MUST simultaneously surface a discoverable, first-layer opt-out
          control for analytics. This is the inverse of the current flow (which
          blocks by default and requires accept). A toggle labelled e.g.,
          "Analytics (active — opt out)" must be visible in the banner or
          accessible within one click (not buried in a second-layer settings
          page). Browser-settings links alone are explicitly insufficient per
          the ICO guidance.
      (b) Multi-purpose technology warning: tenants who set a single cookie
          for both analytics and advertising must be flagged. ID-PRIVACY
          should add a vendor-library rule that detects dual-purpose cookies
          and forces separate consent for the advertising purpose even if the
          analytics purpose is exempt.
      Note: equal prominence of Accept/Reject buttons remains required for
      non-exempt cookies. This requirement is unchanged.

  [X] Cookie Classification ML
      The classifier must now tag cookies with:
        - `ukExemptionType`: analytics | appearance | emergency | transmission
          | strictly-necessary | none (none = consent required in UK)
        - `multiPurposeConflict`: bool (true if the same cookie simultaneously
          serves an exempt and a non-exempt purpose, flagging that the non-
          exempt purpose requires its own consent gate)
      Training data update: use ICO exemption definitions as category
      boundaries. The analytics exemption boundary (improving the service
      vs. building cross-site profiles) must be reflected in the model.

  [X] Consent Storage & Proof
      For the analytics and appearance exemptions, ID-PRIVACY must store:
        - that the user was shown an opt-out control (not a consent gate),
        - whether the user exercised the opt-out and when,
        - that analytics cookies fired under the exemption (not under consent).
      This creates a new consent-record variant: `basis = uk-pecr-exemption`,
      with sub-fields `exemptionType`, `optOutOffered: bool`,
      `optOutExercised: bool`, `optOutTimestamp`. Distinguish from standard
      consent records to avoid misrepresenting exemption-based processing
      as consent-based.

  [X] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP)
      GPC in the UK: if a user sends a GPC signal, it does not automatically
      override an exemption-based analytics deployment, but it is a strong
      indication the user would object. ID-PRIVACY must define policy: does
      receipt of GPC automatically trigger the opt-out for UK analytics
      exemption? Recommend yes (conservative, defensible). Document this
      in the UK ruleset configuration.
      IAB TCF: TCF 2.3 strings do not have a purpose-slot for UK-PECR-
      exempt analytics; the exemption is UK-only and diverges from EU
      ePrivacy. UK tenants should be advised not to rely on TCF Purpose 8
      (analytics) as the basis for the UK exemption; the two regimes are
      now different.

  [X] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing
      The UK ruleset must be split from the EU ruleset. Key divergences:
        - UK now has analytics exemption (EU does not).
        - UK PECR fine ceiling is now GBP 17.5M / 4% (aligned with UK GDPR).
        - Multi-purpose technology rule is now explicit in UK guidance.
      The routing logic must apply `jurisdiction == UK` independently of
      `jurisdiction == EU`; tenants must be able to configure UK behaviour
      separately from their EU GDPR/ePrivacy configuration.

  [X] Vendor / SDK Governance
      Each vendor in the library must be tagged with `ukPecrExemption`:
      analytics | appearance | strictly-necessary | none. Where `ukPecrExemption
      == analytics` and the vendor ALSO serves advertising purposes from the
      same technology, flag `multiPurposeConflict = true` and gate the
      advertising purpose behind consent even in the UK.

  [ ] DSR Engine — No new consumer rights created.

  [X] Audit / Logging
      New audit-log events: `uk_analytics_exemption_applied` (fires when
      analytics cookie deployed under exemption, not under consent),
      `uk_analytics_optout_offered` (records that opt-out control was
      surfaced), `uk_analytics_optout_exercised`, `uk_multipurpose_conflict_
      detected` (fires when a cookie is flagged as dual-purpose in UK context).

  [ ] Cross-border transfer — No change.

  [X] Tenant Config / Admin UI
      New tenant toggles:
        - "Enable UK analytics exemption (PECR s.6ZA)" — activates the new
          flow where analytics fire without initial consent but with an opt-out.
        - "UK analytics opt-out control placement" — first-layer banner /
          one-click from banner / preference centre (with note that preference
          centre alone may not meet ICO discoverability standard).
        - "Block multi-purpose cookies in UK (analytics + advertising)"
          (recommended ON by default).

  [X] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA
      Update UK-specific guidance notes: distinguish UK PECR from EU ePrivacy
      for analytics cookies. Note the 35x penalty increase. Reference DUAA
      Royal Assent date (19 June 2025), commencement date (5 February 2026),
      and ICO final guidance date (29 April 2026) as the controlling
      legislative and guidance chain.

RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
---------------------------------------------------------------
The 35x PECR fine increase transforms what was historically a low-penalty
regime (max GBP 500K) into a major regulatory risk (max GBP 17.5M or 4%
of global turnover). The ICO has announced it will prioritise enforcement
of cookie compliance in 2026. Any tenant who: (a) deploys analytics
cookies under the new exemption without an opt-out, (b) uses multi-purpose
cookies to backdoor analytics exemption for advertising, or (c) treats
GPC as irrelevant in the UK, now faces fine exposure that rivals GDPR
enforcement. Platform risk: the analytics exemption is also a commercial
opportunity — if ID-PRIVACY enables the UK analytics flow, tenants gain
the ability to deploy analytics without a consent-blocking gate, which is
a meaningful user-experience and data-quality improvement. Competitors who
ship this feature first will use it as a differentiator in UK-focused
deals. Reputational: ICO is actively publishing guidance and has telegraphed
enforcement intent; being a named reference in an ICO enforcement notice
as a CMP that did not implement the exemption correctly is a direct
reputational risk for DataSafeguard.

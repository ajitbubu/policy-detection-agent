WHAT CHANGED (plain English)
---------------------------------------------------------------
CNIL Deliberation No. 2026-042, adopted 12 March 2026 and published
14 April 2026, establishes that email tracking pixels (invisible 1x1
images that signal email opens, device type, and timing) are subject
to the same prior-consent requirement as web cookies under Article 82
of the French Data Protection Act (transposing ePrivacy Art. 5(3)).
Prior to this recommendation there was no explicit CNIL instrument
specifically targeting email pixels; the prior rule relied on general
GDPR/ePrivacy interpretation. The new rule is clear: pixel deployment
requires prior free, specific, informed, and unambiguous consent
unless the pixel is strictly necessary to (a) transmit the
communication itself or (b) deliver a service explicitly requested by
the recipient. A narrow additional exemption covers pixels used
exclusively to track deliverability/inactive-subscriber cleansing,
provided they do not build individual profiles. For contacts
collected before 14 April 2026, organisations have until 14 July 2026
to either obtain consent or cease pixel deployment. No grace period
applies to contacts collected on or after 14 April 2026.

---------------------------------------------------------------
WHO IS AFFECTED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Applicability test:  Any organisation sending emails to recipients
                     located in France, regardless of where the
                     sender is established. Also applies to CMP/ESP
                     platforms processing on behalf of such senders.
DataSafeguard customers affected: All customers using ID-PRIVACY to
                     manage consent for French users and any customer
                     providing email marketing consent flows with
                     pixel tracking for FR-jurisdiction contacts.
Customer notice required: YES -- by 2026-06-21 (to give customers
                     sufficient lead time before the 14 July 2026
                     grace period deadline).

---------------------------------------------------------------
ID-PRIVACY IMPACT
---------------------------------------------------------------
Affected subsystems:

  [X] Consent Banner
      Email subscription / newsletter sign-up flows served to French
      users must include a specific purpose disclosure for "email
      tracking (open/read receipts)" and obtain affirmative consent
      for that purpose separately from marketing content consent.
      The banner / sign-up form must not bundle email pixel consent
      with general email marketing consent unless purpose is made
      explicit. Add a dedicated "email tracking" purpose field in
      FR-jurisdiction consent capture for email channel.

  [ ] Cookie Classification ML
      No change to web cookie classification. However, if the
      platform classifies "email marketing SDK" vendor categories,
      those should be tagged to indicate pixel-tracking sub-purpose.

  [X] Consent Storage & Proof
      Proof-of-consent records for email marketing must now record
      email-pixel consent as a distinct purpose attribute. Existing
      consent records collected before 14 April 2026 do not carry
      this attribute; customers must re-collect or implement the
      notice-and-objection workflow for legacy databases. The
      consent record schema must support: channel=email,
      purpose=open_tracking_pixel, jurisdiction=FR,
      legal_basis=consent, consent_timestamp.

  [ ] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP)
      No direct signal-layer impact (GPC is browser-based and does
      not cover email).

  [X] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing
      FR jurisdiction ruleset must be updated to include email-pixel
      consent as a required purpose. The routing logic must apply
      this rule when the customer's consent request is for the
      email channel and the recipient's jurisdiction is FR.

  [X] Vendor / SDK Governance
      Email service providers and marketing automation platforms
      (e.g., Brevo, Mailchimp, Salesforce Marketing Cloud) that
      deploy pixels must be listed as data processors in vendor
      disclosures. The prior-consent gate must be enforced before
      the ESP fires pixels. Platform should surface a vendor
      classification flag: email_open_tracking_pixel (boolean).

  [ ] DSR Engine
      No new rights. Existing right to withdraw consent and right
      to erasure apply and are already handled.

  [X] Audit / Logging
      Consent log for email-pixel purpose must be separable from
      general email-marketing consent in audit exports, because
      CNIL may request proof of the specific purpose-level consent
      during an audit.

  [ ] Cross-border transfer
      No new transfer mechanism requirement.

  [X] Tenant Config / Admin UI
      New per-tenant toggle for email channel: "Require separate
      consent for email open-tracking pixels (FR)". When the
      customer's contact base includes French recipients, this toggle
      should default to ON with a notice. Include a migration
      workflow for legacy databases: notify-and-objection template
      and deadline tracking dashboard showing days remaining to
      14 July 2026.

  [X] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA
      RoPA template for FR email marketing must include email-pixel
      tracking as a separate processing activity with consent as the
      legal basis. DPIA template should flag that combining pixel
      tracking with profiling for targeting may require a standalone
      DPIA.

---------------------------------------------------------------
RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
---------------------------------------------------------------
The CNIL has a well-documented track record of enforcement on
cookie/pixel consent: EUR 150 million (SHEIN), EUR 27 million (Free
Mobile), EUR 15 million (Free) in recent cycles. After 14 July 2026
the theory of liability for email pixel non-compliance is unambiguous.
Customers who continue deploying pixels on existing French contacts
without valid consent face direct CNIL enforcement. DataSafeguard
faces contractual exposure (failure to provide a compliant platform
feature) and reputational risk if the platform does not surface this
workflow before the deadline.

---------------------------------------------------------------
CONFIDENCE
---------------------------------------------------------------
HIGH -- Deliberation No. 2026-042 is confirmed via CNIL's own website
(cnil.fr). Publication date, grace period deadline, and primary
requirements corroborated by Hogan Lovells, BCLP, and JD Supra
analyses that cite the primary text directly. Sole area of
interpretive uncertainty is the exact boundary of the "deliverability
cleansing" exemption; legal review recommended before coding it as
an automatic exemption.

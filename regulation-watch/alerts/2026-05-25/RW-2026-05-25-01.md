WHAT CHANGED (plain English)
---------------------------------------------------------------
The ICO published the final version of its guidance on Storage and
Access Technologies (SATs) on 29 April 2026, superseding the prior
"cookies and similar technologies" guidance. The guidance implements
five new consent exemptions introduced by the Data (Use and Access)
Act 2025 that came into force on 5 February 2026: (1) transmission
of a communication, (2) user-requested service, (3) statistical
analytics to improve a service, (4) appearance/preference adaptation,
and (5) emergency assistance. The analytics exemption is material but
narrow: it applies only where the sole purpose is aggregate
service-improvement statistics, with no advertising feed-through and
no individual-level tracking. Two new sub-chapters clarify (a) what
"a simple and free means of objecting" requires for the exempt
categories (organisations must allow objection and stop processing if
the user objects) and (b) how to handle a single SAT used for
multiple purposes (the highest-friction rule applies -- consent is
needed if any purpose requires it). Maximum PECR fines are now
aligned with UK GDPR at GBP 17.5 million or 4% of global annual
turnover, whichever is higher.

---------------------------------------------------------------
Applicability test:  Any organisation offering online services to
                     UK users or operating under UK establishment,
                     regardless of where the data controller is based.
DataSafeguard customers affected: All customers with UK-jurisdiction
                     rulesets (enterprise and mid-market segments
                     operating in the UK).
Customer notice required: YES -- by 2026-06-30. Customers need to
                     know about (a) the new analytics exemption they
                     may choose to use, and (b) the obligation to
                     provide a "simple means of objecting" if they
                     invoke that exemption. Customers who have already
                     been relying on the DUAA exemptions since
                     February need the finalized ICO interpretation.

ID-PRIVACY IMPACT
---------------------------------------------------------------
Affected subsystems:

  [X] Consent Banner
      The analytics and appearance exemptions allow customers to
      suppress the banner for those specific technology classes IF
      they present a "simple means of objecting" inline (e.g., footer
      link or preference center toggle). The banner must still appear
      for any SATs not covered by an exemption. Layer 1 must not use
      these exemptions as a default-on justification without providing
      the objection mechanism. Add a UK-specific "Analytics exempt
      (object here)" toggle pathway.

  [X] Cookie Classification ML
      UK jurisdiction ruleset must tag analytics cookies as
      potentially exempt under the new "statistical purposes" category.
      The classifier should flag if a cookie appears to serve a dual
      purpose (analytics + advertising), in which case the exemption
      does not apply. Add a new classification attribute:
      uk_analytics_exempt_candidate (boolean).

  [ ] Consent Storage & Proof
      No change to what must be stored for consented categories.
      For exempt categories an objection event must be logged.

  [ ] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP)
      No direct change. GPC received as an opt-out signal continues
      to apply to non-exempt categories. No UK-specific GPC mandate
      introduced by DUAA.

  [X] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing
      UK ruleset must be updated to reflect the new five exemption
      categories. The routing logic must distinguish between:
      (a) cookies requiring consent (unchanged), (b) cookies falling
      under legacy "strictly necessary" exemption, (c) cookies now
      eligible for one of the five new DUAA exemptions. The routing
      config must not auto-apply the analytics exemption without the
      customer enabling it and confirming the "sole purpose" condition.

  [X] Vendor / SDK Governance
      Third-party analytics vendors (e.g., GA4, Hotjar, Amplitude)
      may qualify for the analytics exemption only if the vendor acts
      purely on the customer's behalf and does not process data for
      its own purposes. Vendor disclosure must flag this condition.
      Pre-consent gating for third-party analytics should present a
      new "UK analytics-exempt" option with an objection link.

  [ ] DSR Engine
      No new rights introduced. Existing UK GDPR rights unchanged.

  [X] Audit / Logging
      Objection events under the new exempt categories must be
      captured and stored. Log schema needs a new event type:
      sat_objection_received with fields: jurisdiction=UK,
      exemption_category, timestamp, session_token_hash.

  [ ] Cross-border transfer
      No change in this guidance.

  [X] Tenant Config / Admin UI
      New per-tenant toggle: "Enable UK analytics SAT exemption".
      When enabled, requires the customer to confirm sole-purpose
      condition. System should surface the "simple means of objecting"
      requirement and default to a footer preference link.

  [X] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA
      Template RoPAs for UK-deployed customers must note reliance on
      DUAA statutory exemption as the legal basis for analytics SATs
      rather than consent, and document the objection mechanism.

RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Customers who invoke the analytics exemption without a "simple means
of objecting" are non-compliant with PECR as interpreted by the ICO.
Maximum PECR fine is now GBP 17.5 million or 4% of global annual
turnover -- a 35x increase from the prior GBP 500,000 cap. The ICO
has signalled continued enforcement focus on online tracking (stated
in the April 2026 publication announcement). DataSafeguard faces
reputational and contractual exposure if the platform enables an
exemption pathway without the required objection mechanism.

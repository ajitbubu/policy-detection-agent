WHAT CHANGED (plain English)
---------------------------------------------------------------
The Garante adopted Provision No. 284 on 17 April 2026 (published in
the Official Gazette 29 April 2026), issuing binding guidelines on
the use of tracking pixels in email communications. Prior to this
provision, the Garante had not issued a dedicated instrument on email
pixels; organisations relied on general cookie guidance (Provision
231/2021) which addressed web cookies only. The new guidelines
classify email tracking pixels as access to terminal equipment under
Italian Privacy Code Article 122 (implementing ePrivacy Art. 5(3)),
requiring prior, free, specific, informed, and unambiguous consent.
Two narrow exemptions: (a) pixels strictly necessary to transmit the
communication; (b) pixels strictly necessary to deliver a service
the recipient explicitly requested. For contacts collected before
29 April 2026, a six-month transitional period allows compliance by
28 October 2026, provided the organisation informs the recipient
at the first available opportunity and implements a consent-withdrawal
mechanism. No transitional period for contacts collected after
29 April 2026; consent must be obtained before pixel deployment.
The guidelines apply to all entities -- public and private -- using
pixels in email, including email platform providers and marketing
automation vendors acting as data processors.

---------------------------------------------------------------
WHO IS AFFECTED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Applicability test:  Any organisation sending emails to recipients
                     in Italy, regardless of where the sender is
                     established. Also applies to ESPs and marketing
                     automation platforms processing on behalf of
                     such organisations.
DataSafeguard customers affected: All customers with IT-jurisdiction
                     rulesets, particularly those using email-channel
                     consent flows. Significant for e-commerce,
                     financial services, and publisher customers
                     with Italian user bases.
Customer notice required: YES -- by 2026-07-01, to give customers
                     sufficient lead time before the 28 October 2026
                     transitional period ends. NOTE: the CNIL
                     (France) issued a near-identical recommendation
                     with a shorter deadline (14 July 2026); combined
                     advisory recommended.

---------------------------------------------------------------
ID-PRIVACY IMPACT
---------------------------------------------------------------
Affected subsystems:

  [X] Consent Banner
      Email sign-up / subscription flows for Italian recipients
      must include an explicit, separately-checkboxed purpose for
      "email tracking pixels" (open receipts, device information).
      Bundling pixel consent with general email-marketing consent is
      not sufficient. Add a dedicated "email tracking" purpose field
      in IT-jurisdiction email consent capture, mirroring the FR
      implementation from RW-2026-05-25-02 (can share the same
      engineering sprint).

  [ ] Cookie Classification ML
      No direct change to web cookie classification.

  [X] Consent Storage & Proof
      Email consent records for IT jurisdiction must record pixel
      consent as a distinct purpose attribute. Schema must support:
      channel=email, purpose=open_tracking_pixel, jurisdiction=IT.
      This is the same schema change required for FR (RW-2026-05-25-02)
      -- implement together to reduce duplication.

  [ ] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP)
      No direct signal-layer impact.

  [X] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing
      IT jurisdiction ruleset must require email-pixel consent.
      Routing logic must apply the rule to email-channel consent
      requests for IT-jurisdiction recipients.

  [X] Vendor / SDK Governance
      Same as FR (RW-2026-05-25-02). Known ESPs with pixel-tracking
      capabilities must be flagged as requiring prior consent for
      IT recipients. The vendor taxonomy update from ML sprint
      RW-2026-05-25-02/ML-1 covers this if IT is included in scope.

  [ ] DSR Engine
      No new rights.

  [X] Audit / Logging
      Same as FR (RW-2026-05-25-02). Email-pixel consent records
      must be exportable as a distinct purpose in audit exports.

  [ ] Cross-border transfer
      No new transfer mechanism requirement.

  [X] Tenant Config / Admin UI
      Same migration workflow as FR (RW-2026-05-25-02) but with IT
      jurisdiction and a 28 October 2026 deadline. Extend the
      legacy-database migration workflow to support multi-jurisdiction
      (FR and IT in the same campaign). Countdown dashboard should
      show both deadlines: FR=14 July 2026 and IT=28 October 2026.

  [X] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA
      IT-specific RoPA template must include email-pixel tracking as
      a separate processing activity. Reference Garante Provision 284.

---------------------------------------------------------------
RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
---------------------------------------------------------------
The Garante has fined EUR 12.5 million against Poste Italiane in
April 2026 for invasive app tracking, and EUR 79.1 million against
Enel Energia in 2026 (largest Italian GDPR fine to date). The Garante
actively deploys automated scanning and has a formal inspection
programme for H1 2026. Post-28 October 2026 there is no ambiguity:
pixels on IT contacts without consent are an Article 122 violation.
GDPR Art. 83(5) ceilings apply: EUR 20 million or 4% of global annual
turnover. DataSafeguard faces contractual exposure for any customer
using the platform to send to Italian contacts without having the
email-pixel consent workflow in place.

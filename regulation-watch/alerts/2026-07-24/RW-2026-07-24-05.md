───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHAT CHANGED (≤4 sentences, plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
New Zealand's Privacy Act now includes IPP 3A, requiring agencies to notify individuals when personal information about them is collected indirectly — i.e., from a third party rather than the individual directly — a notification duty that previously only applied to direct collection. Notice must cover what was collected, the purpose, who is holding it, and the individual's access/correction rights, given "as soon as reasonably practicable" after collection, subject to existing exceptions (public health, safety, publicly available information). For a CMP/consent-tech context, this is squarely relevant where a tenant's website or app receives data about a visitor indirectly — e.g., via a third-party pixel, data-broker enrichment, ad-tech vendor, or an SDK that shares profile/identifier data collected elsewhere — because the site operator must now proactively disclose that indirect collection, not just direct-collection cookies set on their own domain.

WHAT CHANGED (≤4 sentences, plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The EU and Brazil issued two separate but coordinated adequacy decisions on 26–27 January 2026: the European Commission found Brazil's LGPD regime "essentially equivalent" to EU protection, and Brazil's ANPD reciprocally found the EU/EEA adequate under LGPD. This is Brazil's first-ever EU adequacy decision and the first mutual, two-way adequacy arrangement of this scale (public and private sector both covered). Previously, EU-to-Brazil and Brazil-to-EU transfers required Standard Contractual Clauses or another Art. 46/Ch. V transfer mechanism; now transfers can flow on the adequacy basis alone, without SCCs, for data covered by each respective law.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHO IS AFFECTED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Applicability test: Any transfer of personal data between an EU/EEA entity and a Brazilian entity (either direction) now benefits from the adequacy pathway; existing SCCs remain valid but are no longer legally required for this specific transfer corridor.
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: Any tenant with EU and Brazil operations, or vendors/SDKs whose data flows cross the EU-Brazil corridor (advertising/analytics vendors headquartered in either region are common in ID-PRIVACY® vendor lists) — estimate: multiple global/EMEA+LatAm tenants, exact count ASSUMED, verify against tenant vendor-transfer maps.
- Customer notice required: YES, but low-urgency — this is a simplification, not a new obligation; continuing to run SCCs for this corridor is not a violation, only unnecessary overhead. Recommend notice within the next regular release cycle rather than emergency comms.

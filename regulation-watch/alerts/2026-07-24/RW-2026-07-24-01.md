WHAT CHANGED (≤4 sentences, plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Chile replaces its 1999-era data protection law with a GDPR-aligned regime effective 1 December 2026. For cookies specifically, secondary reporting is consistent and specific: consent must be freely given, informed, specific and unambiguous; the cookie banner must use an opt-in model with non-essential cookies disabled by default; and a new regulator (Agencia de Protección de Datos Personales, APDP) must stand up and is tasked with issuing dedicated cookie guidelines, international-transfer guidelines, and standard transfer clauses before/around entry into force. This moves Chile from a low-friction, largely notice-based cookie regime to an EU-style opt-in-by-default banner requirement with a dedicated regulator and materially higher fines.

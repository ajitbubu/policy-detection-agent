WHAT CHANGED (≤4 sentences, plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Chile moves from a light-touch, pre-GDPR 1999 data protection law with no dedicated regulator to a comprehensive, GDPR-aligned regime taking full effect December 1, 2026, after a 24-month transition. A new autonomous authority, the Agencia de Protección de Datos Personales (APDP), gains investigative and sanctioning powers, including a public national sanctions registry. Maximum fines rise to 20,000 UTM (~CLP 1.4 billion, roughly USD 1.5M), with repeat violations reaching 4% of annual revenue. The law introduces GDPR-style lawful bases and sensitive-data categories; cookie/online-tracking-specific implementing guidance from APDP has not yet been published.

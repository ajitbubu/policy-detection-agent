WHAT CHANGED (≤4 sentences, plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
On 2026-06-29 the U.S. Supreme Court ruled 6-3 in Trump v. Slaughter that FTC commissioners can be removed by the President at will, overturning the 90-year-old Humphrey's Executor precedent that gave the FTC statutory independence. The European Commission's 2023 EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework adequacy decision relies on FTC independence as a core safeguard (referenced ~259 times in the decision) because the FTC is the primary U.S. enforcement authority for DPF commitments. On 2026-07-31 the EDPB formally wrote to the Commission asking it to assess whether the ruling undermines that safeguard and, by extension, the legal basis for the adequacy decision. The DPF remains valid today, but its foundation is now under active institutional review for the first time since the 2023 decision — this is the discrete trigger event, not a change to the law itself.

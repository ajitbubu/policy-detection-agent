WHAT CHANGED (≤4 sentences, plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Chile is replacing its 1999-era data protection law with a GDPR-modeled comprehensive framework: consent becomes a formal, explicit legal basis for processing (alongside five other bases), a new independent Agencia de Protección de Datos Personales is created with investigative and fining powers, and data subjects gain GDPR-style rights (access, rectification, erasure, portability, objection). The law does not itself contain cookie-specific rules — general transparency/purpose-limitation/consent principles will apply to cookies and similar tracking technologies, and the new Agency is expected to issue secondary regulations/guidelines on cookies after standing up (not yet published). This is the most significant Chilean digital-regulation change in 27 years and takes effect 2026-12-01 after a 24-month transition from its December 2024 publication.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHO IS AFFECTED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Applicability test:  Any controller/processor processing personal data of individuals in Chile, including foreign entities targeting Chilean users (law has extraterritorial reach comparable to GDPR Art. 3).
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: Any ID-PRIVACY tenant with LatAm/Chile (CL) site traffic — currently likely handled under a generic "rest of world" or LGPD-adjacent ruleset; will need a dedicated CL jurisdiction ruleset by 2026-12-01.
- Customer notice required:  YES — customers with material Chile traffic should be notified ahead of 2026-12-01, with a caveat that cookie-specific secondary regulations are still pending and guidance may follow after the law takes effect.

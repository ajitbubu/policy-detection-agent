WHAT CHANGED (≤4 sentences, plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PIPA's mandatory breach-notification trigger ("유출등," Art. 23(2)) is broadened to explicitly include forgery, alteration, and damage of personal data — not just leakage/loss — which pulls tampering with consent records and audit logs into the notification regime. A new administrative-fine ceiling of 10% of total company revenue (Art. 64-2(2)) applies for severe/repeated willful or grossly negligent violations, breaches affecting 10M+ data subjects, or failure to comply with a PIPC corrective order — the highest statutory ceiling of any privacy law globally. Chief Privacy Officer appointment now requires board-of-directors approval and mandatory notification to PIPC for qualifying entities (Art. 31(3)), with new fines for non-appointment or under-qualified appointees (Art. 75(2)(14-2~4)). The underlying substantive cookie/consent rules (opt-in for non-essential cookies, consent-before-collection) are unchanged — this amendment raises the enforcement and evidentiary stakes on the same requirements ID-PRIVACY already implements for KR tenants.

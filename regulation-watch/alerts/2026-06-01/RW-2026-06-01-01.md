WHAT CHANGED (plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Prior rule: No CNIL-specific guidance existed on email tracking pixels; practitioners
relied on general Article 82 cookie consent principles by analogy, with enforcement
risk largely theoretical for email-specific use cases.

New rule: CNIL Deliberation 2026-042, adopted 12 March 2026 and published 14 April
2026, formally brings email tracking pixels within the same Article 82 framework as
browser cookies, requiring prior freely given, specific, informed consent before a
1x1 tracking image may be loaded in an email. Two narrow exemptions apply:
(1) pixels used solely for user-authentication security, and (2) pixels used only to
measure individual open rates for deliverability optimisation (e.g., stopping sends
to perpetually inactive addresses) — provided tracking is strictly limited to that
purpose and does not feed profiling or cross-channel targeting. All other
pixel uses — audience measurement, ad-campaign analytics, profile-building for
retargeting — require prior consent collected at the point the email address is
gathered. Organisations had until 14 July 2026 (90 days from publication) to
notify existing subscribers of pixel usage and give them a genuine opt-out
opportunity; contacts collected from 14 April 2026 onward required compliant
consent from day one.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHO IS AFFECTED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Applicability test:  Any organisation (private, public, association) sending
  emails containing tracking pixels to recipients located in France, regardless of
  where the sender is established. Applies to both controllers and technical
  service providers (ESPs, marketing-automation vendors).

DataSafeguard customers likely affected: All customers who integrate ID-PRIVACY(R)
  with an email service provider or marketing-automation platform and embed tracking
  pixels in campaign emails sent to French-resident subscribers. Mid-to-large
  B2C operators in retail, media, finance, and SaaS are highest exposure.
  Estimated: moderate-to-high proportion of EU-tier customer base.

Customer notice required: YES — by 2026-07-14 (43 days from today).
  Customers must be told to: (a) audit existing email pixel deployments,
  (b) surface a consent mechanism at list-signup, and (c) notify pre-existing
  contacts and give opt-out before July 14.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CNIL has issued fines totalling ~EUR 500 million in 2025 alone. It actively
investigates cookie/tracker complaints and has a track record of self-initiated
sweeps. Article 82 of the French Data Protection Act enables fines up to
EUR 20 million or 4% of global turnover (whichever is higher) for the controller.
DataSafeguard customers who are found to have operated non-compliant email-pixel
tracking after July 14 face direct exposure. DataSafeguard itself faces contractual
and reputational risk if the platform does not support compliant email-pixel consent
flows, since customers may cite the CMP's lack of functionality as a contributing
factor. CNIL enforcement of email-pixel violations is a new and active area
following this recommendation.

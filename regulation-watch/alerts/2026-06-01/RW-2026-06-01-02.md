WHAT CHANGED (plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Prior rule: Under the pre-DUAA version of PECR Regulation 6, only two categories
of storage/access technologies were exempt from the consent requirement: (a) those
strictly necessary to provide the service requested by the user, and (b) those
necessary to carry out a communication. All analytics cookies, functional
preference cookies, and security cookies required either consent or fell into a
contested "strictly necessary" interpretation.

New rule: The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 amended PECR Regulation 6, effective
5 February 2026, to add three new consent-exempt categories of Storage and Access
Technologies (SATs): (1) Statistical Purposes — analytics/measurement technologies
used solely to understand how a service or website is used in order to make
improvements, provided the resulting data is aggregated and cannot identify
individuals, and provided users are given a simple, free means to object to their
use; (2) Appearance / Functionality — technologies that adapt a service's appearance
or functions to a user's stated preference or enhance display/functionality (e.g.,
remembering dark-mode choice, font size, language preference); (3) Security — a
further exception (the exact scope confirmed in ICO guidance) for fraud detection,
system security, and similar protective purposes. The ICO published its finalised
guidance on 29 April 2026 after two public consultations (December 2024, July 2025).
The DUAA simultaneously raised the maximum PECR monetary penalty to GBP 17.5
million or 4% of annual worldwide turnover (whichever is higher), bringing it in
line with UK GDPR penalties.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHO IS AFFECTED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Applicability test: Organisations operating websites or apps accessible to UK
  users, or established in the UK, that deploy cookies or similar storage/access
  technologies. The new exemptions are optional reliefs from the consent
  requirement — organisations that prefer to keep consent-based flows may do so.

DataSafeguard customers likely affected: All customers who serve UK-resident users
  and currently require consent for analytics or functionality cookies under the
  old PECR rules. This represents a significant compliance simplification
  opportunity but also a re-classification obligation: customers must actively
  decide whether to rely on the new exemptions and must update their cookie
  banners, cookie policies, and CMP configurations accordingly. Customers who
  continue presenting analytics cookies as requiring consent but later switch to
  the exemption mid-lifecycle also need a consent-withdrawal / notice-update flow.

Customer notice required: YES — customers should be advised immediately.
  The PECR changes are already in effect (since 2026-02-05) and the guidance
  has been finalised. Customers operating under old configurations may be
  mis-categorising cookies and presenting unnecessary consent banners, or
  conversely may not be providing the required "simple means to object" for
  statistical-purpose cookies.

ID-PRIVACY(R) IMPACT
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Affected subsystems:
  [X] Consent Banner               — UK-scoped banners must be updated to reflect
                                     the new exemption options. If a customer
                                     elects to rely on the statistical-purposes
                                     exemption, analytics cookies should no longer
                                     appear in the consent layer; instead, an
                                     information notice + opt-out mechanism is
                                     required. Banners that still gate analytics
                                     cookies behind consent while the exemption is
                                     in force are not wrong but represent unnecessary
                                     friction. Banners must be reclassified per
                                     customer choice. The appearance/functionality
                                     exemption means preference cookies can now be
                                     quietly moved out of the consent layer entirely
                                     for UK audiences. Cookie-wall (no-access-without-
                                     consent) patterns for analytics-only sites may
                                     now be challengeable as unnecessary dark patterns
                                     if analytics qualify for the exemption.

  [X] Cookie Classification ML     — The UK cookie taxonomy must be updated. Current
                                     ML categories likely include "Analytics/Performance"
                                     requiring consent under UK rules. A new sub-
                                     classification is needed: "Analytics — UK SAT
                                     Exempt (statistical purposes)" vs. "Analytics —
                                     Consent required". Similarly "Functional/Preference
                                     — UK SAT Exempt (appearance)" must be a distinct
                                     label. The classifier must flag whether the
                                     statistical-data-stays-aggregated condition can
                                     be asserted for a given cookie (this may require
                                     a manual override / vendor attestation field).

  [X] Consent Storage & Proof      — For cookies reclassified as exempt, no consent
                                     record is needed. However, if users exercise
                                     their opt-out right for statistical-purpose
                                     SATs, that opt-out signal must be stored and
                                     honoured. Storage schema should accommodate
                                     "opt-out of exempt SAT" as a distinct event
                                     type, separate from consent-grant/withdrawal.

  [X] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP) — The UK diverges from EU GDPR/ePrivacy here:
                                      consent strings (TCF) are not required for
                                      exempt SATs. GPC signal handling: GPC in the
                                      UK context most naturally maps to the opt-out
                                      from statistical-purpose SATs. The platform
                                      should honour GPC as a "simple means to object"
                                      to statistical-purpose SATs under the ICO
                                      guidance, consistent with the ICO's existing
                                      GPC-favourable stance.

  [X] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing   — A new UK-specific ruleset branch must be
                                     created (or the existing UK branch updated) in
                                     the jurisdiction router to distinguish:
                                     (a) consent-required cookies (advertising,
                                     cross-site tracking, profiling),
                                     (b) SAT-exempt cookies (statistical, functional,
                                     security), and
                                     (c) strictly-necessary (existing exemption).
                                     This is a three-tier classification replacing
                                     the previous two-tier model for UK visitors.

  [ ] Vendor / SDK Governance      — No immediate change to vendor disclosure
                                     requirements, but vendor attestations may need
                                     to confirm that analytics SDKs produce only
                                     aggregated, non-identifiable outputs if the
                                     customer wishes to rely on the statistical-
                                     purposes exemption.

  [ ] DSR Engine                   — The "opt-out of statistical SAT" is not a
                                     standard DSR but must be honoured technically.
                                     If the DSR engine handles opt-out requests, a
                                     new request type may be needed.

  [X] Audit / Logging              — ICO enforcement requires evidence that:
                                     (a) the conditions for the exemption are met
                                     (e.g., data remains aggregated),
                                     (b) a "simple means to object" was provided and
                                     honoured, and (c) the organisation can
                                     demonstrate its classification decision.
                                     Audit logs must capture exemption-basis decisions
                                     and opt-out events.

  [ ] Cross-border transfer        — No new SCC/adequacy change triggered directly,
                                     but if analytics data previously collected
                                     under consent is now collected under exemption
                                     and transferred to a US vendor, the transfer
                                     legal basis analysis should be re-confirmed.

  [X] Tenant Config / Admin UI     — New configuration options needed per tenant
                                     for UK jurisdiction:
                                     - "Rely on statistical-purposes SAT exemption"
                                       (requires OPT-OUT mechanism, not consent)
                                     - "Rely on appearance/functionality SAT exemption"
                                       (no consent or opt-out mechanism required)
                                     These should be surfaced as UK-specific
                                     compliance options in the admin UI with clear
                                     explanatory text and the ICO guidance link.

  [X] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA  — For cookies reclassified to exemption, the
                                     legal basis in the RoPA shifts from "consent"
                                     to "statutory exemption — PECR Reg 6(2A)".
                                     Cookie policy template must be updated for
                                     UK-scoped customers to explain the new
                                     exemption structure and the opt-out right for
                                     statistical SATs.

RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PECR penalty ceiling has been raised from GBP 500,000 to GBP 17.5 million (or
4% of annual worldwide turnover) by the DUAA. The ICO completed a sweep of the
UK's top 1,000 websites in April 2026 and has signalled continued enforcement
activity. Customers relying on ID-PRIVACY(R) who are not configured for the new
three-tier UK SAT model risk: (a) over-obtaining consent for now-exempt cookies
(creating unnecessary friction and potential mismatch if consent records are later
audited against a changed legal basis), and (b) missing the opt-out mechanism
obligation for statistical-purpose SATs, which the ICO treats as a hard requirement
for the exemption to hold. Either scenario exposes customers to regulatory scrutiny
under the heightened penalty regime. DataSafeguard faces customer contractual
exposure for failing to support a compliant UK configuration.

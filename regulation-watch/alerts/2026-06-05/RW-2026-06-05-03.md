WHAT CHANGED
---------------------------------------------------------------
South Korea's National Assembly passed an amendment to the Personal Information
Protection Act (PIPA) on 12 February 2026. The headline change is a new maximum
administrative fine of 10% of total annual revenue for the most severe categories
of violation (intentional or grossly negligent repeat violations within 3 years;
incidents affecting 10 million or more individuals; or failure to comply with a
PIPC corrective order followed by a breach). Previously the maximum was 3% of
relevant turnover. The amendment also designates the CEO as the ultimate
responsible person for data protection, expands the definition of notifiable
"data breach" to include forgery, alteration, and destruction (not only loss/
theft/disclosure), and requires notification even before conclusive verification
in some cases. Core consent requirements for cookies and behavioral tracking under
PIPA are not substantively changed; the amendment does not add new cookie-specific
obligations. The higher penalty ceiling changes the financial risk calculus for
customers using ID-PRIVACY for Korean traffic.

---------------------------------------------------------------
WHO IS AFFECTED
---------------------------------------------------------------
- Applicability test:   Organisations controlling or processing personal
                        information of Korean data subjects (territorial scope
                        extends to foreign operators via PIPA Article 3 read
                        with PIPC guidance).
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: Customers who operate Korean-
  language sites or target Korean users; typically large platform or
  e-commerce customers.
- Customer notice required: YES -- customers with Korean traffic should be
  notified of the elevated fine ceiling when the amendment becomes effective
  (2026-08-12) so they can reassess their PIPA compliance posture.
  Recommend advisory note within 60 days.

RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
---------------------------------------------------------------
The primary risk is that customers are unaware of the elevated fine ceiling
(10% vs prior 3% of revenue) and do not adjust their PIPA compliance posture
before the effective date. For a large platform customer, 10% of global revenue
represents a materially larger exposure. There is no risk of ID-PRIVACY platform
non-compliance from this amendment (no new technical obligations for the CMP).
The reputational risk is customer surprise if a PIPC enforcement action occurs
post-2026-08-12 and the customer was not warned. PIPC has been increasingly active
in 2025-2026, including high-profile investigations into global platforms.

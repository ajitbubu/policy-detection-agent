---------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT CHANGED
---------------------------------------------------------------
The CNIL adopted Recommendation No. 2026-042 on 12 March 2026 (published 14 April
2026) extending the French cookie consent rule (Article 82 of the French Data
Protection Act, transposing ePrivacy Directive Art. 5(3)) explicitly to invisible
tracking pixels embedded in emails. Under the prior interpretive landscape, email
open-tracking pixels occupied an ambiguous zone. The CNIL now requires prior
consent from the email recipient as the general rule for any pixel that analyses
open rates, builds recipient behavioural profiles, or detects suspected fraud. Two
narrow exemptions exist: (a) pixels used solely to facilitate the email
communication itself (transmission exemption) and (b) pixels strictly necessary to
deliver a service the recipient explicitly requested. For existing contact lists
(addresses collected before 14 April 2026), organisations have until 14 July 2026
to notify subscribers about pixel tracking and provide a real means to object; this
is not a retroactive consent collection obligation but a notification-and-opt-out
obligation. For new subscribers acquired from 14 April 2026 onward, a compliant
consent collection flow must be in place from the moment of email address
collection -- consent cannot be collected inside a welcome email because the open
event records data before the recipient can respond.

---------------------------------------------------------------
WHO IS AFFECTED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Applicability test:  Any data controller (sender) using tracking pixels in emails
  sent to recipients in France, regardless of sender establishment. Applies under
  Art. 82 French Data Protection Act and territorial scope of ePrivacy / GDPR.
DataSafeguard customers likely affected: Any tenant who deploys email marketing
  or transactional email pixels (ESP integrations, CRM tracking, CDP attribution
  pixels) involving French recipients. Email-channel tracking is a standard feature
  in martech stacks and most mid-to-large tenants will have some French email
  audience. Particularly relevant to tenants in e-commerce, media, and SaaS sectors.
Customer notice required: YES -- (a) by 2026-07-14, tenants with existing French
  contact lists using open-tracking pixels must have issued subscriber notification
  and opt-out; (b) tenants who have not done this may need ID-PRIVACY's help
  generating consent re-confirmation flows or "email pixel consent" banners. Alert
  should be sent to affected customer segment within 14 days.

---------------------------------------------------------------
ID-PRIVACY IMPACT
---------------------------------------------------------------
Affected subsystems:

  [X] Consent Banner
      The primary impact is NOT the web cookie banner but the consent capture
      point at email subscription. ID-PRIVACY's banner/form overlay and preference
      centre flows need a new "Email Tracking Pixels" purpose category that can be
      surfaced at point-of-email-address-collection (e.g., in a sign-up form
      overlay or inline preference centre), distinct from web cookie purposes.
      This is a new purpose in the consent taxonomy: `email_tracking_pixels` under
      jurisdiction `FR` (and potentially other EU jurisdictions that follow CNIL's
      lead).

  [ ] Cookie Classification ML
      No change -- email pixels are out-of-scope for the web cookie scanner.
      However, if ID-PRIVACY's scanner can detect ESP tracking pixels loaded on
      web pages (confirmation pages, thank-you pages), a new label
      `email_tracking_pixel` would be useful metadata for tenant reporting.
      Flag as a stretch goal, not a compliance blocker.

  [X] Consent Storage and Proof
      Proof-of-consent for email pixel tracking must capture: the disclosure
      shown at subscription (i.e., what tracking purposes were disclosed), the
      affirmative act timestamp, and the channel (email vs. web). The existing
      consent schema should accommodate this with a `channel: email` field and a
      `purpose: email_tracking_pixels` value. For the transition-period
      notification-and-opt-out events, store a `fr_pixel_notification_sent`
      event and any resulting `fr_pixel_opt_out` event per contact.

  [ ] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP)
      No direct change -- GPC/TCF strings cover web tracking. Email pixel consent
      does not propagate via TCF strings. No IAB framework currently covers email
      pixel consent signals.

  [X] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing
      The `email_tracking_pixels` purpose category should be gated by a `FR`
      (and potentially broader EU) jurisdiction flag. For tenants that route
      French users to a separate EU ruleset, the existing geo-routing infrastructure
      can carry this but needs the new purpose exposed as a configurable item.

  [ ] Vendor / SDK Governance
      Email service providers (ESPs: Mailchimp, Klaviyo, Braze, Salesforce
      Marketing Cloud, etc.) are the relevant vendors. ID-PRIVACY's vendor
      governance module should flag ESPs as potentially deploying email tracking
      pixels and prompt tenants to confirm consent-collection practices.
      No automated SDK gating possible in email context; this is a documentation
      and policy flow, not a technical gate.

  [ ] DSR Engine
      No new rights specific to this recommendation; existing Art. 17 GDPR
      deletion and Art. 21 objection rights already cover this. Ensure "email
      tracking pixels" purposes are included in the DSAR data-map so that a
      deletion or opt-out request from a French contact flows through correctly.

  [X] Audit / Logging
      Log events: `fr_email_pixel_notification_sent` (with notification text hash
      and timestamp), `fr_email_pixel_opt_out` (with timestamp), and
      `email_pixel_consent_granted` (with disclosure hash and action timestamp).
      Retention: align with CNIL enforcement investigation window (at minimum
      3 years recommended for France).

  [ ] Cross-border transfer
      No change from this recommendation specifically.

  [X] Tenant Config / Admin UI
      New tenant configuration item: "Email Pixel Consent (France)" -- enable/
      disable, configure disclosure text, configure transition-notification flow.
      Admin UI should surface the 2026-07-14 deadline and prompt tenants who have
      not yet configured the notification flow.

  [X] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA
      Update DPIA/RoPA templates to add an "email tracking pixels" processing
      activity row for FR-scoped tenants. Document legal basis as consent (Art. 6
      (1)(a) GDPR) plus Article 82 LIL compliance via the CNIL recommendation.

RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
---------------------------------------------------------------
The CNIL issued EUR 486.8 million in fines in 2025, the largest annual total in its
history, with cookie-related violations accounting for the majority. The 90-day
transition period (expiring 14 July 2026) is explicitly a grace period before
enforcement begins; CNIL has stated enforcement actions will follow expiration.
Fines under Art. 83 GDPR can reach EUR 20 million or 4% of global annual turnover.
Customer exposure: any DataSafeguard tenant operating email marketing to French
recipients without a compliant consent or notification flow as of 14 July 2026 is
directly exposed. ID-PRIVACY's platform does not currently surface email pixel
consent as a distinct managed purpose, meaning tenants relying on the platform
alone have a gap. Reputational: CNIL routinely names companies in enforcement
decisions; a named tenant who used ID-PRIVACY would ask why the platform did not
flag or address this in time.

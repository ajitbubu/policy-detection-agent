---------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT CHANGED
---------------------------------------------------------------
The ICO finalised its "Guidance on the Use of Storage and Access Technologies" on
29 April 2026, operationalising three new cookie consent exemptions that the Data
(Use and Access) Act 2025 (DUAA) introduced into PECR with effect from 5 February
2026. The three new exemptions -- Statistical Purposes, Appearance, and Emergency
Assistance -- sit alongside the two pre-existing exemptions (Communication and
Strictly Necessary), meaning UK organisations may now deploy five categories of
storage and access technologies without triggering the PECR consent requirement.
The guidance also adds two new sub-chapters clarifying (a) what "a simple means of
objecting, free of charge" means for the Statistical and Appearance exemptions, and
(b) how to handle a technology that serves multiple purposes where only some are
exempt. The PECR fine ceiling simultaneously increased from GBP 500,000 to GBP
17.5M / 4% global turnover on 5 February 2026 under the DUAA; this guidance now
tells organisations which banners they actually need to show under the new regime.

---------------------------------------------------------------
WHO IS AFFECTED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Applicability test:  Any controller deploying storage or access technologies
  (cookies, pixels, fingerprinting, local storage, etc.) to users in the UK under
  PECR. Establishment test and targeting test both apply; non-UK organisations
  targeting UK users are in scope.
DataSafeguard customers likely affected: All tenants who have enabled a UK ruleset
  (or who target UK traffic without a dedicated ruleset and are defaulting to EU/
  GDPR rules). Estimated to be a large share of the EU/UK customer base.
Customer notice required: YES -- customers operating UK properties should be
  informed that (a) three new consent-exempt categories are now available, reducing
  required consent gates, and (b) they must add a "simple means of objecting" UI
  element for Statistical and Appearance technologies. Timeline: within 30 days.

---------------------------------------------------------------
ID-PRIVACY IMPACT
---------------------------------------------------------------
Affected subsystems:

  [X] Consent Banner
      New UK-specific behaviour required:
      (1) Analytics/statistics cookies used for the "sole purpose" of improving the
          service may now be deployed without a consent gate in UK -- but ONLY if
          they do not simultaneously feed advertising targeting. The banner must
          expose a "simple means of objecting" (an opt-out control) for this
          category, not a full consent prompt.
      (2) Appearance/preference cookies (dark-mode detection, language preference,
          layout adaptation) may be deployed without consent in UK but require the
          same simple opt-out.
      (3) Emergency-assistance cookies (geolocation for emergency services) are
          fully exempt with no opt-out requirement.
      Current banner templates likely show a consent gate for analytics in UK, which
      is now unnecessarily strict but must add an objection mechanism if relaxed.
      Practical change: add "Opt-out" toggle for Statistical and Appearance
      categories in UK ruleset, rather than "Accept/Reject" toggle.

  [ ] Cookie Classification ML
      No change to classifier categories required; existing "analytics" and
      "preferences/functionality" labels map correctly to the new exemptions.
      Metadata flag needed: `ukExempt = statistical | appearance | emergency` per
      cookie record, to drive banner rendering logic.

  [X] Consent Storage and Proof
      For cookies served under a PECR exemption (not consent), no consent record
      needs to be stored -- but an opt-OUT record must be stored if the user
      exercises the "simple means of objecting." Schema update needed: add an
      `ukPecrExemptionOptOut` event type alongside existing consent events.

  [ ] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP)
      No change -- GPC/TCF signals remain unchanged; PECR exemptions are a
      separate legal-basis track that does not flow through TCF strings.

  [X] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing
      The UK ruleset must now diverge from the EU/ePrivacy ruleset on analytics
      and preferences categories. Tenants who share an EU+UK template today must
      either (a) split into separate UK and EU templates, or (b) add a UK-specific
      override layer that relaxes the consent gate for Statistical and Appearance
      and substitutes an opt-out control. This is the single most operationally
      significant change.

  [ ] Vendor / SDK Governance
      No new gating requirement -- the exemptions apply to first-party statistical
      and appearance use cases. If a vendor SDK is used for these purposes it
      benefits from the exemption, but the sole-purpose test must be verified per
      vendor. Add a metadata flag `ukPecrExemptionEligible` to vendor records.

  [ ] DSR Engine
      No new rights created; existing UK GDPR access/deletion rights unchanged.

  [X] Audit / Logging
      Log opt-out events under the new "simple means of objecting" flow. Ensure
      the audit trail distinguishes "user objected to Statistical exemption" from
      "user withdrew consent," as the legal basis and downstream effect differ.

  [ ] Cross-border transfer
      No change.

  [X] Tenant Config / Admin UI
      New toggle per UK ruleset: "Deploy Statistical cookies under PECR exemption
      (UK only) -- requires simple opt-out UI." Similar toggle for Appearance. A
      warning should fire if the tenant enables the Statistical exemption for a
      cookie that is also mapped to a vendor SDK that feeds advertising (which would
      fail the sole-purpose test).

  [X] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA
      Update UK-jurisdiction template notes to reflect that analytics/statistics
      processing may rely on PECR exemption rather than consent, and to document
      that an opt-out mechanism is in place. DPIA/RoPA rows for UK analytics
      processing should be updated.

RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
---------------------------------------------------------------
PECR fine ceiling is now GBP 17.5 million or 4% of global annual turnover -- a
35x increase from the prior GBP 500,000 cap. The ICO has stated it is actively
monitoring the top 1,000 UK websites. The primary compliance risk is the reverse
of most cookie alerts: tenants who rely on an unnecessarily narrow consent gate
(requiring consent for analytics when the PECR exemption would suffice) are not
directly exposed, but tenants who fail to add the "simple means of objecting"
opt-out for Statistical and Appearance technologies are out of step with the
guidance and potentially exposed to enforcement. Customer contractual exposure:
tenant MSAs typically warrant PECR compliance; a PECR enforcement notice against
a tenant that lacked a proper opt-out mechanism could be traced to platform
misconfiguration.

CONFIDENCE
---------------------------------------------------------------
HIGH on the substantive facts (publication date 2026-04-29; three new DUAA
exemptions: Statistical, Appearance, Emergency; "simple means of objecting"
requirement for Statistical and Appearance; PECR fine ceiling GBP 17.5M / 4%).
All material elements are corroborated across the ICO news announcement URL and
five independent primary-tier secondary sources (Bird & Bird, ZwillGen, Womble
Bond Dickinson, Costello Advisory, ICO exceptions sub-page). Confidence is
reduced to MEDIUM on the precise UX standard for "simple means of objecting"
because the ICO guidance explicitly states PECR does not define this, and the
ICO sub-chapter text says it "could be provided through the existing consent
mechanism" -- counsel should confirm whether a preference-centre link satisfies
this or whether a persistent on-page control is expected. Severity HIGH (not
CRITICAL) because the guidance does not ban any existing ID-PRIVACY behaviour;
it creates an optional new feature (PECR exemption mode) and an associated
opt-out obligation.

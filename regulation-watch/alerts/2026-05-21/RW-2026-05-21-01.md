WHAT CHANGED (<=4 sentences, plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
On 29 April 2026 the ICO published the final version of its comprehensive guidance on storage and access technologies (cookies, tracking pixels, device fingerprinting, and equivalents), consolidating two prior draft consultations and codifying the PECR changes that took legal effect on 5 February 2026 under the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (DUAA). The most material change for a CMP is the introduction of five new categories of tracking technology that are now exempt from the PECR consent requirement — covering technologies used: (1) for transmission of a communication, (2) to provide a service the user has explicitly requested, (3) to collect aggregate statistical information to improve the service (analytics exemption), (4) to improve or adapt appearance to a user's preference, and (5) to identify a user requiring emergency assistance — but each exemption is strictly purpose-limited and the analytics exemption explicitly does not extend to analytics data fed into advertising targeting. The guidance also raised the PECR maximum fine ceiling from £500,000 to £17.5 million or 4% of global annual turnover (whichever is higher), a 35-fold increase that fundamentally changes the risk calculus for cookie-banner quality, and added two new sub-chapters clarifying (a) what "a simple means of objecting" requires in practice (the objection mechanism must be no harder to access than the original consent mechanism), and (b) when the same technology used for multiple simultaneous purposes requires separate disclosures. On affiliate marketing pixels the ICO confirmed explicitly that a tracking pixel used to attribute affiliate-link clicks and conversions requires consent, closing a previously ambiguous gap.

WHO IS AFFECTED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Applicability test:  Any organisation that deploys cookies, pixels, or device fingerprinting on devices of users in the UK, regardless of the organisation's place of establishment. PECR applies to the sender/setter of the technology, not only to data controllers established in the UK.
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: All ID-PRIVACY tenants with UK web traffic. This is the entire UK-routed customer segment. Tenants using analytics SDKs that also feed advertising should review immediately whether their current analytics-exemption claim is defensible. Tenants using affiliate tracking pixels must ensure those pixels are consent-gated in the UK banner.
- Customer notice required: YES — within 21 days (by 2026-06-11). Notice should cover: (a) new analytics consent-exemption availability (and its limitations), (b) affiliate pixel now explicitly requires consent in UK, (c) banner objection mechanism parity requirement, (d) new PECR fine ceiling that affects their own risk exposure.

ID-PRIVACY® IMPACT
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Affected subsystems:
  [X] Consent Banner               — Three changes: (1) Banner logic for UK jurisdiction must offer an objection mechanism ("Reject All" or equivalent persistent opt-out) that is no harder to reach than the initial consent action — if "Accept All" is one click on Layer 1, a compliant objection path must also be one click on Layer 1 or an equally prominent and accessible path. (2) Analytics cookies / pixels that are used exclusively for aggregate service-improvement stats may now be displayed to UK users as exempt (no consent toggle required for that category), but the platform must enforce that this exemption fires only when the analytics vendor is not also used for advertising targeting. (3) Affiliate tracking pixels must be categorised as requiring consent in UK-jurisdiction banners; any current configuration classifying them as "functional" or "strictly necessary" is non-compliant.
  [X] Cookie Classification ML     — Three classifier updates required: (a) Add a new label "analytics-exempt-UK" for technologies that qualify for the DUAA statistical-improvement exemption, with a hard exclusion rule blocking that label from any vendor also tagged "advertising" or "targeting." (b) Add "affiliate-tracking" as a sub-category that inherits consent-required=true for UK routing. (c) The existing "functional" and "strictly necessary" labels must be audited to ensure no affiliate pixels are incorrectly assigned those labels in the UK rule set.
  [X] Consent Storage & Proof      — For technologies newly exempt from consent under DUAA, the consent record schema must record the exemption basis claimed (new field: `pecrExemptionBasis` with enumerated values matching the five DUAA categories) rather than a consent grant. This is necessary to demonstrate to ICO that no consent was required, not that consent was absent.
  [ ] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP)— No change to signal propagation logic. UK consent exemptions operate at the banner/classification layer, not the signal layer.
  [X] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing   — UK routing rule set must be updated to: (a) activate the five new PECR exemption flags, (b) block the analytics exemption for dual-use analytics+advertising vendors, (c) enforce affiliate-pixel consent-required. This is a config-layer change to the UK ruleset; no change to other jurisdiction rulesets.
  [X] Vendor / SDK Governance      — Vendor library must be annotated with: (a) `pecrExemptionEligible: [list of applicable exemption codes]`, (b) `usedForAdvertisingTargeting: true/false` (determines analytics exemption blocking), (c) `affiliateTracking: true/false`. These flags drive the UK classification auto-assignment.
  [ ] DSR Engine                   — No change.
  [X] Audit / Logging              — Audit logs for UK-routed sessions must record the exemption basis claimed for any technology not presented with a consent toggle. ICO enforcement pattern is increasingly granular; logging the exemption basis is the evidentiary foundation for any ICO investigation response.
  [ ] Cross-border transfer        — No change.
  [X] Tenant Config / Admin UI     — New toggles per UK ruleset: "Enable DUAA analytics exemption (UK)" with a confirmation gate that the tenant has verified the analytics vendor is not used for advertising targeting. Warning label: "Enabling this exemption for a dual-use analytics+advertising vendor is non-compliant and could expose you to ICO enforcement at the new £17.5M penalty ceiling."
  [X] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA  — Update UK PECR guidance section in all customer-facing documentation. Update sample UK DPIA to reference the DUAA PECR changes and the new penalty ceiling. Add a worked example on the analytics exemption and affiliate pixel treatment.

RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Penalty ceiling: £17,500,000 or 4% of global annual turnover per enforcement action — a 35-fold increase from the prior £500,000 ceiling. The ICO has demonstrated willingness to fine large organisations (British Airways 2019, Marriott 2020, TikTok 2023 at £12.7M, etc.). The affiliate pixel finding is immediately actionable: any ID-PRIVACY tenant whose UK banner does not gate affiliate tracking pixels behind consent is now in direct conflict with the ICO's explicitly stated position. The objection parity requirement is a known ICO enforcement priority (it featured in the ICO's 2023-2025 cookie sweep). Tenants who configured banners under the old £500,000 risk model may have accepted banner-design shortcuts that are not sustainable at the £17.5M ceiling. Customer contractual exposure: most tenant MSAs warrant compliance with applicable law; the ICO guidance is the applicable standard in the UK. ID-PRIVACY reputational exposure: the ICO guidance has been widely reported; competitors will ship the analytics exemption and affiliate-pixel fix quickly.

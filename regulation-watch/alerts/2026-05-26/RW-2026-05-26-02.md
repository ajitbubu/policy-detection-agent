WHAT CHANGED (plain English)
---------------------------------------------------------------
Prior to this recommendation, there was no explicit CNIL guidance on whether email
tracking pixels (invisible 1x1 images used to detect email opens, device type, IP
address, and link clicks) required consent under the ePrivacy Directive. Many operators
treated email pixels as analogous to server-side logging rather than as "trackers."
The CNIL's new recommendation (deliberation 2026-042) explicitly applies the same
consent framework as cookies to email tracking pixels: any pixel deployed for marketing,
advertising, or commercial personalisation purposes requires prior, informed, freely
given consent, collected at the moment the email address is first captured.

The recommendation establishes a narrow consent-exempt category: pixels used solely
to measure deliverability at an individual level (e.g., detecting that an address
is permanently inactive, for list hygiene) do not require consent. All other pixels --
open tracking, click tracking, geolocation inference, device fingerprinting, and
conversion attribution -- require consent. Consent must be as easy to refuse as to
grant, withdrawal must be as easy as giving consent, and a reject-all mechanism
must be offered. For new contacts collected from 14 April 2026, these rules apply
immediately with no grace period. For contacts collected before 14 April 2026,
operators must provide clear disclosure of pixel use by 14 July 2026 (T-49 days
from today's scan date of 2026-05-26); failure to do so exposes the full contact
list to an enforcement action.

WHO IS AFFECTED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Applicability test:    Any organisation sending commercial or transactional emails to
                       recipients in France (establishment test or targeting test under
                       GDPR / Loi Informatique et Libertes). Email service providers,
                       marketing automation platforms, and analytics vendors that supply
                       pixel infrastructure are affected as processors.
DataSafeguard customers likely affected: Customers using ID-PRIVACY to manage email
                       consent flows, or customers whose email marketing vendors insert
                       tracking pixels into campaigns reaching French users. Estimate:
                       any customer with EU/FR active deployment.
Customer notice required:  YES -- customer advisory needed by 2026-06-06 warning of the
                       July 14 hard deadline for existing contact lists.

---------------------------------------------------------------
ID-PRIVACY IMPACT
---------------------------------------------------------------
Affected subsystems:
  [X] Consent Banner               -- ID-PRIVACY consent capture widgets embedded in
                                      email signup forms must explicitly surface email
                                      pixel tracking as a separate consent purpose (not
                                      bundled with general cookie consent). The purpose
                                      description must be shown at the point of email
                                      address collection, not at first website visit.
  [X] Cookie Classification ML     -- Email tracking pixels must be classified as a
                                      distinct tracker category ("email pixel / open
                                      tracker") separate from web-embedded analytics
                                      pixels. The classifier's current scope covers
                                      web technologies; email pixel detection is a new
                                      domain. Scope extension required.
  [X] Consent Storage & Proof      -- Email pixel consent must be stored as a distinct
                                      consent record linked to the email address (not
                                      just a cookie/device identifier). Proof-of-consent
                                      must record: (a) when consent was given, (b) the
                                      specific pixel purposes disclosed, (c) the channel
                                      (email signup form vs website). This is a new
                                      consent-record schema extension.
  [ ] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP) -- GPC is a browser-level signal; email pixel
                                       consent is address-level. No direct GPC mapping,
                                       but consent withdrawal mechanism must be provided
                                       (e.g., unsubscribe link that also withdraws pixel
                                       consent).
  [X] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing   -- FR jurisdiction routing must trigger the email-
                                      pixel consent requirement for any consent capture
                                      associated with French users.
  [X] Vendor / SDK Governance      -- Email marketing vendors (ESPs) that inject pixels
                                      must be listed in the vendor disclosure layer as
                                      processors for email tracking. Prior consent must
                                      gate pixel injection by the vendor. New vendor
                                      category needed: "Email analytics / pixel vendor."
  [ ] DSR Engine                   -- Withdrawal of email pixel consent must propagate
                                      to the ESP/pixel vendor; existing DSR engine
                                      may need email-consent-withdrawal workflow.
  [X] Audit / Logging              -- Each email pixel consent record must be auditable:
                                      timestamp, disclosed purposes, consent signal
                                      mechanism, withdrawal events.
  [ ] Cross-border transfer        -- Not directly affected beyond existing SCCs.
  [X] Tenant Config / Admin UI     -- New consent-purpose type: "Email tracking pixel"
                                      with jurisdiction = FR (and EU broadly). Clients
                                      must be able to configure pixel purpose text,
                                      opt-out link, and link to ESP vendor.
  [X] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA  -- RoPA templates for email marketing workflows must
                                      add the pixel-as-tracker disclosure. DPIA for
                                      email marketing use cases needs updating.

RECOMMENDED ACTIONS
---------------------------------------------------------------
ENGINEERING (Abhishek's track):
  1. Extend the consent-record schema to support email-channel consent: add fields
     email_pixel_consent_given (bool), email_pixel_purposes_disclosed (text[]),
     email_pixel_consent_timestamp, email_pixel_withdrawal_timestamp.
     -- est. 8 pts -- by 2026-06-13
  2. Build a new consent-capture widget variant for email signup forms that surfaces
     the "email tracking pixel" purpose inline (not on first web visit). This must
     include a reject-all mechanism for pixel consent.
     -- est. 8 pts -- by 2026-06-27
  3. Add "Email analytics / pixel vendor" as a new vendor category in the Vendor /
     SDK Governance module. Allow customers to map their ESP to this category and
     gate pixel activation on the presence of a valid email pixel consent record.
     -- est. 5 pts -- by 2026-06-27
  4. Implement a consent-withdrawal propagation hook: when email pixel consent is
     withdrawn, call a configurable webhook to the ESP to suppress pixel injection
     for that address. -- est. 5 pts -- by 2026-07-04
  5. Update audit logging to capture email-pixel-specific consent events.
     -- est. 3 pts -- by 2026-06-27

ML (Sumeet's track):
  1. Define and implement a new tracker classification category: "email_pixel /
     open_tracker" distinct from web analytics pixels. This requires training data
     from email HTML samples, not web crawl data -- new data acquisition needed.
     -- est. 13 pts -- by 2026-07-11
  2. Deliverability-only pixel exemption classifier: build a rule or model to
     distinguish CNIL-exempt deliverability measurement pixels from consent-required
     open/click/geo pixels based on pixel endpoint, query parameters, and context.
     -- est. 8 pts -- by 2026-07-18

PRODUCT / LEGAL:
  1. Draft standard email-pixel consent purpose text for FR jurisdiction, compliant
     with CNIL deliberation 2026-042 requirements. -- by 2026-06-06
  2. Issue customer advisory explaining the July 14 deadline for existing contact
     lists, recommended remediation steps, and what ID-PRIVACY changes are coming.
     -- by 2026-06-06 (URGENT -- 49 days to enforcement)
  3. Legal review: confirm deliverability-only exemption scope matches CNIL guidance.
     -- by 2026-06-13
  4. Update email-marketing DPIA template and RoPA addendum. -- by 2026-06-30

DEPENDENCIES / BLOCKERS:
  - CNIL has not yet published formal technical specifications for the pixel consent
    mechanism (e.g., whether a pixel suppression header or ESP-level switch is
    sufficient). Legal interpretation required.
  - ESP vendor cooperation needed for consent-withdrawal propagation (webhook or API).
  - Email-domain training data must be sourced independently of web-crawl corpus.

---------------------------------------------------------------
RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
---------------------------------------------------------------
CNIL has stated enforcement begins 14 July 2026. The CNIL's maximum fine under GDPR
Article 83(5) is EUR 20M or 4% of global annual turnover. The CNIL fined companies
a cumulative ~EUR 500M in 2025 and has a strong track record of following through
after publication of formal recommendations. Customers operating email marketing
to French audiences without compliant pixel consent are in direct violation after
14 July. DataSafeguard's platform, if it enables customers to deploy email workflows
without the pixel consent mechanism, may be characterised as a non-compliant processor
tool, creating contractual and reputational exposure.

WHAT CHANGED (plain English)
---------------------------------------------------------------
The ICO published its finalised "Guidance on the use of storage and access technologies"
on 29 April 2026, superseding the prior 2024 detailed cookies guidance and incorporating
changes mandated by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (DUAA), which came into force on
5 February 2026. The material delta is the addition of THREE new statutory exceptions
to the PECR consent requirement, bringing the total from two to five. Under the old
rules, only "communication" and "strictly necessary" uses were exempt from prior consent.
Under the new rules, three additional categories are exempt without consent -- provided
strict purpose-limitation and transparency conditions are met:

  (1) Statistical purposes -- aggregate statistics on how a service is used, for the
      purpose of service improvement only; no individual profiling permitted.
  (2) Appearance -- adapting how a website or app appears/functions to match the user's
      stated preference or device characteristics (e.g. remembered font size, dark mode).
  (3) Security -- protecting the security of the network or service (fifth exception;
      subject to additional safeguards).

Critically, none of the three new exceptions remove the transparency obligation. Operators
must (a) inform users clearly that these technologies are used, and (b) provide a simple,
free, and effective means to object (opt out). The guidance also adds two new sub-chapters
clarifying what "simple means of objecting" requires and how to handle dual-purpose
technologies used for both exempt and non-exempt purposes (the latter must be consented).

The prior rule permitted only "transmission" and "strictly necessary" exemptions; all
analytics, appearance, and security cookies required prior consent. The new rule carves
these three narrow categories out from the consent gate while preserving opt-out rights.

---------------------------------------------------------------
WHO IS AFFECTED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Applicability test:    Any operator of a UK-directed website or app that deploys cookies
                       or similar storage/access technologies (device fingerprinting,
                       tracking pixels, localStorage, etc.) subject to PECR.
DataSafeguard customers likely affected: All UK-active customers (estimated majority of
                       enterprise tier). Customers in scope must update cookie category
                       configurations, consent logic, and transparency disclosures.
Customer notice required:  YES -- by 2026-06-30 (guidance is already in force; customers
                       need to reconfigure before the ICO's ongoing compliance sweep,
                       which has already assessed the top 1,000 UK websites).

---------------------------------------------------------------
ID-PRIVACY IMPACT
---------------------------------------------------------------
Affected subsystems:
  [X] Consent Banner               -- Three new exempt categories must be surfaced with
                                      opt-out toggle (not consent toggle); layer-1 and
                                      preference-centre UI must distinguish "consent-gated"
                                      from "opt-out-available" categories for UK jurisdiction.
  [X] Cookie Classification ML     -- Classifier must recognise and tag cookies into the
                                      three new ICO-defined exempt categories (Statistical,
                                      Appearance, Security) as distinct from Analytics
                                      (consent-required) and Strictly Necessary. Training
                                      data needs UK-specific labelling pass.
  [ ] Consent Storage & Proof      -- No change to proof-of-consent model for exempted
                                      categories (no consent to store), but opt-out
                                      signals for exempt categories must be logged.
  [X] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP) -- Opt-out mechanism for the three new exempt
                                       categories must be wired to GPC detection: a GPC
                                       signal should be treated as a standing objection
                                       for all non-strictly-necessary storage, including
                                       the new exempt categories.
  [X] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing   -- UK ruleset must activate the new 5-exception logic.
                                      EU/EEA visitors to UK sites still fall under GDPR/
                                      ePrivacy; the new exceptions apply to PECR only.
                                      Routing must not bleed ICO exceptions into EU mode.
  [X] Vendor / SDK Governance      -- Vendors previously classified as consent-required
                                      for analytics/appearance may now qualify as exempt
                                      under ICO rules. Vendor taxonomy needs an ICO-
                                      exception flag so clients can configure correctly.
  [ ] DSR Engine                   -- No change.
  [X] Audit / Logging              -- Opt-out actions on exempt-category technologies
                                      must be logged (new sub-chapter requirement).
  [ ] Cross-border transfer        -- Not directly affected.
  [X] Tenant Config / Admin UI     -- New per-jurisdiction toggle: "ICO exempt categories
                                      enabled" (Statistical / Appearance / Security). Must
                                      default to off until client explicitly enables.
  [X] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA  -- Cookie audit templates and standard DPIAs for UK
                                      deployments need updating to reflect 5-exception model.

RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
---------------------------------------------------------------
PECR penalty ceiling was raised on 2026-02-05 to GBP 17.5M or 4% of global annual
turnover (whichever is higher), matching UK GDPR levels. The ICO has already completed
a compliance sweep of the UK's top 1,000 websites and is actively publishing non-
compliant organisations. Customers whose deployments still treat statistical and
appearance cookies as consent-required will over-collect consent (low direct risk),
but customers who erroneously treat them as no-notice exempt (without providing the
opt-out) face direct enforcement exposure. DataSafeguard's contractual exposure under
DPA clauses is material if the platform ships a technically non-conformant UK configuration.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHAT CHANGED (≤4 sentences, plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Chile is replacing its 1999 data-protection law with a GDPR-inspired regime: a new independent Data Protection Agency (APDP) with investigative, sanctioning and audit powers; full ARCO+ data-subject rights; mandatory 72-hour breach notification; a cross-border transfer framework (adequacy-style safeguards for transfers to non-adequate countries); and fines of up to 20,000 UTM (~US$1.6M) or 4% of revenue on recidivism. The statute does not name "cookies" specifically, but its consent/legal-basis standard and cross-border transfer rules apply directly to online tracking and to any CMP operating in Chile. The 24-month transition window from publication ends, and the law becomes fully operative, on 2026-12-01.

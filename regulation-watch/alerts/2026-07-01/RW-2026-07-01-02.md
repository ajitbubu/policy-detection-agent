───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHAT CHANGED (≤4 sentences, plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The IAB's Fifth Amended and Restated MSPA — the most substantial revision since 2023 — adds advertiser-specific contractual provisions: by default, ad-tech partners and downstream participants are now treated as "service providers" to advertisers under most state privacy laws (with a CCPA-specific carve-out), advertisers are not required to build or deploy new technical opt-out signals (a suppression-list model is retained instead), and permitted data uses are now enumerated to meet CCPA's "limited and specified purpose" requirement. This is primarily a contract-role change among MSPA signatories rather than a new consumer-facing technical signal, but it is paired with an updated MSPA Technical Signaling Implementation Guidelines document (GPP US-National/US-State section fields) that took effect 2026-06-04 — the field-level content of that update has not yet been independently verified in this scan. Because ID-PRIVACY's CMP emits GPP strings on behalf of tenants who are themselves MSPA signatories (publishers) and interacts with downstream advertiser/service-provider role flags, any field or semantic change in the Technical Signaling Guidelines is directly load-bearing for GPP string correctness.

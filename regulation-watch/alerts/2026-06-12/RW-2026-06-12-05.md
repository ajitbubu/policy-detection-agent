───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHAT CHANGED (plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
On 20 March 2025, Mexico enacted a comprehensive new Federal Law on the Protection of Personal Data held by Private Parties (commonly still called LFPDPPP or FLPPDPP), replacing the 2010 statute in its entirety effective 21 March 2025. This law was enacted before this scan's 90-day window (2026-03-13) but its enforcement implications continue to mature and it had not been previously reported in an ID-PRIVACY alert. Key changes relevant to consent and cookies: (1) consent definition tightened — consent must now be "freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous"; the old law required only a "manifestation of will," which was a lower bar; (2) tacit consent still permitted for non-sensitive personal data (cookies that do not collect sensitive, financial, or biometric data), meaning if a privacy notice is displayed and the user does not expressly object, consent may be implied; (3) explicit written consent remains required for sensitive data; (4) enforcement authority transferred from INAI (dissolved) to the Secretariat of Anti-Corruption and Good Governance (SACG), which is a cabinet-level ministry; (5) criminal penalties introduced (previously only administrative); (6) "Aviso de Privacidad" (privacy notice) must disclose use of cookies, web beacons, and similar tools with opt-out instructions.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHO IS AFFECTED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Applicability test:  Private entities that process personal data of Mexican residents, regardless of establishment location, where goods/services are directed at Mexico. Standard targeting test applies.
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: All customers with Mexican user bases or websites targeting Mexican users. Mexico is Latin America's second-largest digital market. Customers in e-commerce, media, and financial services are highest risk.
- Customer notice required:  YES — advisory needed. While the law has already been in effect since March 2025, many customers may not have updated their MX banner configuration to reflect the tightened consent definition. This alert surfaces a compliance gap that should be closed.

ID-PRIVACY IMPACT
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Affected subsystems:
  [X] Consent Banner               — MX-locale banners must: (a) present the Aviso de Privacidad with explicit disclosure of cookies, web beacons, and similar tracking tools, including opt-out instructions; (b) for sensitive data collected via cookies, obtain explicit written consent (opt-in); (c) for non-sensitive cookie data, tacit consent is still valid — a notice with a clear mechanism to reject is sufficient. Banners should not present pre-ticked acceptance as the sole option when sensitive data is involved. Verify that the MX banner template correctly differentiates sensitive vs. non-sensitive cookie categories.
  [X] Cookie Classification ML     — MX locale must correctly classify cookies collecting financial or biometric data as "sensitive — explicit consent required MX." Non-sensitive analytics/functional cookies may still use tacit consent flow in MX. Review MX classification thresholds.
  [X] Consent Storage & Proof      — Consent records for MX must capture whether explicit or tacit consent was used for each category. Proof-of-consent records must be defensible under SACG investigation; SACG has greater investigative powers than legacy INAI.
  [ ] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP)— Mexico does not mandate GPC or TCF. No signal-layer change required.
  [X] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing   — MX ruleset must be reviewed and updated: (a) enforce unambiguous consent language (no "by using this site you agree" patterns); (b) sensitive-cookie categories require explicit opt-in; (c) Aviso de Privacidad must include tracking technology disclosure. Validate current MX ruleset against the 2025 law's requirements.
  [ ] Vendor / SDK Governance      — No new vendor-specific requirement.
  [X] DSR Engine                   — The new law expands data subject rights (access, rectification, erasure, objection, portability-like rights). Verify MX locale DSR engine covers all rights under the new law. SACG complaint filing path may differ from legacy INAI. Update DSR routing documentation.
  [ ] Audit / Logging              — No material change beyond existing proof-of-consent requirements.
  [ ] Cross-border transfer        — Cross-border transfer rules exist under the new law but are not materially different for consent-layer purposes from the 2010 statute.
  [X] Tenant Config / Admin UI     — Update MX jurisdiction label in Admin UI to reference the 2025 law (not the 2010 LFPDPPP). Add configuration note: "SACG is now the enforcement authority." Update any INAI-referencing UI text.
  [X] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA  — Update all MX documentation to reference the new 2025 law and SACG as enforcement body. Remove INAI references. Update privacy notice template for MX to include tracking-technology disclosure section.

RISK IF NOT ACTIONED

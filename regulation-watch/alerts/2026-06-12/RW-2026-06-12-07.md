WHAT CHANGED (plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024 introduced two provisions with 2026 effective dates relevant to ID-PRIVACY customers: (1) From 1 July 2026, AML/CTF-designated entities (real estate agents, lawyers, accountants, conveyancers, dealers in precious metals) that previously fell under the AU$3 million small business exemption are now covered by the Privacy Act and must comply with Australian Privacy Principles — this brings ~100,000 new businesses into Privacy Act scope, many of whom will need consent management solutions for the first time. (2) From 10 December 2026, APP entities using automated decision-making that could significantly affect individuals' rights or interests must disclose in their privacy policy the types of personal information used and the types of decisions made by automated means. This transparency obligation extends to any use of AI/ML to make decisions using cookie-derived data. Neither provision creates new cookie consent requirements per se, but they expand the regulated population and add disclosure obligations that affect privacy policy templates.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHO IS AFFECTED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Applicability test:  Provision 1: AML/CTF-designated entities in Australia. Provision 2: All APP entities using automated decision-making with personal data.
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: Provision 1 — new customer segment opportunity (100,000+ newly regulated businesses needing CMPs); Provision 2 — existing customers using AI-driven personalization or targeting.
- Customer notice required:  NO for existing customers (no banner changes required). YES for product marketing: the July 2026 small-business expansion creates a new prospect segment.

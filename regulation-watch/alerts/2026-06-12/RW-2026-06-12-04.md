WHAT CHANGED (plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The OAIC released an exposure draft of the Privacy (Children's Online Privacy) Code 2026 on 31 March 2026 under a mandate from the Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024. The consultation closed 5 June 2026 (yesterday), and the final Code must be registered by 10 December 2026. The Code applies to operators of designated online services likely to be accessed by children (under 15) or primarily directed at children. Key obligations affecting ID-PRIVACY customers: (1) consent for use of children's personal data in targeted advertising requires explicit parental consent (child under 15 cannot consent independently); (2) age assurance measures are required — operators must take "reasonable steps" to determine whether a user is a child; (3) geolocation tracking notifications must be surfaced to children; (4) personal data must be deleted on child's request; and (5) mandatory privacy impact assessments for high-risk processing involving children. Prior position: Australia's Privacy Act 1988 and Australian Privacy Principles applied to children's data but with no age-specific consent threshold or targeted-advertising ban.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHO IS AFFECTED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Applicability test:  Online service providers whose services are "primarily concerned with the activities of children" or "likely to be accessed by children" in Australia. Operators of apps, games, websites, and social media services. Applies to operators regardless of establishment location if they direct services at Australian children.
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: Any customer operating consumer-facing websites, apps, or games with Australian traffic — especially entertainment, education, gaming, retail, and social media sectors. Customers must determine if their service is likely accessed by children and configure ID-PRIVACY's AU locale accordingly.
- Customer notice required:  YES — advisory needed by 2026-10-01 to give customers time to assess applicability and re-configure AU banners before the December enforcement date.

ID-PRIVACY IMPACT
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Affected subsystems:
  [X] Consent Banner               — AU-locale banners for "children-likely" services must: (a) present a parental consent flow for users under 15 (child cannot independently consent); (b) block targeted advertising cookies by default with no opt-in available for children without parental approval; (c) surface geolocation tracking notification. This requires an age-gating layer in the banner UI — either age-assurance integration or a parental consent gate before any non-essential cookie consent is captured.
  [X] Cookie Classification ML     — AU locale for children-directed services must auto-classify any behavioral/advertising/profiling cookie as "requires parental consent — AU children's code." Add "children's service" flag to AU tenant config that triggers this classification override.
  [X] Consent Storage & Proof      — Consent records for AU children's services must record: whether parental consent was obtained, the child's age-assurance outcome, and the version of the Code in effect. Proof-of-consent schema must be extended for AU-children context.
  [ ] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP)— No direct change; existing GPC/TCF signals apply but parental consent gate precedes any signal processing for children.
  [X] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing   — AU ruleset must fork: AU-general (existing Privacy Act rules) and AU-children (new Code). Trigger AU-children ruleset when tenant has enabled "children-likely service" flag for AU. Route jurisdiction == AU + children-service flag = true to the children ruleset.
  [ ] Vendor / SDK Governance      — No new vendor-specific requirement, but ad-tech and profiling SDKs must be blocked by default for under-15 users in AU.
  [X] DSR Engine                   — Children (and their parents on their behalf) have an explicit right to request deletion of personal data. The DSR engine must support AU-children deletion requests and route them appropriately.
  [X] Audit / Logging              — Parental consent records, age-assurance outcomes, and PIA completion status must be logged for AU-children services.
  [ ] Cross-border transfer        — No direct change.
  [X] Tenant Config / Admin UI     — Add AU tenant flag: "children-likely service" (boolean). When enabled, activate the AU-children ruleset (parental consent gate, advertising cookie block, deletion right). Add PIA reminder in Admin UI for AU-children tenants.
  [X] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA  — Update AU data flow maps to include the Code's mandatory PIA requirement for high-risk processing. Provide a PIA template addendum for AU-children services.

RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The OAIC can enforce the Code as an APP Code with the full force of the Privacy Act 1988, including civil penalty orders of up to AUD 50 million (or greater if based on turnover/benefit) for serious or repeated interference with privacy. Any customer running advertising-supported or profiling-heavy services that attract Australian children without parental consent gates faces direct exposure. DataSafeguard customers in the gaming, education, and entertainment verticals are the highest risk segment. Failure to provide a configurable AU-children mode in ID-PRIVACY would leave those customers unable to comply using the platform.

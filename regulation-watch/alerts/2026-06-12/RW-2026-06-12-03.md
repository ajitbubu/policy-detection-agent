WHAT CHANGED (plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
On 27 January 2026, the European Commission adopted an adequacy decision under GDPR Article 45 recognizing Brazil's LGPD as providing "essentially equivalent" protection to EU law, and Brazil's ANPD simultaneously adopted Resolution No. 32/2026 granting reciprocal adequacy to the EU. This eliminates the need for GDPR Chapter V safeguards (EU Standard Contractual Clauses, Binding Corporate Rules, or other mechanisms) when transferring personal data from the EEA to Brazil. Previously, organizations transferring EU personal data to Brazil were required to implement SCCs or other Article 46 mechanisms. Conversely, Brazil's own transfer regime (ANPD Resolution 19/2024, effective August 2025) requires Brazilian SCCs for transfers from Brazil to non-adequate countries; the EU is now adequate from Brazil's perspective too, so Brazilian-origin data can flow to EU processors without Brazilian SCCs. The adequacy decision is subject to four-year review and an ongoing judicial challenge (Court of Justice Case C-703/25 P concerning the DPF; this decision is a separate instrument and not directly challenged).

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHO IS AFFECTED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Applicability test:  Organizations operating consent management or transferring personal data between EEA and Brazil — both directions. DataSafeguard is affected if its infrastructure processes EU-resident personal data and routes it through Brazilian data centers (or vice versa), or if its customers do.
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: Customers with dual EU and Brazilian user bases who previously implemented EU SCCs for their Brazil data flows. They can now simplify their transfer mechanisms for EU-to-Brazil flows. Customers relying on Brazilian SCCs for Brazil-to-EU flows can also simplify.
- Customer notice required:  NO (this change is beneficial/simplifying). Notify customers as a positive update via release notes or advisory — not a compliance deadline.

Update RoPA transfer-mechanism field options. Update any customer-facing DPIA guidance that previously flagged Brazil as a non-adequate country.

RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Risk of inaction is low but real: customers continuing to rely on SCCs for EU-to-Brazil transfers face no legal penalty (SCCs remain valid), but they incur unnecessary compliance overhead. A greater risk is that ID-PRIVACY's documentation and Admin UI incorrectly flagging Brazil as non-adequate could lead a customer to incorrectly believe they still need to implement SCCs, causing them to configure a more restrictive data flow than required. No fine exposure for DataSafeguard directly from this gap.

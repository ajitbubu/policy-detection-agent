───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHAT CHANGED (plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
India's DPDP Rules 2025 were formally notified on 13 November 2025, creating the country's first full implementing framework under the DPDP Act 2023. The rules introduce a Consent Manager (CM) concept: a registered third-party intermediary that allows users to give, manage, review, and withdraw consent across multiple data fiduciaries from one interoperable platform. Phase II provisions (Rule 4) come into force exactly 12 months after gazette publication — 13 November 2026. The critical ID-PRIVACY implication is that only India-incorporated entities with minimum net worth of INR 2 crore (~USD 240,000) may register as a Consent Manager; foreign CMPs are explicitly excluded from registration. ID-PRIVACY as a foreign-incorporated entity cannot directly register as a Consent Manager but may continue to operate as a technical CMP on behalf of data fiduciaries. This creates an architecture gap: customers in India may need a registered Indian Consent Manager as an intermediary layer, and ID-PRIVACY's consent signals must be interoperable with that layer. The prior position was no specific CM framework existed in India.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHO IS AFFECTED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Applicability test:  The DPDP Act applies to processing of personal data within India and, extraterritorially, to entities offering goods or services to individuals in India, regardless of where data is processed.
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: All customers with Indian user bases or India-targeted websites/apps. DataSafeguard itself is affected if it offers services to Indian users or processes Indian personal data on behalf of customers.
- Customer notice required:  YES — by 2026-10-13 (30 days before effective date) to allow customers to assess whether they need a registered Indian Consent Manager and how their ID-PRIVACY integration must be structured.

ID-PRIVACY IMPACT
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Affected subsystems:
  [X] Consent Banner               — Indian-locale banners must support consent capture in all 22 scheduled Indian languages (or at minimum, English + regional language of the user's state). Banner must present consent as a freely given, specific, informed, unconditional, and unambiguous affirmative action. Services cannot be conditioned on consent for unrelated processing. Current banner templates must be reviewed for India locale compliance.
  [ ] Cookie Classification ML     — No new cookie categories; existing framework applies.
  [X] Consent Storage & Proof      — Consent records for Indian users must be capable of interoperating with a registered Consent Manager's platform via secure APIs. Architecture review needed: can ID-PRIVACY's consent record export satisfy CM interoperability spec? Consent records must be retainable for seven years (CM retention requirement).
  [X] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP)— India does not mandate GPC or TCF. However, ID-PRIVACY must be able to receive consent status signals from a registered Consent Manager acting as intermediary and apply them correctly to downstream vendor firing.
  [X] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing   — IN ruleset must be configured: (a) no service conditioning on consent, (b) multilingual consent notice, (c) children's data prohibition on tracking/behavioral advertising. Route jurisdiction == IN to IN ruleset.
  [ ] Vendor / SDK Governance      — No new vendor-specific requirement beyond existing consent gating.
  [X] DSR Engine                   — Data principals can withdraw consent "as easily as they gave it." DSR engine must support consent withdrawal routed through the registered CM layer as well as direct withdrawal via ID-PRIVACY.
  [X] Audit / Logging              — Seven-year consent record retention required for CM obligations. Verify IN-locale consent logs meet this retention period.
  [ ] Cross-border transfer        — Not directly affected by Phase II; Phase III (May 2027) introduces data localization and transfer restrictions.
  [X] Tenant Config / Admin UI     — Add IN-specific configuration: (a) Consent Manager intermediary endpoint (for customers using a registered Indian CM), (b) language selector for 22-language notice requirement, (c) service-conditioning guard flag.
  [X] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA  — Update data flow maps and DPIAs for Indian operations to document the CM intermediary architecture and 7-year retention obligation.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The DPDP Act imposes financial penalties up to INR 250 crore (~USD 30 million) per violation for data fiduciaries who fail to meet consent obligations. The DPBI's enforcement powers include directing remediation, suspending processing, and imposing fines. India's digital market is too large to ignore — any customer with Indian user traffic that relies on ID-PRIVACY for consent must have a compliant architecture by November 2026. If DataSafeguard has no interoperability story with registered Indian CMs, customers may migrate to India-native CMPs, representing a significant churn risk.

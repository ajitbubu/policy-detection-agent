WHAT CHANGED (≤4 sentences, plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Korea's PIPC can now fine a company up to 10% of total global revenue (not just Korea-related revenue) for the most severe violations — intentional/grossly-negligent repeat violations within 3 years, a single incident affecting ≥10 million data subjects, or ignoring a PIPC corrective order followed by a breach — up from the prior 3%-of-related-revenue ceiling. The amendment also creates personal supervisory liability for CEOs/company representatives (now designated the "ultimate person responsible" for personal-information protection), tightens breach-notification timing to a "credible likelihood of compromise" standard (notify before full verification, not after), and mandates ISMS-P certification for designated large controllers (deferred to 2027-07-01). PIPA's existing cookie-consent rule is unchanged (opt-in consent already required where cookie data can be combined with other data to identify an individual) — the delta is entirely in penalty severity, personal accountability, and breach-response speed, which sharply raises the cost of any consent-capture gap that later surfaces in a breach investigation.

WHO IS AFFECTED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Applicability test:  Any data controller/processor subject to PIPA — i.e., any organization processing personal information of individuals in Korea, including foreign entities without a Korean establishment that target Korean users (extraterritorial reach consistent with existing PIPA scope; unchanged by this amendment).
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: any tenant with a Korean-facing property (web/app) or Korean user base of meaningful size, particularly tenants large enough to be at risk of a ≥10-million-data-subject incident or a repeat-violation finding. Higher urgency for tenants likely to be designated "large controllers" (ISMS-P track).
- Customer notice required:  YES — by 2026-08-21 (T-21 days), specifically to Korean-market tenants: explain the new 10% revenue exposure, CEO personal liability, and the practical implication that proof-of-consent completeness/immutability is now a CEO-level risk item, not just a compliance checkbox.

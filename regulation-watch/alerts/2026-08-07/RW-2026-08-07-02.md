WHAT CHANGED (≤4 sentences, plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The ICO restructured its international-transfers guidance in January 2026 to reflect the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025's new "not materially lower" adequacy/safeguards standard (replacing the prior EU-derived "essentially equivalent" framing) and introduced a "three-step test" for identifying restricted transfers. On 2026-07-30 the ICO further clarified that the UK's own adequacy regulations for the US (which underpin the "UK Extension to the EU-US DPF") are legally independent of the European Commission's EU-US adequacy decision — meaning a successful EU-side legal challenge to the EU-US DPF (see the pending CJEU Latombe appeal and the EDPB's 2026-07-31 letter questioning FTC independence post-Trump v. Slaughter) would not automatically invalidate the UK Extension, and vice versa. Practically, this confirms UK and EU transfer-adequacy analyses can now diverge and must be assessed separately rather than treated as a single "DPF-adequate" determination.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHO IS AFFECTED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Applicability test:  Any organization relying on UK adequacy regulations / the UK Extension to the EU-US DPF, or the EU-US DPF itself, to legitimize transfers of tracking/consent data (e.g., analytics, ad-tech, or CMP vendor logs) from the UK or EEA to the US.
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: any tenant transferring UK or EU visitor data (including consent records, cookie IDs, or vendor-SDK payloads) to US-based sub-processors self-certified under the DPF or its UK Extension — i.e., most multi-region tenants using US cloud/analytics/ad-tech vendors behind the ID-PRIVACY CMP.
- Customer notice required:  NO immediate notice required (no operative legal change yet — this is clarifying guidance, not a new restriction). Recommend a proactive advisory note to UK/EU multi-region tenants explaining that UK and EU transfer-adequacy status may diverge going forward, so a single "DPF-covered" vendor tag should no longer be assumed to satisfy both regimes.

WHAT CHANGED
---------------------------------------------------------------
South Korea's National Assembly passed PIPA amendments on 2026-02-12, formally
promulgated on 2026-03-10 with an effective date of 2026-09-11. Three changes
are material to ID-PRIVACY and its Korean-market tenants. First, the maximum
administrative fine ceiling rises to 10% of total global revenue (up from 3%
of related revenue) for repeat or large-scale intentional/grossly negligent
violations, matching or exceeding GDPR-level exposure. Second, the CEO of a
data controller is now the legally designated "ultimate responsible person" for
personal information protection; for large-scale controllers, appointment of
the Chief Privacy Officer must be approved by the Board and reported to PIPC.
Third, the definition of a "breach" now includes forgery, alteration, and
damage (not just loss/theft/leakage), and the notification trigger moves to
"possibility of a breach" -- an earlier threshold. None of these changes alter
Korea's existing opt-in consent requirement for cookie-collected personal data;
they raise the stakes of non-compliance and elevate accountability within
customer organisations.

---------------------------------------------------------------
WHO IS AFFECTED
---------------------------------------------------------------
- Applicability test:  Controllers processing personal information of Korean
  residents, including those doing so via cookies and online tracking technologies.
  PIPA applies on an establishment or data-subject basis.
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: Any ID-PRIVACY tenant whose
  digital properties target Korean users (e-commerce, SaaS, media, fintech
  operating in Korea). Estimated as a moderate subset of the customer base;
  Korean market is significant for APAC-active enterprise tenants.
- Customer notice required: YES -- Korean-active tenants need to be advised
  of the higher fine ceiling and CEO-accountability provisions so they can
  update their internal governance and ensure ID-PRIVACY's consent records are
  adequate as evidentiary support. Recommend customer advisory by 2026-07-15.

RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Korean PIPC has demonstrated active enforcement. The 10% of global revenue
fine ceiling (effective 2026-09-11) is now the highest in APAC and matches
or exceeds GDPR in absolute terms for large businesses. A banner misconfiguration
causing unconsented cookie deployment to Korean users could, under the new
regime, result in a fine materially larger than under the previous 3%-of-
related-revenue ceiling. The CEO-accountability provision also means
DataSafeguard's Korean customers face personal liability risk if compliance
controls are inadequate -- this elevates the reputational and contractual
stakes of an ID-PRIVACY misconfiguration.

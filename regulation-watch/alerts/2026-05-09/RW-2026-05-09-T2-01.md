WHAT CHANGED
---------------------------------------------------------------
The ICO published the final (non-draft) version of its guidance on storage and
access technologies on 29 April 2026, superseding the draft versions circulated
under two consultation rounds in 2024-2025. The guidance formally codifies the
five consent-exempt purposes introduced by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025
(DUAA, commenced 5 Feb 2026): Communication, Strictly Necessary, Statistical
Purposes (first-party analytics), Appearance (user preference/functionality),
and Emergency Assistance. Two new sub-chapters clarify (a) what "simple means
of objecting" means in the UK post-DUAA context, and (b) how to handle storage
technologies that serve multiple simultaneous purposes. Prior guidance (2012
ICO cookie guidance and interim PECR updates) did not recognise the Statistical
and Appearance exemptions; those required consent. The baseline alert
RW-BASELINE-2026-05-09 (item 3) covered the DUAA commencement; this alert
covers the final interpretive guidance that operationalises those exemptions.

---------------------------------------------------------------
WHO IS AFFECTED
---------------------------------------------------------------
- Applicability test:  Any organisation that deploys cookies or similar
  technologies on services targeting UK users (establishment or targeting test
  under UK GDPR / PECR).
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: All tenants with UK geo-routing
  active — any customer serving UK users. Estimated broad applicability.
- Customer notice required: YES — customers need to know they can now remove
  consent gates for qualifying analytics (statistical) and preference
  (appearance) cookies under UK rules without EU equivalence. Recommend
  customer advisory within 30 days.

  [ ] Cookie Classification ML     -- No change to classifier categories; the
                                       five DUAA exemptions map to existing
                                       taxonomy. However, confirm "statistical
                                       purposes" classification is distinct

                                       (combo use loses exemption).
  [ ] Consent Storage & Proof      -- No change to proof-of-consent schema for
                                       consented categories; exempted categories
                                       do not require a consent record but may
                                       require an audit trail that the exemption
                                       condition is met.
  [ ] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP)-- No change; UK PECR exemptions are
                                       purpose-based, not signal-based.
  [X] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing   -- New sub-ruleset "UK-DUAA" must diverge
                                       from "EU-ePrivacy" on Statistical and
                                       Appearance categories. Must not bleed
                                       into EU tenants.
  [ ] Vendor / SDK Governance      -- No direct change; vendors serving
                                       exempted purposes still need disclosure
                                       but not consent gating under UK rules.
  [ ] DSR Engine                   -- No change.
  [X] Audit / Logging              -- Guidance recommends maintaining records
                                       showing a technology actually meets an
                                       exemption's single-purpose test. Admin
                                       UI should allow tenants to tag a cookie
                                       with its claimed DUAA exemption and log
                                       that classification decision.
  [ ] Cross-border transfer        -- No change.
  [X] Tenant Config / Admin UI     -- UK tenants need a new toggle: "Apply DUAA
                                       exemptions" per cookie category. Default

                                       avoid inadvertent EU misapplication.
  [X] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA  -- DUAA exemption claims should be reflected
                                       in RoPA entries; DPIA template for UK
                                       should reference the five-exemption list.

RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
---------------------------------------------------------------
No immediate fine risk to DataSafeguard -- the risk is competitive and
contractual: UK tenants whose banners over-collect consent for Statistical and
Appearance cookies are technically compliant but create unnecessary consent
friction. Competitors who implement DUAA relief will offer lower bounce rates.
More acutely, any UK tenant that misapplies the DUAA exemption to a cookie
that is not single-purpose (e.g., analytics + retargeting combo) is exposed to
ICO enforcement under PECR with the post-DUAA ceiling of GBP 17.5M / 4% of
global turnover. DataSafeguard's classifier must not tag mixed-purpose cookies
as exempt.

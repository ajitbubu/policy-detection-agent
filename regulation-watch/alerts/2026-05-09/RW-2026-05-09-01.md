WHAT CHANGED (≤4 sentences, plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The FTC's stipulated order against Kochava and its subsidiary Collective Data Solutions (CDS) bars the sale, license, transfer, sharing, or disclosure of "sensitive location data" — defined as precise location data tied to medical facilities, religious sites, schools/childcare for minors, homeless shelters, domestic-violence shelters, and military or federal-law-enforcement sites — unless the data broker has obtained the consumer's "affirmative express consent" AND the use is to provide a service the consumer directly requested. The order also requires (i) a documented sensitive-location-data program with an explicit blocklist, (ii) a supplier-assessment program that confirms upstream consent for every location dataset ingested, (iii) consumer access, deletion, and consent-withdrawal mechanisms, (iv) deidentification within 90 days of all historical precise-location data for which consent cannot be confirmed, and (v) an FTC incident-reporting obligation when a downstream third party violates the contractual consent terms. This is the first federal consent-decree-grade articulation of the consent standard for the precise-location-data resale market and now sets the de-facto federal compliance bar for every adtech/data-broker SDK that participates in that market.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHO IS AFFECTED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Applicability test:  Direct order binds Kochava and CDS only. **De-facto applicability** to: (a) any registered data broker that resells precise mobile-device location data; (b) any SDK / ad-tech vendor that supplies precise location data into resale chains (mobile-measurement partners, supply-side ad platforms, mobile attribution kits, weather/utility SDKs that monetise location); (c) any publisher or app operator whose CMP gates downstream sale of precise location data. The FTC has signalled this is the consent standard it will apply prospectively under Section 5; non-Kochava actors who deviate face deceptive-practice / unfair-practice exposure on the same theory.
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: any tenant operating mobile apps in the US that ingests, sets, or transmits precise-location SDKs (location-aware advertising, retail-foot-traffic analytics, weather, mapping/POI). Not limited to data brokers — the supplier-assessment requirement flows the obligation upstream to every CMP that disclosed those vendors and downstream to every customer that purchased the data.
- Customer notice required:  YES — within 30 days for tenants whose vendor list includes precise-location-data SDKs. Notice should explain (a) the new de-facto federal "affirmative express consent" standard for precise location, (b) that ID-PRIVACY is updating sensitive-location handling, and (c) the action they need to take (re-prompt, vendor blocking, or upgrade to the new sensitive-location category).

ID-PRIVACY® IMPACT
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Affected subsystems:
  ☑ Consent Banner               — New "Sensitive Location" purpose category required as a separately-toggled, non-bundled choice in US jurisdictions; cannot be combined with general "Targeted Advertising" or "Functional" toggles. Affirmative express consent UX standard means a single combined "Accept All" cannot satisfy this on its own — the sensitive-location purpose must be presented with a clear, conspicuous disclosure of categories collected and purposes of use.
  ☑ Cookie Classification ML     — New classifier label needed for "precise location data tied to sensitive POI." Inputs: SDK vendor list, geofence proximity to POI categories from the order's enumerated list. Likely needs a maintained POI ontology (medical, religious, education-for-minors, shelter, military/LE) feeding into vendor-disclosure metadata.
  ☑ Consent Storage & Proof      — Proof-of-consent record must now capture: clear-and-conspicuous disclosure shown, categories disclosed, purposes disclosed, affirmative action timestamp, consent-withdrawal events, and per-vendor consent flag for sensitive-location data. This is stricter than the existing CCPA/state proof-of-consent schema; we likely need an additional `sensitiveLocationConsent` field with disclosure-text hash.
  ☑ Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP)— GPP US-National string and US state-specific strings need a sensitive-location signal bit (likely a US National extension; coordinate with IAB Tech Lab). Until GPP supports this natively, propagation must occur out-of-band via vendor SDK gating.
  ☐ Geo / Jurisdiction Routing   — No change (US-wide federal precedent; do not condition on state).
  ☑ Vendor / SDK Governance      — **Largest impact.** Required: (a) per-vendor flag identifying SDKs that touch precise location, (b) supplier-assessment workflow proving the upstream supplier collected affirmative express consent, (c) automatic gating of precise-location SDK firing until sensitive-location consent is captured, (d) a tenant-facing "sensitive-location vendor block list" that ID-PRIVACY maintains and ships, mapped to the FTC's six POI categories.
  ☐ DSR Engine                   — No new right created by the order, but the consent-withdrawal mechanism must integrate with the existing preference center.
  ☑ Audit / Logging              — New evidence requirements: log of sensitive-location consent prompts shown, action taken, vendor receipts, and proof of supplier-assessment completion. Retention should match a reasonable expectation of FTC investigation-window (≥3 years recommended).
  ☐ Cross-border transfer        — No change.
  ☑ Tenant Config / Admin UI     — New tenant toggles: "Enable sensitive-location consent flow (US)", per-vendor sensitive-location flag, supplier-assessment status indicator, sensitive-POI ontology selection.
  ☑ Documentation / DPIA / RoPA  — Update sample DPIA / RoPA templates to reference FTC v. Kochava as the controlling US standard for precise-location processing; add sensitive-location risk row.

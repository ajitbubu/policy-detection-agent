───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHAT CHANGED (≤4 sentences, plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Chile's Law 21.719 replaces its outdated 1999 data protection statute with a GDPR-aligned regime, creating Chile's first dedicated Data Protection Agency (APDP), introducing tiered administrative penalties, and — critically for consent management — explicitly prohibiting pre-ticked/pre-checked consent boxes. The law also directs the new Agency to issue cookie-specific guidelines, which had not yet been published as of this scan. This is a shift from a largely unenforced, GDPR-lagging regime to an active enforcement structure with an explicit banner-design rule (no default-on consent) taking effect 2026-12-01.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHO IS AFFECTED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Applicability test: Any controller processing personal data of individuals in Chile, including via cookies/online tracking where the resulting data is treated as personal information under the new law's broadened definitions.
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: Any ID-PRIVACY® tenant with Chile-facing properties — likely a modest but non-trivial subset of LatAm-operating enterprise tenants.
- Customer notice required: YES — by 2026-11-01 (30 days ahead of the 2026-12-01 effective date), especially for tenants whose Chile banner currently defaults to any non-essential category as pre-checked/opt-out.

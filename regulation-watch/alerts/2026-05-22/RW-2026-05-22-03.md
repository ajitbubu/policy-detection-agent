WHAT CHANGED (plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The CNIL published its first formal recommendation specifically governing invisible tracking pixels in electronic mail, extending the consent framework it applies to website cookies under Article 82 of the French Data Protection Law directly to email tracking. Previously, the consent requirement for email pixels was generally understood to apply but no detailed CNIL recommendation existed; this recommendation codifies the CNIL's interpretation and provides the operational framework for compliance. The recommendation requires prior, free, specific, and informed consent from the email recipient for most pixel tracking use cases (especially marketing open-rate tracking, click tracking, and profiling), and specifies that consent must be as easy to withdraw as it was to give — including the ability to withdraw pixel-specific tracking while continuing to receive emails. A narrow exemption is available for aggregate email deliverability measurement (identifying inactive recipients) provided it involves no individual-level profiling and the results are not shared with third parties. When collecting consent, organisations must provide a reject/withdraw mechanism of equal prominence to the consent mechanism.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHO IS AFFECTED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Applicability test:  Applies to any organisation (public or private) sending emails to recipients in France that contains invisible tracking pixels. Scope is not limited to French entities — any sender targeting French recipients is in scope under Article 82 (establishment or targeting test).
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: Tenants using ID-PRIVACY in email marketing, transactional email, or newsletter contexts with French recipient lists. While ID-PRIVACY's core platform is a web CMP, tenants may use it to manage consent records that feed email service providers. Tenants whose ESPs deploy open-tracking pixels need to confirm their consent records cover the pixel-tracking purpose. Tenants operating email preference centres through ID-PRIVACY need to add an email-pixel-tracking toggle.
- Customer notice required:  YES — within 30 days. Notify relevant tenants that the CNIL has formalised the consent requirement for email tracking pixels in France and that their email preference centre / consent records must reflect this purpose.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
ID-PRIVACY IMPACT
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Affected subsystems:
  [X] Consent Banner               — If ID-PRIVACY operates an email preference centre or a web-based consent interface that feeds email service providers, a new consent purpose "Email Open/Click Tracking" must be available as a toggleable option for the France jurisdiction. This purpose must be separately granular from "Email Marketing" (a user can consent to receiving marketing emails without consenting to pixel tracking).
  [ ] Cookie Classification ML     — Not directly impacted (email pixels are not web cookies). However, if the classifier is extended to cover "tracking technologies" broadly (which the CNIL recommendation applies to via Article 82), a future classifier enhancement for email pixel vendors may be warranted.
  [X] Consent Storage & Proof      — Consent records for France-targeted email pixel tracking must capture: the specific purpose disclosed (open-rate tracking, click tracking, profiling), the identity of the pixel operator/vendor, the timestamp of consent, and withdrawal events. If ID-PRIVACY's consent schema does not include an `email-pixel-tracking` purpose, it must be added for tenants managing email consent in France.
  [ ] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP) — No change. GPC/GPP are web-layer signals; not applicable to email pixels.
  [X] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing   — The France (FR) jurisdiction profile should be reviewed to confirm that the `email-pixel-tracking` purpose category is surfaced in preference centres served to French recipients.
  [X] Vendor / SDK Governance      — Email service providers (ESPs) and email analytics vendors that deploy tracking pixels are now governed tracking technology vendors for French compliance purposes. ID-PRIVACY's vendor library should include major ESP pixel operators (Mailchimp, Brevo, Salesforce Marketing Cloud, HubSpot, etc.) with a `deploys-email-tracking-pixel: true` flag. Tenants should be able to gate pixel-vendor activation on email-pixel-tracking consent.
  [ ] DSR Engine                   — No new DSR right created. Existing GDPR access/deletion rights cover pixel-collected data.
  [X] Audit / Logging              — Consent records for email-pixel-tracking must be retained as proof of consent with the same rigour as web consent records. Withdrawal events must be logged and the withdrawal must propagate to the ESP within a reasonable time (CNIL standard: "without undue delay").
  [ ] Cross-border transfer        — No change.
  [X] Tenant Config / Admin UI     — Add `email-pixel-tracking` as a configurable consent purpose in the tenant admin UI, with France (FR) as the default applicable jurisdiction. Allow tenants to map this purpose to their ESP vendor(s).
  [X] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA  — Update the FR jurisdiction documentation to reference the CNIL recommendation. Add guidance notes for tenants operating email preference centres.

RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The CNIL issued approximately EUR 486.8 million in fines in 2025 and is actively enforcing cookie/tracker rules. While this recommendation is non-binding, it articulates the interpretation the CNIL will apply in enforcement actions under Article 82 — which carries the same fine ceiling as GDPR violations (EUR 20 million or 4% of global turnover). Tenants sending tracked emails to France without pixel-specific consent are operating on the assumption the CNIL has not yet enforced this specific rule, which is no longer a safe assumption now that a formal recommendation exists. DataSafeguard's role as a consent management platform means tenants will look to ID-PRIVACY to surface and manage this new purpose.

WHAT CHANGED (plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The Italian Garante published binding guidelines (Provision No. 284) requiring prior, free, specific, and informed consent for the use of tracking pixels in emails — applying the same consent standard Italy uses for website cookies under Article 122 of the Italian Privacy Code (implementing ePrivacy Directive Article 5(3)) directly and explicitly to email pixels. This is a binding provision, unlike the CNIL's recommendation; non-compliance after the six-month transitional period constitutes a direct violation of Italian privacy law. The guidelines state that pixel tracking is lawful "only on the condition that the recipient is informed in advance," regardless of the communication purpose or sender type. Narrow exemptions include: statistical measurement of aggregate email deliverability (open-rate counting without individual profiling), security/authentication protocols, and mandatory institutional communications (fraud alerts, contractual notices). For existing email deployments, organisations have until 28 October 2026 to: (a) inform recipients of the pixel use at the first available opportunity, (b) implement a withdrawal mechanism that allows recipients to stop pixel tracking without stopping email receipt. Users must be able to make selective choices — stop pixel tracking only, or stop all tracking-based email.

RISK IF NOT ACTIONED

This is a binding Garante provision published in the Official Gazette, not non-binding guidance. Non-compliance after 2026-10-28 for existing deployments is a direct violation of Italian privacy law, carrying GDPR-level fines (up to EUR 20 million or 4% of global annual turnover) under the Italian Privacy Code. The Garante is one of Europe's most active enforcement authorities — it issued 83 fines and a EUR 12.5 million fine in April 2026 alone. The six-month transitional window is generous but finite; tenants who have not implemented withdrawal mechanisms by the deadline face immediate enforcement risk. DataSafeguard's responsibility as a CMP provider is to surface this requirement and offer tenants the tooling to comply.

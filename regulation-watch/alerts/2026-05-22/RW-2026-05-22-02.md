───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHAT CHANGED (plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The ICO published its final, comprehensive guidance on Storage and Access Technologies (SATs) on 29 April 2026, replacing the prior detailed cookies guidance and broadening scope to cover all tracking technologies including pixels, device fingerprinting, web storage, and tag-based scripts. The guidance incorporates three new consent exemptions introduced by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (effective 5 February 2026): (1) Statistical Purposes — aggregate analytics used solely to understand how a website is used (not user tracking or advertising); (2) Appearance — storing user display/accessibility preferences; (3) Emergency Assistance — locating a user who has requested emergency help. For these two non-communication exemptions (Statistical and Appearance), organisations must still provide clear information and a "simple means of objecting free of charge" — meaning an accessible toggle or equivalent must be available even where prior consent is not required. The guidance also adds two new chapters clarifying what "simple means of objecting" requires and how to handle a single technology used for multiple purposes. In parallel, the DUA Act raised the maximum PECR fine 35-fold to £17.5 million or 4% of global annual turnover, fundamentally changing the risk calculus for cookie non-compliance.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHO IS AFFECTED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Applicability test:  PECR applies to any organisation that places or accesses storage/tracking technologies on devices of users in the UK. Establishment in UK is not required — targeting UK users suffices. Covers websites, apps, email tracking pixels, and SDKs.
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: All tenants with UK traffic (effectively all international and UK-based tenants). Tenants using analytics without consent who want to rely on the new Statistical Purposes exemption must configure the UK ruleset to use the exemption with the "simple means of objecting" control. Tenants relying on preference cookies now have a cleaner legal path via the Appearance exemption. Tenants whose analytics go beyond aggregate measurement (i.e., user-level profiling) cannot rely on Statistical Purposes and must continue to collect consent.
- Customer notice required:  YES — within 30 days. Tenants need to understand (a) the new exemptions they may be able to use for analytics/preference cookies, (b) the obligation to provide a simple means of objecting even under the exemptions, and (c) the dramatically increased penalty exposure under the DUA Act.

ID-PRIVACY IMPACT
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Affected subsystems:
  [X] Consent Banner               — For UK tenants relying on Statistical Purposes or Appearance exemptions: (a) the banner/preference centre must include an "objection" control (toggle/button) for these categories, rendered by default in the "on" state, with users able to switch to "off" at any time free of charge; (b) the exemption toggle is not a consent toggle — it does not require a prior opt-in, but must offer a real-time objection mechanism. This is architecturally different from the existing consent-required flow. Need a new "exemption-with-objection" banner component variant for the UK-GB ruleset.
  [X] Cookie Classification ML     — The classifier must now support a new label: `analytics-aggregate-only` (eligible for Statistical Purposes exemption) distinct from `analytics-user-tracking` (requires consent). Analytics cookies that track individual users across sessions or are used in advertising cannot be classified as `analytics-aggregate-only`. Also need a new label: `appearance-preference` for the Appearance exemption.
  [X] Consent Storage & Proof      — For technologies deployed under an exemption, there is no consent record to capture — but the platform must log: (a) which exemption was applied, (b) whether a user objection was received, (c) the objection timestamp and what vendor firings were blocked. This is a new record type distinct from the existing consent-capture schema.
  [ ] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP) — No changes required by this guidance. UK has no GPC mandate equivalent.
  [X] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing   — The UK-GB jurisdiction profile must be updated to expose three new configuration options: enable Statistical Purposes exemption (on/off), enable Appearance exemption (on/off), enable Emergency Assistance exemption (on/off). Defaults should reflect the ICO's conditions (e.g., Statistical Purposes default-on only if the tenant's analytics tool is confirmed aggregate-only). Routing logic must gate these exemptions to UK-targeted requests only.
  [X] Vendor / SDK Governance      — Vendors flagged as analytics tools need to be sub-classified as either aggregate-only or user-level. Only aggregate-only vendors can be governed under the Statistical Purposes exemption; user-level analytics vendors must continue through the consent gate. Need a new vendor metadata field `analyticsType: aggregate | user-level` in the vendor library.
  [ ] DSR Engine                   — No new DSR obligations.
  [X] Audit / Logging              — New log event types: `exemption_applied` (with exemption type, technology name, jurisdiction), `exemption_objection_received` (with timestamp, user action, vendor firings blocked). Required to demonstrate compliance with the ICO's transparency and objection-mechanism requirements.
  [ ] Cross-border transfer        — No change to transfer mechanisms. Existing UK GDPR adequacy/SCC framework unchanged.
  [X] Tenant Config / Admin UI     — New UK-specific configuration panel: "PECR Exemptions" with toggles for Statistical Purposes, Appearance, and Emergency Assistance. Each toggle should link to the ICO guidance conditions. Multi-purpose technology scenarios (same cookie used for analytics and advertising) must be flagged in the admin UI — the advertising purpose cannot benefit from the Statistical Purposes exemption.
  [X] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA  — Update UK compliance documentation to: (a) reference the new guidance URL; (b) document the "simple means of objecting" obligation for exempt technologies; (c) note the 35-fold PECR penalty increase under the DUA Act.

RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The DUA Act raised the PECR maximum penalty from £500,000 to £17.5 million or 4% of global annual turnover — a 35-fold increase. The ICO has an active enforcement programme on cookies (it reviewed the top 1,000 UK websites in 2025). Any tenant deploying analytics or preference cookies without offering a "simple means of objecting" (for exempt purposes) or valid consent (for non-exempt purposes) now faces materially higher fines. DataSafeguard's contractual warranty of PECR compliance for its UK tenants requires the platform to reflect this guidance. The increased penalty ceiling also increases the attractiveness of subject complaints and enforcement referrals against non-compliant banners.

WHAT CHANGED (4 sentences, plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (DUAA, Royal Assent 19 June 2025) amended PECR effective
5 February 2026, introducing five new categories of cookies and similar technologies that are
now exempt from the UK consent requirement: analytics/statistics cookies (aggregate service
improvement), security/fraud-prevention cookies, functionality cookies, software-update cookies,
and interface-customisation cookies. However, the exemptions are conditional: each exempt
technology must be used solely for the stated purpose (no mixed use with advertising targeting),
and operators must clearly disclose the use and provide a "simple means of objecting, free of
charge." The ICO finalised authoritative guidance on 29 April 2026 clarifying how the objection
mechanism works (can be delivered via existing consent banners, toggles defaulted on but
user-reversible), and confirmed that mixed-use analytics (e.g. analytics that also feed
ad targeting) still require full consent. Separately, the DUAA raises the maximum PECR fine
ceiling from GBP 500,000 to GBP 17.5 million or 4% of global annual turnover (whichever is
higher), aligning PECR with UK GDPR enforcement levels effective 5 February 2026; and a formal
data-protection complaints procedure must be in place by 19 June 2026 (T-22 days from today).

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHO IS AFFECTED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Applicability test:  Any organisation that stores or accesses information on a device of
  a subscriber or user in the UK — i.e., all website operators and app publishers using cookies
  or equivalent technologies targeting UK users, regardless of where the operator is established.
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: All tenants with UK traffic (universal). Tenants
  currently requiring consent for analytics-only cookies may reconfigure to the new exemption
  (reducing consent friction). Tenants using analytics that also feed ad personalisation must
  NOT apply the exemption. All tenants must have a complaints procedure by 19 June 2026.
- Customer notice required: YES — by 2026-06-05 (two weeks before the complaints-procedure
  deadline). Notice should cover: (a) new analytics exemption and its conditions/limitations,
  (b) "simple means of objecting" requirement for exempt cookies, (c) elevated PECR fine
  ceiling, (d) complaints procedure obligation.

RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Fine ceiling for PECR breaches is now GBP 17.5 million or 4% of global annual turnover
(from GBP 500,000) as of 5 February 2026 — a 35x increase in maximum exposure. The ICO
has committed to active PECR enforcement in 2025-2026. Tenants incorrectly applying the
analytics exemption to mixed-use (analytics + ad targeting) cookies face immediate
enforcement exposure. The complaints-procedure obligation carries the same elevated fine
ceiling; absence of a documented procedure is a straightforward audit finding. Customer
contractual exposure: any tenant MSA warranting "applicable law compliance" is affected.
Competitive: UK analytics exemption is a revenue-positive feature for tenants (reduced
consent friction); competitors who ship it first will cite it in sales conversations.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHAT CHANGED (4 sentences, plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The CNIL's Deliberation 2026-042, published 14 April 2026, formally applies the Article 82
consent framework (France's transposition of the ePrivacy Directive Article 5(3)) to tracking
pixels embedded in marketing emails, treating them as regulated trackers equivalent to browser
cookies. The prior position was ambiguous — the CNIL's general cookie framework did not
explicitly address email pixels — meaning many organisations were deploying marketing-email
open-rate and click-tracking pixels without separately obtained pixel-specific consent. Under
the new recommendation, organisations must obtain prior, specific, and informed consent from
email recipients before inserting a tracking pixel into any email sent for marketing, analytics
of campaign performance, or personalisation purposes; the only exempt use is a narrow technical
deliverability pixel solely identifying whether an email was delivered (not read) to remove
bounced addresses. For email addresses collected from 14 April 2026 onward, compliance is
immediate; for pre-existing contacts, a three-month transition period runs to 14 July 2026,
during which organisations may continue pixel use only if they clearly inform recipients and
offer a genuine opt-out — after that date, full consent is required.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHO IS AFFECTED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Applicability test: Any organisation sending email marketing to French recipients using
  tracking pixels, regardless of where the organisation is established (ePrivacy territorial
  scope follows the location of the device receiving the communication, not the sender).
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: All tenants operating email marketing programs
  targeting France. Email marketing is adjacent to CMP scope: if ID-PRIVACY governs vendor
  consent (including email service providers or ESPs with embedded tracking), and if tenants
  use the platform for cookie/consent proof, the email-pixel consent record must also flow
  through (or integrate with) the CMP consent store. Tenants that use cross-channel consent
  (web + email) within ID-PRIVACY are directly affected. Tenants with France-only or
  EU-wide email programs that have not re-flagged tracking pixels as a separate consent
  purpose are non-compliant as of today for new contacts.
- Customer notice required: YES — by 2026-06-14 (one month before enforcement). Notice must
  explain: (a) the CNIL's new pixel classification as a regulated tracker, (b) the consent
  requirement and its timing, (c) the transition period for existing contacts ends 14 July
  2026, (d) how ID-PRIVACY's vendor governance / consent storage supports pixel consent records.

ID-PRIVACY IMPACT
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Affected subsystems:
  [X] Consent Banner               — If tenants use ID-PRIVACY to capture email sign-up consent
                                     (e.g. preference centre, web form overlay), a new consent
                                     purpose "Email Tracking Pixels — France" must be available
                                     as a separately-toggled, non-bundled purpose on the consent
                                     form (cannot be bundled with general "Email Marketing"
                                     consent). Affirmative, granular consent is required.
  [X] Cookie Classification ML     — Email tracking pixels are a new tracker category distinct
                                     from browser cookies. Classifier must be extended to detect
                                     and label email tracking pixels (typically 1x1 GIF or PNG
                                     requests in HTML email source, or redirect-tracking links)
                                     as `email-tracking-pixel` with sub-labels:
                                     `pixel-marketing-consent-required` vs.
                                     `pixel-deliverability-exempt` (FR).
  [X] Consent Storage & Proof      — A new consent record type is needed: email-pixel consent.
                                     Fields: email address (hashed), purpose (`email_pixel_FR`),
                                     consent timestamp, disclosure text hash, opt-out timestamp.
                                     This must be linkable to the subscriber's email address,
                                     not just a browser cookie ID. If the platform already stores
                                     email-based consent (e.g. for GDPR email marketing), a new
                                     purpose field is sufficient; if not, a new identifier model
                                     is required.
  [ ] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP)— Not directly applicable to email-pixel signals (GPC/TCF
                                     are browser-scoped). No change needed.
  [X] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing   — Add `email-pixel-consent-required` flag gated by
                                     `jurisdiction == FR` (or EU-wide conservative option).
                                     The recommendation is CNIL-specific but other national DPAs
                                     may follow; implement with a per-jurisdiction toggle.
  [X] Vendor / SDK Governance      — ESPs and email analytics vendors (SendGrid, Mailchimp,
                                     Braze, Klaviyo, Iterable, Salesforce Marketing Cloud, etc.)
                                     that inject tracking pixels must be flagged as
                                     `emailTrackingPixelVendor = true`. Gating: pixel-injecting
                                     vendors must be blocked for French recipients unless
                                     `email_pixel_FR` consent is on record. This requires
                                     integration between the CMP consent store and the ESP
                                     (e.g. suppression list API or tag-level gating).
  [ ] DSR Engine                   — No new rights created; existing right to withdraw consent
                                     applies.
  [X] Audit / Logging              — Log consent capture events for email pixel purpose (FR),
                                     opt-out events, and suppression actions. Retain for
                                     CNIL investigation window (minimum 3 years recommended).
  [ ] Cross-border transfer        — No change.
  [X] Tenant Config / Admin UI     — New purpose definition available: "Email Tracking Pixels
                                     (France — CNIL 2026-042)." Tenant must be able to activate
                                     this purpose, link it to their ESP vendors, and configure
                                     suppression-list export for non-consenting French recipients.
  [X] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA  — Add email tracking pixel to the DPIA risk matrix for
                                     France. Update the RoPA template to include email-pixel
                                     processing as a distinct processing activity with
                                     "consent" as the legal basis and the CNIL recommendation
                                     as the controlling guidance.

RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CNIL maximum fine for ePrivacy / Article 82 violations: EUR 20 million or 4% of global annual
turnover (GDPR-aligned via the French Data Protection Act). The CNIL has a documented track
record of issuing pixel-related enforcement orders (Google Analytics enforcement in 2022;
multiple cookie fines in 2021-2025). The email-pixel recommendation is the CNIL's first
formal extension of the cookie rule to email; enforcement activity is likely to be high-profile
as a statement case. Tenants who continue sending marketing emails to French recipients with
tracking pixels after 14 July 2026 without pixel-specific consent are directly exposed.
The three-month transition period is a hard deadline, not a soft one — the CNIL has stated
explicitly that sanctions are expected to begin after that date. Customer contractual exposure:
tenants using ID-PRIVACY as their consent management layer who are found non-compliant may
assert that the platform failed to support the required consent flow.

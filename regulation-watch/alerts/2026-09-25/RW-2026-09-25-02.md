WHAT CHANGED (≤4 sentences, plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Chile's Ley 21.719 (approved 2024, 24-month transition) fully enters into force on 2026-12-01, replacing the prior 1999-era data protection regime and Transparency Council oversight with a new dedicated regulator, the Agencia de Protección de Datos Personales (APDP). The new consent standard requires consent to be free, prior, informed, specific, and expressed through a clear affirmative act — implied consent, pre-ticked boxes, or "keep browsing = consent" patterns become invalid. Cookie banners must give "accept" and "reject" equal visual prominence/ease, and consent must be revocable "by means similar or equivalent" to how it was granted. The law also introduces formal cross-border transfer adequacy language that consumer-facing privacy notices must address when data leaves Chile (e.g., via foreign-hosted analytics/ad/payment vendors).

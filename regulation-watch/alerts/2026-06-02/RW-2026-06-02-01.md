---------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT CHANGED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Previously, CNIL guidance on the Article 82 French Data Protection Act (ePrivacy transposition) consent
requirement for cookies and trackers did not explicitly address email-embedded tracking pixels as a
distinct category. The CNIL adopted this recommendation on March 12, 2026 and published it April 14,
2026, treating email tracking pixels as regulated trackers subject to the same prior-consent
requirement as web cookies under Article 82. The core delta is that sending a tracking pixel in an
email to a French-resident contact now requires a separate, granular prior consent obtained at the
moment the email address is collected -- generic email-marketing consent is insufficient. Consent for
advertising pixel tracking must be separate from, and more specific than, consent to receive
marketing emails. Enforcement (formal notices, investigations, sanctions) begins July 14, 2026 for all
contacts regardless of collection date; the only grace period is a 90-day window (April 14 to July 14)
for controllers to notify and provide opt-out to existing contacts collected before April 14, 2026.

---------------------------------------------------------------
WHO IS AFFECTED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Applicability test:  Any organisation sending emails containing tracking pixels to recipients in
                     France, regardless of where the sender is established. Article 82 applies on a
                     terminal-equipment basis (the pixel fires on the recipient's device in France).
DataSafeguard customers likely affected: All customers using the ID-PRIVACY platform who (a) operate
                     email marketing to French residents and (b) fire tracking pixels (open-rate pixels,
                     advertising attribution pixels, profiling pixels) in those emails. This is a broad
                     cross-section of e-commerce, publisher, financial services, and SaaS customers.
Customer notice required: YES -- by 2026-07-07 at the latest (one week before enforcement date) to
                     give customers time to act. Customers need to know whether their email service
                     provider or marketing automation platform is capturing granular pixel-consent
                     separately from send-consent, and whether they can produce proof of consent per
                     data subject.

---------------------------------------------------------------
ID-PRIVACY IMPACT
---------------------------------------------------------------
Affected subsystems:

  [X] Consent Banner               -- Customers may wish to surface pixel-tracking consent as a
                                      distinct purpose in the consent banner (e.g. "Email open
                                      tracking" as a separate toggle), especially on email sign-up
                                      forms. Banner configuration must allow a purpose category for
                                      "email tracking pixels" distinct from "email marketing."

  [X] Cookie Classification ML     -- The classifier must recognise email tracking pixel tags
                                      (typically 1x1 GIF or transparent PNG with a unique URL
                                      containing a recipient identifier) fired via email clients as
                                      a distinct technology type separate from web cookies. If
                                      ID-PRIVACY scans email templates or ESP tag configurations,
                                      a new category "email_tracking_pixel" must be added.

  [X] Consent Capture & Storage    -- For French-resident data subjects, the consent record must
                                      include: purpose = "email_tracking_pixel", separate from
                                      "email_marketing"; timestamp of collection; form/source URL;
                                      the specific pixel vendor(s) consented to. Proof-of-consent
                                      must be linkable to individual data subject for auditability.

  [ ] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP) -- No direct change; GPC operates on web browsing context.
                                      However if a consent-or-pay or preference-signal model is
                                      used that encompasses email, the GPP string scoping may need
                                      review.

  [X] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing   -- The France ruleset must be updated to gate email tracking
                                      pixel firing on affirmative consent (jurisdiction == FR, or
                                      recipient_country == FR). This is distinct from existing
                                      cookie-on-web-page routing logic.

  [ ] Vendor / SDK Governance      -- No structural change, but the vendor disclosure for email
                                      pixel vendors (e.g. Mailchimp open-tracking, Salesforce
                                      Marketing Cloud, Braze, Iterable) must be added to the
                                      France-scoped vendor list if not already present.

  [ ] DSR Engine                   -- Consent withdrawal must be actioned for email pixels
                                      separately; this is an existing capability but the pixel
                                      category must be wired into DSR withdrawal flows.

  [X] Audit / Logging              -- CNIL requires proof of consent per data subject for pixels.
                                      Audit logs must capture pixel-specific consent grants and
                                      withdrawals with sufficient granularity to respond to
                                      regulator requests.

  [ ] Cross-border transfer        -- No direct change.

  [X] Tenant Config / Admin UI     -- New jurisdiction toggle: "Email pixel consent required (FR)"
                                      with a separate purpose category in the admin UI. Customers
                                      must be able to configure the email sign-up form consent text
                                      and the pixel firing gate from the tenant dashboard.

  [X] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA  -- RoPA entries for email marketing must be updated to reflect
                                      the email tracking pixel as a separate processing activity
                                      with its own legal basis (consent) and purpose, not bundled
                                      under "email marketing."

RECOMMENDED ACTIONS
---------------------------------------------------------------
ENGINEERING (Abhishek's track):
  1. Add "email_tracking_pixel" as a named purpose category in the consent purpose taxonomy and
     schema; gate pixel firing in the ID-PRIVACY tag-firing engine on affirmative consent for
     this category when jurisdiction == FR.
     -- est. 5 pts -- by 2026-06-30

  2. Expose a "pixel_consent_required_FR" jurisdiction flag in tenant configuration; default ON
     for all FR-scoped consent configurations. Gating logic: if pixel_consent_required_FR=true
     and recipient_country=FR and consent_status[email_tracking_pixel] != "granted", suppress
     pixel inclusion in email template.
     -- est. 3 pts -- by 2026-06-30

  3. Update the consent record schema to store pixel-specific consent separate from general
     email-marketing consent: add fields consent_purpose, pixel_vendor_ids[], consent_source
     (form URL or API call identifier), and consent_timestamp_pixel.
     -- est. 5 pts -- by 2026-06-30

  4. Build or extend the opt-out/unsubscribe link generation to include a pixel-specific consent
     withdrawal action (a "stop tracking my email opens" link in every marketing email to FR
     residents). This must be a single-click action per CNIL recommendation.
     -- est. 4 pts -- by 2026-07-07

  5. Extend audit logging to record pixel consent grant, withdrawal, and per-send pixel
     suppression events with data-subject linkability.
     -- est. 3 pts -- by 2026-07-07

ML (Sumeet's track):
  1. Add "email_tracking_pixel" as a classifier category; train on 1x1 GIF/PNG patterns in email
     templates and pixel URL signatures from major ESPs (Mailchimp, Braze, Iterable, SFMC,
     HubSpot). This is needed if ID-PRIVACY auto-classifies tags in email templates.
     -- est. 8 pts -- by 2026-07-07

PRODUCT / LEGAL:
  1. Publish a customer advisory: "Action required if you send marketing emails to French
     residents with open-tracking or advertising pixels -- CNIL enforcement begins July 14, 2026."
     Include step-by-step guidance on using the new email pixel consent purpose category.
     -- by 2026-06-16

  2. Update the France jurisdiction profile in the compliance documentation to cover email
     tracking pixels as a distinct consent obligation separate from the cookie consent regime.
     -- by 2026-06-23

  3. Legal review: confirm whether the CNIL recommendation is binding as formal guidance under
     French law (it is not a formal deliberation with binding force on third parties, but
     enforcement posture makes it de-facto binding). Recommend flagging to counsel.
     -- by 2026-06-16

DEPENDENCIES / BLOCKERS:
  * The recommendation is non-legislative (it is CNIL guidance, not a statutory rule), but
    enforcement after July 14, 2026 is explicitly signalled. Legal review should confirm
    whether Article 82 already required consent for pixels (most counsel say yes) such that
    this is a clarification, not a new obligation -- this affects urgency messaging to customers.
  * Customers must have an email service provider or marketing automation platform that can
    conditionally suppress open-tracking pixels per recipient consent status; confirm if
    ID-PRIVACY issues direct pixel suppression signals to ESPs or only manages the consent record.

---------------------------------------------------------------
RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
---------------------------------------------------------------
CNIL has issued 83 sanctions totalling EUR 486.8 million in 2025, with cookie and tracker
violations accounting for the majority. The maximum fine under Article 82 / GDPR is EUR 20 million
or 4% of global annual turnover. CNIL has a history of issuing formal notices followed by
public fines with short cure periods (historically 3 months). DataSafeguard customers who use
email tracking pixels without separate pixel consent for French recipients will be exposed to
investigation. DataSafeguard faces contractual and reputational exposure if the platform does
not provide the tooling to comply within the transition window.

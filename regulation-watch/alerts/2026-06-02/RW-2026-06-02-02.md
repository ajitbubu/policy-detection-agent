WHAT CHANGED
---------------------------------------------------------------
The ICO replaced its legacy detailed cookies guidance with finalised "Storage and Access
Technologies" (SAT) guidance on April 29, 2026, incorporating the five new consent exemptions
introduced to PECR by Schedule A1 of the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (in force February 5,
2026). The prior rule required consent for almost all non-strictly-necessary cookies; the new rule
adds three practically relevant exemptions -- (1) aggregate analytics/statistics cookies used
solely to improve the service (no advertising mix), (2) preference/appearance personalisation
cookies, and (3) emergency-assistance location identification -- where consent is no longer
required provided a "simple means of objecting" (opt-out) is offered and the technology is not
mixed with advertising or tracking purposes. The guidance also expands scope beyond cookies
to cover all storage and access technologies: tracking pixels, device fingerprinting, web storage,
and tag-based scripts. The maximum PECR fine has been uplifted from GBP 500,000 to GBP 17.5M or
4% of global turnover, whichever is higher, since February 5, 2026.

---------------------------------------------------------------
WHO IS AFFECTED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Applicability test:  Any organisation deploying cookies or similar storage/access technologies
                     on devices of UK-based users; the SAT guidance applies on a terminal-
                     equipment basis regardless of where the controller is established.
DataSafeguard customers likely affected: All customers with UK-scoped consent configurations.
                     Those currently requiring consent for pure analytics cookies (no ad mix) in
                     the UK may be able to remove that consent gate, but must instead offer an
                     opt-out. Customers using dual-purpose analytics + advertising tags must
                     continue to gate on consent.
Customer notice required: YES -- customers should be informed of (a) the new exemption categories
                     available to them for UK consent configurations, (b) the requirement to
                     provide a "simple means of objecting" for exempted categories, and (c) the
                     expanded scope to pixels and fingerprinting. No hard enforcement deadline
                     introduced by this guidance (DUAA penalties already in force since Feb 5).
                     Customer communication recommended by 2026-06-30.

ID-PRIVACY IMPACT
---------------------------------------------------------------
Affected subsystems:

  [X] Consent Banner               -- For UK-scoped banners, the banner logic must support
                                      "exempt with opt-out" as a third state alongside "consent
                                      required" and "strictly necessary, no consent." Analytics
                                      cookies classified as pure-statistics (no advertising mix)
                                      in UK context may fire without prior consent but must have
                                      an opt-out mechanism accessible from the banner or a
                                      persistent preference centre. Reject-all button must still
                                      cover the exempted-but-opt-outable categories. ICO also
                                      added sub-chapter on "simple means of objecting" -- the
                                      opt-out path must not be harder to find than the accept path.

  [X] Cookie Classification ML     -- The UK classifier ruleset must distinguish between:
                                      (a) analytics cookies that are pure-statistics (eligible for
                                      Schedule A1 exemption), (b) analytics cookies mixed with
                                      advertising (not exempt), and (c) preference/appearance
                                      cookies (exempt with opt-out). Training data should be
                                      updated to flag dual-purpose tags. The scope expansion to
                                      tracking pixels, device fingerprinting, web storage, and
                                      tag-based scripts requires classifier coverage for these
                                      technology types under UK rules.

  [X] Consent Capture & Storage    -- Where the exemption is used (no consent captured), the
                                      system must still record that the opt-out status was
                                      checked and that no objection was registered at time of
                                      fire. Proof-of-opt-out-availability must be auditable.

  [ ] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP) -- No material change; GPC is not recognised as a mandatory
                                       opt-out signal under UK law currently.

  [X] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing   -- UK ruleset must be updated: add three new processing
                                      states for UK: consent_required, exempt_opt_out_offered,
                                      strictly_necessary. Classification logic must route tags to
                                      correct state based on ML classifier output and admin
                                      override. Mixed-purpose tags default to consent_required
                                      regardless of analytics-exemption eligibility.

  [X] Vendor / SDK Governance      -- Vendor library for UK must be reviewed against the new
                                      exemption criteria. Vendors whose tags were previously
                                      requiring consent may now qualify for exempt-with-opt-out
                                      status under UK law; this must be surfaced in the vendor
                                      configuration UI so customers can make an informed choice.

  [ ] DSR Engine                   -- No direct change to DSR response windows or mechanics.

  [X] Audit / Logging              -- The guidance adds a new sub-chapter on multi-purpose use
                                      of the same technology. Where the same tag serves both an
                                      exempt purpose and a consent-required purpose, the audit
                                      log must record which legal basis was applied for each fire
                                      event. Mixed-purpose tag firing must be traceable.

  [ ] Cross-border transfer        -- No direct change.

  [X] Tenant Config / Admin UI     -- Add a UK-specific toggle in tenant configuration:
                                      "Apply Schedule A1 analytics exemption (UK only)." Default
                                      should be OFF (conservative) with documentation guiding
                                      customers on when it is appropriate to enable. The "simple
                                      means of objecting" requirement must be surfaceable as a
                                      configurable opt-out link or preference-centre entry.

  [X] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA  -- UK compliance documentation should be updated to reflect
                                      the new technology scope (pixels, fingerprinting, web
                                      storage) and the three-tier processing state model.
                                      Customer-facing compliance guides for the UK configuration
                                      must be refreshed.

---------------------------------------------------------------
RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
---------------------------------------------------------------
The DUAA has aligned PECR maximum fines with UK GDPR: GBP 17.5 million or 4% of global annual
turnover (in force since February 5, 2026). The previous PECR ceiling was GBP 500,000 -- a 35x
increase in maximum exposure. The ICO has signalled increased enforcement activity on cookies
and tracking. Failure to offer a "simple means of objecting" for Schedule A1 exempt categories
(if customers choose to use the exemption) would expose customers to ICO enforcement.
Additionally, continued classification of tracking pixels and device fingerprinting as outside
PECR scope (old cookies-only framing) would be non-compliant with the expanded guidance scope,
creating product liability risk for DataSafeguard.

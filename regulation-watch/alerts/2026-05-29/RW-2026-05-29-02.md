---------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT CHANGED (plain English)
---------------------------------------------------------------
In April 2024, the EDPB issued Opinion 08/2024 addressing
pay-or-consent (consent-or-pay) models specifically for "large online
platforms." On 12 May 2026, the EDPB published updated, expanded
guidance that removes the "large platform" size threshold and extends
the same four-criteria consent validity test to ALL online services
offering any pay-or-consent configuration. The new guidance also
expands the analysis beyond the strict binary "accept tracking OR
pay" model to cover multi-tier variants and provides operational
criteria that national DPAs can use directly in enforcement. Critically
for CMP design: (1) a binary pay-or-consent gate is invalid regardless
of platform size; (2) a three-tier model (accept tracking / pay for
ad-free / accept tracking-light alternative with reduced
personalisation) can produce valid consent if four cumulative design
criteria are met; and (3) the price differential between the paid
tier and the consent tier must be proportionate to the value of the
data processed.

---------------------------------------------------------------
WHO IS AFFECTED
---------------------------------------------------------------
- Applicability test:  Any controller offering services to EU/EEA
  data subjects who operates ANY pay-or-consent gate (binary or
  tiered) -- now regardless of platform size.
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: All customers operating
  pay-or-consent or "consent wall" flows in EU/EEA jurisdictions.
  Previously, smaller customers may have believed Opinion 08/2024
  did not apply to them (it referenced large platforms). That
  exemption is now closed.
- Customer notice required: YES -- by 2026-07-01. Customers using
  a binary pay-or-consent flow must be told this is now non-compliant
  under extended EDPB guidance and that they need to either add a
  third tracking-light tier or remove the paywall gate entirely.

---------------------------------------------------------------
RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Under GDPR Art. 83(5), fines for invalid consent can reach EUR 20M
or 4% of global annual turnover. The CNIL has previously fined
Google EUR 150M and Meta EUR 390M for consent banner deficiencies;
it has demonstrated willingness to fine CMPs indirectly through
customer liability. Smaller platform customers now explicitly fall
within scope of the extended guidance; DPAs no longer need to argue
"large platform" to invoke this standard. Customer contractual
exposure to DataSafeguard is real if the platform ships banner
templates that produce legally defective EU/EEA consent.

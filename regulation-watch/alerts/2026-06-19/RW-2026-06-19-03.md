───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHAT CHANGED (plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
South Korea's National Assembly passed the most consequential rewrite of
the Personal Information Protection Act since the 2023 overhaul. The prior
maximum administrative penalty was 3% of total annual revenue; the amendment
creates a new tier of aggravated penalties reaching 10% of total annual
revenue for three specific scenarios: (1) repeated intentional or grossly
negligent violations within three years; (2) violations affecting 10 million
or more individuals; (3) failure to comply with a PIPC corrective order that
results in a data breach. The amendment also designates the CEO as personally
liable for the entity's data protection failures (statutory supervisory
accountability at the board level), and makes the ISMS-P (Information &
Personal Information Security Management System) certification mandatory for
entities meeting specified thresholds, with enforcement starting 2027-07-01.
The underlying PIPA consent and cookie framework is unchanged: cookies
collecting personal information require opt-in consent; privacy policies must
disclose automatic data collection devices (cookies, pixels, SDKs). What
changes for ID-PRIVACY is the exposure profile: a consent banner defect on
a large Korean platform could now attract a penalty 3x higher than before.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The penalty ceiling for a consent violation affecting 10+ million Korean
users rises from 3% to 10% of total global annual revenue on 11 September
2026. For a large global platform, this could represent hundreds of millions
of dollars. The PIPC has been increasingly active in enforcement (fine
calculation methodology tightened in May 2026). A cookie consent defect
that previously merited a manageable fine now carries catastrophic exposure
for any customer with significant Korean user bases. DataSafeguard has
contractual obligations to ensure its customers' consent flows meet applicable
law — a banner defect in KR-scoped configurations post-September 2026
creates contractual liability exposure for DataSafeguard as well.

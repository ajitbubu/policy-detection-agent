───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHAT CHANGED (plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
India's MeitY notified the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules 2025 on
13 November 2025, operationalising the DPDP Act 2023. For CMP operators,
the critical provision is Rule 4, which creates a statutory "Consent Manager"
role — an entity registered with the Data Protection Board of India (DPBI)
that acts as an intermediary through which Data Principals manage consent
across multiple Data Fiduciaries. Rule 4 takes effect 12 months post-
notification, i.e., 13 November 2026 (147 days from this scan). The prior
state was no binding consent management framework in India; the DPDP Act 2023
existed but lacked implementing rules. The new rules require that: (a) non-
essential cookies require opt-in consent before firing; (b) consent notices
must be in English and any other Indian language the user requires; (c)
consent must be withdrawable as easily as it was given; (d) consent records
must be retained for 7 years; and (e) the cookie banner must include granular
category-level controls. Importantly, ordinary enterprise CMPs (like ID-PRIVACY
deployed by Data Fiduciaries) are distinct from the registered "Consent Manager"
under Rule 4, but ID-PRIVACY's customers who wish to designate a Consent
Manager intermediary for their users will need to integrate with a DPBI-
registered entity by November 2026.

WHO IS AFFECTED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Applicability test:    Data Fiduciaries processing personal data of Indian
                       Data Principals, regardless of where the fiduciary
                       is established. Targeting test: any website or app
                       collecting data from users in India is in scope.
DataSafeguard customers likely affected:
                       All ID-PRIVACY customers with Indian user traffic
                       (any global e-commerce, publisher, or SaaS platform
                       serving India — a very large segment). India has
                       ~800 million internet users.
Customer notice required:  YES — by 2026-09-01. Customers need to know:
                       (1) their banners must meet DPDP Rules consent
                       standards by May 2027 at latest; (2) if they wish to
                       use a registered Consent Manager intermediary (optional
                       for most), they must plan that integration; (3) consent
                       record retention must be extended to 7 years.

                                   Indian Data Principals must be retained for

                                   identifier, timestamp, consent signal given,
                                   and language in which notice was provided.

                                   equal granularity.

                                   opt-in architecture is correct for India;

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Administrative penalties under the DPDP Act 2023 reach up to INR 250 crore
(approx. USD 30 million) for failure to take reasonable security safeguards,
and up to INR 50 crore for non-compliance with additional obligations including
consent requirements. While the DPBI is not yet in full enforcement mode,
Phase III (May 2027) is the hard deadline and regulators in India have signaled
serious intent. More immediately, DataSafeguard's customers operating in India
— including global tech platforms — face reputational and contractual risk if
their consent layer is found non-compliant. India's 800 million internet users
represent a commercially critical market for many customers.

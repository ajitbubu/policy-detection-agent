WHAT CHANGED (≤4 sentences, plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Australia's first binding, sector-specific children's privacy code must be finalized and registered no later than 2026-12-10. The Code applies not just to social media but to any APP entity operating a service "likely to be accessed by children" or "primarily concerning children's activities" — games, streaming, ed-tech, school management systems, even internet-connected baby monitors. It gives sharper, stricter teeth to existing Australian Privacy Principles specifically for minors, which in practice means default-off tracking/advertising cookies and SDKs for services in scope, age-appropriate consent flows, and tighter default privacy settings. This is a genuinely new, code-level (not just guidance-level) instrument tied to a fixed statutory registration deadline, not an open-ended proposal.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHO IS AFFECTED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Applicability test:  APP entities operating a social media service, relevant electronic service, or designated internet service that is likely to be accessed by children or primarily concerns children's activities — broader than "kids apps," captures general-audience services with material child usage.
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: Any customer with Australia-facing consumer web/app properties that could plausibly be accessed by under-18s (games, ed-tech, media, general consumer apps) — recommend a tenant scoping exercise now given the broad "likely to be accessed by children" test.
- Customer notice required:  YES — by 2026-11-10 (T-30), so customers can assess in-scope status and adjust default tracking behavior before the Code is registered.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHAT CHANGED (≤4 sentences, plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Chile is replacing its 1999-era privacy law with a GDPR-aligned regime: from 2026-12-01, consent must be freely given, specific, informed and revocable (not implied), six new/strengthened data-subject rights become enforceable, and a new independent regulator (APDP) gets audit and sanctioning powers (fines up to 20,000 UTM, ≈US$1.7M, with recidivism multipliers). There is no standalone ePrivacy-style cookie article — cookies and tracking technologies are swept in under the general lawfulness/consent/transparency/purpose-limitation principles, meaning any non-essential cookie or SDK placed on a Chile-facing property now needs a valid, revocable, purpose-specific consent record traceable to these new standards. This is a full regime replacement, not a guidance update — the old law is gone on 2026-11-30.

WHAT CHANGED (<=4 sentences, plain English)
---------------------------------------------------------------
The ICO published its final, authoritative guidance on storage and access technologies (SATs) on 29 April 2026, replacing all prior cookie guidance and broadening scope explicitly to cover tracking pixels, device fingerprinting, web storage, and tag-based scripts -- not just cookies. The guidance codifies five categories of SAT that are now exempt from PECR consent, two of them new under the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (DUAA): (i) statistical/analytics SATs whose sole purpose is improving the service (no ad targeting) and (ii) appearance/preference SATs whose sole purpose is adapting how the site looks or functions -- both requiring an easily accessible, free-of-charge "simple means of objecting" rather than prior consent. Two new sub-chapters clarify what that "simple means of objecting" looks like in practice (a visible, low-friction toggle or equivalent in the CMP interface -- browser settings alone do not suffice) and whether the same SAT can serve multiple purposes simultaneously (generally not, because purpose-bundling breaks the "sole purpose" condition for both new exemptions). The maximum PECR fine ceiling rose 35-fold from £500,000 to £17.5 million or 4% of global annual turnover (whichever is higher), effective 5 February 2026 under DUAA, making PECR enforcement now materially comparable to UK GDPR.

ID-PRIVACY IMPACT
---------------------------------------------------------------
Affected subsystems:
  [X] Consent Banner               -- Two changes required for UK-routed visitors:
                                      1. NEW EXEMPTIONS FLOW: For tenants using analytics-only cookies (no ad-targeting bleed) or appearance/preference cookies, the banner must shift from consent-capture to "inform + easy opt-out" UX. The current "Accept / Reject / Manage" layer 1 flow is still valid but the legal basis label must change from "consent" to "legitimate use under PECR exemption" for the exempted categories.
                                      2. "SIMPLE MEANS OF OBJECTING": The CMP must surface a visible, low-friction toggle (or equivalent) for the statistical and appearance exempted categories on a layer that users can easily reach. Browser settings cannot be cited as satisfying this requirement. The ICO has stated the mechanism can be embedded within the existing consent preference centre but it must be reachable without unreasonable friction.
                                      3. SCOPE EXPANSION: Banner copy and cookie declaration must now reference pixels, fingerprinting, web storage objects, and tag-based scripts, not only "cookies." UK-language template updates required.

  [X] Cookie Classification ML     -- Scope expansion requires the classifier to label SAT types beyond HTTP cookies: tracking pixels, device fingerprinting signals, web-storage keys (localStorage/sessionStorage/IndexedDB), and tag-based script injections. New label: `sat_type` with values [cookie, pixel, fingerprint, web_storage, tag_script]. The statistical-exemption classifier must also detect whether an analytics SAT has advertising-targeting bleed (if yes, exemption does not apply and consent is required). This is a training-data and label-schema change.

  [X] Consent Storage & Proof      -- Proof-of-consent records for UK visitors must now distinguish between: (a) full consent captured (for non-exempt SATs), (b) exemption-applied with objection-opportunity offered (for statistical/appearance SATs), and (c) no interaction required (for transmission/strictly-necessary/emergency SATs). A new `uk_sat_legal_basis` field is needed in the consent record, with values [consent, pecr_statistical_exempt, pecr_appearance_exempt, pecr_strictly_necessary, pecr_transmission, pecr_emergency]. Objection events (user exercised "simple means of objecting") must be logged separately.

  [X] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP)-- No direct change to TCF/GPP string encoding. However, the "objection" signal for PECR-exempt analytics/appearance categories is not currently represented in the IAB TCF string (which is a consent-or-no-consent model). ID-PRIVACY should file a product note: for UK tenants operating TCF, the "disclosedVendors" segment (mandatory since TCF 2.3 on 2026-02-28) and the new PECR exemption categories are not perfectly mapped. An interim out-of-band preference flag (`uk_pecr_objection`) should propagate to analytics/appearance vendor SDKs until IAB Europe addresses this in TCF.

  [X] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing   -- The UK ruleset in the jurisdiction router must be updated to: (a) activate the five-exception logic for all UK-routed visitors, (b) apply the "simple means of objecting" sub-flow for statistical and appearance categories, (c) block reliance on browser settings as the sole opt-out mechanism. This is separate from the EU/EEA ruleset (ePrivacy Directive applies there; DUAA/PECR applies to UK). UK-EU split routing must remain in place post-Brexit.

  [X] Vendor / SDK Governance      -- Vendor metadata must be updated to flag: `pecr_exempt_category` per vendor/SDK (values: transmission / strictly_necessary / statistical / appearance / emergency / none -- requires consent). SDKs flagged `statistical` or `appearance` must have the "simple means of objecting" propagation wired (i.e., if user exercises opt-out, the SDK fires a suppression signal). SDKs currently classified as "analytics" need a secondary check for advertising-targeting bleed to determine whether they retain the statistical exemption or require consent.

  [ ] DSR Engine                   -- No new rights created. Existing rights unaffected.

  [X] Audit / Logging              -- New evidence requirements: log entries for (a) SAT type deployed (extended taxonomy), (b) legal basis applied per SAT per visit, (c) "simple means of objecting" interaction events, (d) whether analytics/appearance SAT was validated as advertising-bleed-free at time of classification. Retention: ICO investigations typically look back 2-3 years; recommend 3-year minimum.

  [ ] Cross-border transfer        -- No change from this guidance.

  [X] Tenant Config / Admin UI     -- New tenant toggles needed for the UK jurisdiction: "Enable PECR statistical exemption" (with bleed-check attestation), "Enable PECR appearance exemption", "Simple means of objecting mechanism" (toggle / link / preference-centre path). Tenants must also be able to review and confirm their vendor SAT-type classifications before enabling exemptions.

  [X] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA  -- Update UK-specific DPIA and RoPA templates to reference DUAA 2025 as the legal instrument amending PECR, list all five SAT exemptions, and document the "simple means of objecting" mechanism for statistical/appearance SATs. Prior templates referencing PECR 2003 without DUAA amendment are now out of date.

RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Maximum PECR fine is now £17,500,000 or 4% of global annual turnover -- 35x higher than before DUAA. The ICO has signalled it will take a risk-based enforcement approach focused on the level of intrusion and adequacy of consent/opt-out mechanisms. UK tenants deploying analytics SATs without a "simple means of objecting" are operating outside the exemption criteria and therefore require consent, which most do not currently have structured as PECR-compliant. Tenants with advertising-targeted analytics (e.g., GA4 with ad features enabled, Meta Pixel, DoubleClick) cannot rely on the statistical exemption at all and must have full consent in place. The expanded scope to pixels, fingerprinting, and web-storage means that tenants who believed their consent obligations ended at cookie-level may be exposed on additional SAT types. Class-action plaintiff firms in the UK (following the CNIL Google/Shein enforcement pattern) will cite the ICO guidance as the compliance benchmark. ICO's own "online tracking strategy" published alongside this guidance signals active review of UK websites is planned for 2026. Competitors (OneTrust, Didomi, Usercentrics) will ship UK DUAA updates quickly; slow response is a sales and positioning risk.

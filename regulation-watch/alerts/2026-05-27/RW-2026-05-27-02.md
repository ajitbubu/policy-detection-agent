WHAT CHANGED (plain English)
---------------------------------------------------------------
The Minnesota Consumer Data Privacy Act (MCDPA, Minn. Stat. Ch. 325O)
has been in force since July 31, 2025. Until July 31, 2026, the
Minnesota Attorney General must give a business a 30-day written
notice and opportunity to cure any alleged violation before bringing
enforcement. After July 31, 2026, that cure period expires and the
AG may file civil action immediately without prior warning. The
practical delta for a CMP is: what was a "notify-and-fix" situation
becomes direct enforcement exposure in 65 days. No new law was
passed; this is a pre-set enforcement escalation built into the
original statute.

---------------------------------------------------------------
WHO IS AFFECTED
---------------------------------------------------------------
- Applicability test: Entities that (a) conduct business in
  Minnesota or produce products/services targeted to Minnesota
  residents, and (b) either control/process the personal data of
  100,000+ Minnesota consumers per year, or derive over 25% of
  gross revenue from selling personal data and process 25,000+
  Minnesota consumers' data.
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: Any medium-to-large US
  customer with Minnesota consumer traffic that has not yet fully
  implemented MCDPA requirements. Particularly at risk: customers
  who have not yet enabled the US-MN jurisdiction ruleset in
  ID-PRIVACY or who have a gap in their opt-out signal
  implementation.
- Customer notice required: YES — by 2026-07-01. Customers should
  be warned that the cure-period safety net expires 2026-07-31 and
  any remaining MCDPA configuration gaps (opt-out for sale/sharing,
  opt-in for sensitive data, UOOMs/GPC signal honor, privacy notice
  accuracy) must be closed before that date.

RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Minnesota AG civil penalties: up to $7,500 per violation. The AG
has joined multi-state enforcement actions on GPC signal compliance
(with California, Colorado, and Connecticut), demonstrating active
enforcement appetite. After July 31, 2026, there is no mandatory
30-day cure window — a single enforcement referral could trigger
immediate civil action against a DataSafeguard customer. Customer
contractual exposure: SLA obligations to deliver compliant CMP
behavior in all active jurisdictions. Reputational impact if a
customer receives an AG notice citing MCDPA non-compliance while
using ID-PRIVACY.

WHAT CHANGED (plain English)
---------------------------------------------------------------
The ICO replaced its legacy cookies guidance with final, binding
guidance on "Storage and Access Technologies" (SATs) on 29 April 2026.
The key regulatory delta is the introduction of five PECR consent
exemptions enacted by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (effective
5 February 2026), now fully explained in this guidance: analytics
(statistical purposes), appearance/UX adaptation, emergency
assistance, and the existing strictly-necessary and transmission
exemptions. Under the old PECR rules, first-party analytics cookies
required consent; under the new rules they do NOT require consent
provided (a) their sole purpose is aggregate site-improvement
statistics, (b) they are not used for advertising targeting,
cross-site tracking, or user-level profiling, and (c) the operator
provides clear disclosure and a free, simple means for users to
object. A new sub-chapter clarifies what "simple means of objecting"
means operationally (e.g., a toggle in the existing consent
mechanism). A second new sub-chapter rules that where the same cookie
or SDK serves an exempt purpose AND a non-exempt purpose (e.g.,
analytics + ad targeting), the exemption does not apply and full
consent is required. The maximum PECR fine has been raised 35-fold
from £500,000 to £17.5 million or 4% of global annual turnover
(whichever is higher), aligning PECR with UK GDPR penalty levels.

---------------------------------------------------------------
WHO IS AFFECTED
---------------------------------------------------------------
- Applicability test:  Any organisation that deploys cookies or
  similar technologies (pixels, fingerprinting, SDKs) on services
  targeting users in the UK, regardless of where the organisation
  is established.
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: All customers with UK
  traffic — approximately the full UK-enabled tenant base. Any
  tenant currently requiring consent for first-party analytics
  cookies in the UK may now be able to remove that consent gate
  (opportunity), but only after careful purpose-limitation audit.
  Tenants that dual-purpose analytics cookies (analytics + ad
  attribution) must maintain consent.
- Customer notice required: YES — by 2026-06-26. Customers need to
  know: (1) the analytics exemption option and its strict conditions,
  (2) the obligation to add a "simple means of objecting" if relying
  on the exemption, (3) the multiple-purposes rule that voids the
  exemption, and (4) the elevated fine ceiling.

---------------------------------------------------------------
ID-PRIVACY IMPACT
---------------------------------------------------------------
Affected subsystems:

  [X] Consent Banner
      UK jurisdiction ruleset must support a new "analytics exemption"
      consent mode. Where a tenant opts into the exemption, analytics
      category cookies must fire without prior consent but the banner
      must surface an opt-out toggle for the statistical/analytics
      purpose. The reject-all / accept-all flow for non-exempt
      categories is unchanged. Banner layer 1 must not suppress the
      analytics opt-out toggle behind a second layer.

  [X] Cookie Classification ML
      UK analytics cookie classification must be audited for
      purpose-limitation. Cookies that feed into advertising
      measurement or user-level profiling cannot be classified as
      "analytics-exempt" in the UK ruleset. The classifier needs
      a new UK-specific label: "analytics-exempt-eligible" vs
      "analytics-consented-required". Training data must include
      dual-purpose analytics+ad SDKs (GA4 with ad features enabled,
      Meta Pixel with conversions API, etc.) which must remain in
      the consent-required bucket.

  [X] Consent Storage & Proof
      For tenants using the analytics exemption: proof-of-consent
      records for analytics cookies must be replaced by proof-of-
      disclosure + objection-mechanism records. The consent schema
      needs a new event type: "sat-exemption-deployed" with fields
      for exemption category, disclosure text version, and
      objection-mechanism type. Existing consent receipt structure
      can remain for non-exempt categories.

  [X] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP)
      If a user sends a GPC signal, the platform must treat it as a
      valid exercise of the "simple means of objecting" for any
      active analytics exemption on UK-routed sessions. GPC handling
      logic for jurisdiction == UK must be updated to suppress
      analytics-exempt cookies on GPC=1, not just opt-out-sale
      categories.

  [ ] Cross-border transfer              (not affected by this change)

  [X] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing
      Add/update UK ruleset: analytics_consent_required = FALSE where
      purpose is strictly aggregate site improvement. Add toggle per
      tenant: "use_analytics_exemption_uk" (default OFF — tenants must
      opt in after their own purpose-limitation audit). Gate the
      multiple-purposes check: if a cookie vendor is mapped to both
      analytics and advertising categories, force analytics_consent_
      required = TRUE regardless of the toggle.

  [X] Vendor / SDK Governance
      Vendor registry must flag SDKs that are dual-purpose
      (analytics + advertising). These vendors must not be eligible
      for the analytics exemption in the UK ruleset. Vendor intake
      questionnaire must add question: "Does this technology also
      process data for advertising measurement, attribution, or
      user-level profiling?" Affected vendors include GA4 with Google
      Signals/ad features enabled, Meta Pixel + CAPI, Hotjar Heatmaps
      + Recordings if recordings feed ad retargeting.

  [X] Audit / Logging
      New audit event type needed: "uk-sat-exemption-asserted" with
      timestamp, cookie name, vendor, exemption category used, and
      disclosure/objection mechanism reference. ICO enforcement expects
      documented evidence that the exemption conditions were met.

  [X] Tenant Config / Admin UI
      New per-tenant toggle: "UK Analytics SAT Exemption" (off by
      default). When enabled, surface a checklist gate requiring the
      tenant to confirm: (a) sole purpose is aggregate site
      improvement, (b) no ad-targeting or cross-site use, (c)
      disclosure text updated, (d) objection mechanism live. The UI
      must block enabling the exemption if any vendor in the analytics
      category is also mapped to advertising.

  [ ] DSR Engine                         (not directly affected)
  [ ] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA        (DPIA may need update if
                                          relying on new exemption;
                                          recommend legal review)

RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Max PECR fine: £17.5 million or 4% of global annual turnover
(raised from £500,000 by Data (Use and Access) Act, effective
2026-02-05). ICO is actively reviewing the top 1,000 UK websites
for cookie compliance, with specific focus on analytics cookies
fired before consent. Customers using ID-PRIVACY who are found to
be firing analytics cookies without consent (and without the new
exemption mechanism) face enforcement. Conversely, customers who
COULD use the exemption but cannot because the platform does not
support it face a competitive disadvantage versus CMPs that have
already shipped the feature. Reputational risk to DataSafeguard
if a customer is found non-compliant while using the platform.

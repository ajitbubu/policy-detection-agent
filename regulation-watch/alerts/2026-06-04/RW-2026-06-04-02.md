---------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT CHANGED
---------------------------------------------------------------
On 14 April 2026, the CNIL published a formal Recommendation (adopted 12 March 2026,
Decision No. 2026-042) extending strict cookie-consent rules to email tracking pixels.
Previously, the regulatory status of open-tracking pixels in email was ambiguous; the
CNIL has now confirmed that inserting a tracking pixel in an email constitutes placing
a tracker under Article 82 of the French Data Protection Act (transposing ePrivacy), and
therefore requires prior, free, specific, informed, and unambiguous consent -- unless the
pixel is strictly necessary to enable or facilitate electronic communication or is
strictly necessary to deliver an online service the user explicitly requested. Consent
must be collected at the point of email-address capture, not inside the email itself (an
email opened before consent is recorded already fires the pixel, making in-email consent
mechanically invalid). Every marketing email must include a dedicated pixel opt-out link
separate from the standard unsubscribe link; clicking it must immediately halt pixel
firing for that contact.

---------------------------------------------------------------
WHO IS AFFECTED
---------------------------------------------------------------
- Applicability test:  Organisations sending emails to recipients in France that contain
  tracking pixels (open-rate pixels, click pixels, render pixels). Applies under
  territorial scope of French law / ePrivacy; targets all senders who track French
  residents via email.
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: Any customer using email-channel marketing with
  French recipient lists -- e-commerce, SaaS, media, B2B marketers. Email marketing
  integrations or SDKs that ID-PRIVACY governs are directly in scope.
- Customer notice required: YES -- URGENT. New contacts from 2026-04-14 must already be
  captured with compliant consent. Legacy contacts must be notified and given opt-out
  opportunity by 2026-07-14 (T-40 days from today).

RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
---------------------------------------------------------------
CNIL issued nearly EUR 500 million in fines in 2025 and has explicitly escalated cookie
and tracker enforcement. The Recommendation applies an existing enforcement framework
(Art. 82) to email pixels -- meaning CNIL can investigate and fine without a new
procedural step. Fine ceiling under GDPR: EUR 20 million or 4% of global annual turnover.
Customers sending pixel-enabled emails to French recipients after 14 July 2026 without
prior consent for legacy contacts will be in direct violation. DataSafeguard customers
who rely on ID-PRIVACY for consent management may have contractual claims if the platform
does not support the required email-channel consent capture and gating by the deadline.

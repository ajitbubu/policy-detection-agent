WHAT CHANGED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Oregon joined California, Colorado, Connecticut, and eight other states in requiring
businesses to honor universal opt-out signals (including GPC) as valid opt-out requests
for targeted advertising and sale of personal data, effective 1 January 2026. The Oregon
Consumer Privacy Act's 30-day cure period for UOOM non-compliance expired on 1 January
2026, meaning the Oregon AG can now pursue enforcement without prior notice-and-cure.
An additional and distinct requirement takes effect on 1 July 2026: controllers must
include specific information about recognized universal opt-out signal methods in their
privacy notices, disclosing that GPC or equivalent UOOM signals are honored. This
privacy-notice disclosure requirement is the immediately approaching deadline (T-27 days).
GPC signals from Oregon-located users must be honored within 15 days of receipt.

---------------------------------------------------------------
WHO IS AFFECTED
---------------------------------------------------------------
- Applicability test:  Controllers that determine purposes and means of processing the
  personal data of Oregon residents; that during a calendar year control or process
  personal data of 100,000+ Oregon consumers, or 25,000+ consumers while deriving 25%+
  of revenue from personal data sales. Targeting test applies.
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: Customers who operate websites or apps with
  meaningful US traffic and who monetise through targeted advertising or data sales.
  Customers already complying with California GPC requirements are largely covered on the
  signal-honoring side; the gap is the privacy-notice disclosure and Oregon-specific
  routing configuration.
- Customer notice required: YES -- advisory by 2026-06-18 informing customers of the
  July 1 privacy-notice update requirement.

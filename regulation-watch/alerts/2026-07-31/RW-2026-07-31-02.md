WHAT CHANGED (≤4 sentences, plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
South Korea's PIPA amendment (Act 21445, effective 2026-09-11) raises the maximum administrative fine ceiling from 3% to 10% of a violator's total revenue for the most severe categories of violation, and introduces statutory personal accountability for the CEO as the designated responsible officer for data protection compliance. It also mandates ISMS-P security certification for designated large-scale data controllers (deferred to 2027-07-01). While the headline changes are penalty structure and executive accountability rather than new consent-capture mechanics, existing PIPA consent rules — explicit opt-in when cookie/device data is combinable with other data to identify an individual, and separate consent required for sensitive data, cross-border transfer, public disclosure, and third-party provision — now carry dramatically higher stakes: a consent-flow defect that previously risked a 3%-of-revenue fine can now expose a company to a 10%-of-revenue fine if repeated or large-scale. This materially raises the cost of any gap in ID-PRIVACY's Korea consent-evidence trail.

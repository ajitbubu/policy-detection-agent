WHAT CHANGED (≤4 sentences, plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
As of 2026-07-01, a new national standard (GB/T 46903-2025) supplies the detailed methodology that Chinese personal-information processors must follow when performing the "compliance audits" already mandated since 2025-05-01 for any processor handling more than 10 million individuals' personal information (audits required at least every 2 years, or on-demand after an incident/complaint). The standard explicitly directs auditors to assess consent mechanisms, cross-border transfer bases, automated decision-making, and minors' data handling as part of the audit scope. Previously, the Audit Measures existed without a standardized methodology for what "adequate" consent-mechanism evidence looks like; this standard now gives regulators and third-party audit institutions a concrete checklist. For any DataSafeguard tenant with a large China user base, this raises the evidentiary bar for what the CMP must be able to produce on demand — not a new consent flow requirement, but a new audit-evidence requirement.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHO IS AFFECTED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Applicability test:  Binds personal information processors under PIPL (Art. 3 extraterritorial reach applies) that process personal information of more than 10,000,000 individuals — a China-market-scale threshold, not a China-incorporation threshold.
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: tenants with China-facing consumer apps/websites at meaningful scale (>10M China-based end users). Likely a small subset of the overall customer base, concentrated among large e-commerce, gaming, and consumer-app tenants.
- Customer notice required:  NO for the general customer base; YES (targeted) for tenants known to process China personal information at >10M-individual scale — by 2026-09-15, ahead of their next biennial audit window.

RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Direct fine exposure is bounded by PIPL's existing penalty ceiling (up to RMB 50 million or 5% of prior-year annual turnover for serious violations), not a new penalty introduced by this standard. Real risk is audit-failure exposure for large-China-scale tenants: if a tenant's biennial compliance audit (mandatory under the 2025-05-01 Measures) cannot produce evidence meeting the new standardized methodology, that itself can be treated as a compliance gap by CAC/provincial authorities, independent of any underlying consent violation. Reputational risk is low-to-moderate — this is a niche, audit-methodology-level change relevant mainly to large China-scale tenants, not a general customer-facing issue.

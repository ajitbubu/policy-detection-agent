WHAT CHANGED (plain English)
---------------------------------------------------------------
The ICO published its finalised Guidance on the Use of Storage and Access
Technologies on 29 April 2026 -- replacing the prior cookies guidance that
had been in place since 2019. The most material engineering delta is the
introduction of five new consent exemptions under the Data (Use and Access)
Act 2025 (effective 05 Feb 2026): technologies used (1) to transmit a
communication, (2) to provide a service the user requested, (3) to collect
aggregate statistical information to improve the service (analytics exemption),
(4) to adapt appearance to user preference, and (5) to identify a user needing
emergency assistance. The analytics exemption is strictly purpose-limited: it
covers only aggregate, non-identifiable data used solely to improve the service
-- analytics cookies that also feed ad targeting or third-party profiling do NOT
qualify. Additionally, the finalised guidance adds two new sub-chapters:
(a) what constitutes a "simple means of objecting" (reject path must require no
more steps than the accept path), and (b) whether a single technology can serve
multiple purposes (each purpose must independently satisfy the applicable rule).
Separately, the Data (Use and Access) Act raised the PECR penalty ceiling from
GBP 500,000 to GBP 17.5 million or 4% of global annual turnover (whichever is
higher), with a further DUAA obligation requiring all organisations to have a
formal data-protection complaints procedure in place by 19 June 2026.

WHO IS AFFECTED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Applicability test:  Any organisation established in the UK or targeting UK
                     users; PECR applies on an establishment basis, not a
                     targeting basis, but ICO enforces broadly against UK-
                     accessible services.
DataSafeguard customers likely affected: All customers whose CMP banners serve
                     UK visitors -- estimated 100% of enterprise tier, high
                     proportion of SMB tier.
Customer notice required: YES -- by 2026-06-10 at latest, to give customers
                     time to review cookie classification and confirm whether
                     their analytics tags qualify for the new exemption before
                     enforcement scrutiny rises post-29-April.

---------------------------------------------------------------
ID-PRIVACY PLATFORM IMPACT
---------------------------------------------------------------
Affected subsystems:

  [X] Consent Banner
      The guidance codifies "simple means of objecting" as a named requirement.
      Reject-all on layer 1 with equal prominence is now explicitly required in
      finalised UK guidance. Any banner variant serving UK visitors that buries
      the reject path must be corrected. New sub-chapter also clarifies that a
      single cookie serving multiple purposes must satisfy all applicable rules
      for each purpose -- relevant to any "functional + analytics" bundling in
      existing banner configurations.

  [X] Cookie Classification ML
      Five new exemption categories must be surfaced in the classification
      output and in auto-generated cookie policies. The analytics exemption has
      strict conditions (aggregate, non-identifiable, service-improvement only)
      that the classifier must test before applying the "exempt" label. Any
      cookie that also feeds advertising or third-party profiling cannot be
      classified as "analytics-exempt." Training data and label taxonomy need
      updating.

  [X] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP)
      No direct GPC signal change, but the expanded exemption regime means the
      geo-routing logic for UK visitors must now distinguish five exemption
      categories vs. the prior two (strictly necessary + explicit consent only).
      UK TCF string handling is not affected -- TCF is an EU instrument -- but
      UK-specific consent signals must route correctly to the updated ruleset.

  [X] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing
      UK ruleset module must be updated to reflect the five new DUAA exemptions
      (effective 05-Feb-2026, now authoritatively confirmed in finalised
      guidance). The prior UK ruleset assumed consent-or-strictly-necessary
      only; the new ruleset must evaluate each of the five exemption categories
      before presenting a consent prompt. This is a breaking change to the UK
      consent decision tree.

  [X] Vendor / SDK Governance
      Third-party analytics vendors must be reviewed against the new exemption
      criteria. Vendors that share data with ad networks or use it for
      cross-site profiling cannot be classified as exempt. Vendor risk scores
      and exemption flags must be re-evaluated for all UK-scoped vendors.

  [X] Audit / Logging
      The DUAA complaints procedure requirement (by 19 June 2026) means
      ID-PRIVACY must be able to export a consumer's consent history in a
      format suitable for a formal complaint response within 30 days of receipt.
      Audit log completeness for UK visitors must be confirmed.

  [X] Tenant Config / Admin UI
      New per-tenant toggle required: "UK analytics exemption" -- on/off -- with
      a sub-check that confirms the customer's analytics vendor meets the strict
      conditions. Default should be OFF (consent required) with an opt-in if
      the customer confirms their analytics is aggregate-only.

  [ ] DSR Engine                   (not directly affected by this guidance)
  [ ] Cross-border transfer        (not directly affected by this guidance)
  [ ] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA  (minor: update UK jurisdiction fact sheets)

RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Fine ceiling is now GBP 17.5 million or 4% of global annual turnover -- a
35-fold increase from the prior GBP 500,000 cap. The ICO has stated it will
use its new powers actively. UK-serving customers without a first-layer
reject-all button or who incorrectly apply the analytics exemption (e.g., to
tags that also feed ad networks) face enforcement risk. Any DataSafeguard
customer complaint to the ICO after 19 June 2026 without a formal complaints
procedure triggers automatic non-compliance. Reputational exposure is high
given ICO's public enforcement communications.

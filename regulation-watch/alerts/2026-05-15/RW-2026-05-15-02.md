WHAT CHANGED (plain English)
---------------------------------------------------------------
On 14 April 2026, the CNIL published Decision No. 2026-042 -- its first ever
formal recommendation specifically governing email tracking pixels. Prior to
this, email pixels occupied a regulatory grey zone under Article 82 of the
French Informatique et Libertes Act (the national implementation of Article
5(3) ePrivacy Directive): the CNIL had not issued specific guidance and the
practice was treated inconsistently. The new recommendation closes that gap.
Under it, inserting a tracking pixel in an email requires prior, free,
specific, informed, and unambiguous consent from the recipient, collected at
the time the email address is gathered -- unless the pixel is used solely to
enable or facilitate the electronic communication, or is strictly necessary
for a requested online service (a narrow deliverability-only exemption for
inactive-subscriber suppression). For contacts collected before 14 April
2026, organizations have until 14 July 2026 to inform subscribers of the
pixel use and obtain or re-confirm consent (or cease pixel tracking). For
contacts collected from 14 April 2026 onward, there is no transitional
period -- consent must be in place from the moment of subscription.

---------------------------------------------------------------
WHO IS AFFECTED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Applicability test:  Any entity subject to French law or to ePrivacy Directive
                     Article 5(3) as implemented in France that sends emails
                     containing tracking pixels to recipients. Applies to
                     DataSafeguard customers that (a) operate email marketing
                     in France or (b) deploy email SDK/pixel integrations for
                     French-resident subscribers.
DataSafeguard customers likely affected: Any customer using ID-PRIVACY's
                     vendor governance or SDK governance module for email
                     service providers (ESPs) with pixel tracking; French-
                     market customers sending newsletters, transactional, or
                     marketing emails.
Customer notice required: YES -- by 2026-07-01 (to give customers time to
                     update consent flows before the 14 July enforcement date).

---------------------------------------------------------------
ID-PRIVACY PLATFORM IMPACT
---------------------------------------------------------------
Affected subsystems:

  [X] Consent Banner
      Email tracking pixels are a consent surface distinct from the web
      cookie banner. If ID-PRIVACY generates or audits email consent flows,
      the email consent module (or the vendor governance "email SDK" check)
      must be updated to capture consent for tracking pixels at the point of
      email address collection. The pixel consent purpose must be listed
      separately from generic email marketing consent -- bundled consent
      is not valid under this recommendation.

  [X] Cookie Classification ML
      The classifier should be extended to flag email tracking pixels as a
      distinct tracking technology category requiring consent under FR law.
      Current taxonomy likely handles web cookies and web pixels; email pixel
      as a separate category with a France-specific consent obligation must
      be added. The narrow deliverability exemption (inactive suppression
      only) needs a corresponding exemption label.

  [X] Consent Capture & Storage
      Proof-of-consent for email pixels must now be captured and stored
      separately from web cookie consent. The CNIL requires consent to be
      obtained at the time of email address collection. If ID-PRIVACY provides
      a consent capture SDK or integrations with ESPs, those integrations must
      support a dedicated "email pixel tracking" purpose field and store the
      timestamp, IP, form ID, and purpose text at point of collection.

  [X] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing
      French jurisdiction ruleset must be updated to route "email tracking
      pixel" traffic to the new consent requirement. If ID-PRIVACY has an
      email consent module, it needs a jurisdiction flag for France (and
      potentially other EU states where similar DPA guidance exists).

  [X] Vendor / SDK Governance
      Email service providers (ESPs) that embed tracking pixels (e.g., via
      open-rate pixels) must be flagged in the vendor registry as requiring
      FR-specific consent gating. Vendor risk assessment templates must include
      the new "email pixel -- FR consent required" field. Any ESP that cannot
      disable pixel injection without a valid consent flag must be flagged as
      HIGH risk for French deployments.

  [X] Audit / Logging
      Consent for email pixel tracking must be separately logged and retrievable
      as proof-of-consent. The audit record must capture: purpose (email pixel
      tracking), collection channel (email subscription form), date/time,
      consent text shown to user, and the subscriber's identifier.

  [ ] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP)  (not directly affected -- email context)
  [ ] DSR Engine                      (existing access/delete rights sufficient)
  [ ] Cross-border transfer           (not directly changed by this guidance)
  [ ] Tenant Config / Admin UI        (minor: new FR email pixel consent toggle)
  [ ] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA    (update France jurisdiction fact sheet)

---------------------------------------------------------------
RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
---------------------------------------------------------------
CNIL fines can reach 4% of global annual turnover under GDPR / up to
EUR 300,000 under the Informatique et Libertes Act for ePrivacy violations
per event. The CNIL issued EUR 486.8 million in total sanctions in 2025 and
has explicitly stated enforcement will begin after 14 July 2026. DataSafeguard
customers who continue pixel tracking on non-consented subscribers after that
date face formal notices. If ID-PRIVACY's ESP vendor governance module does not
surface this risk, DataSafeguard could face contractual exposure as a
processor/subprocessor. Google (EUR 325 million) and Shein (EUR 150 million)
were both fined in a single day by CNIL in September 2025 for tracking
violations -- the regulator's appetite for enforcement is high.

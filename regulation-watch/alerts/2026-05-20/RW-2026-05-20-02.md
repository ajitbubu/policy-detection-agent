WHAT CHANGED (plain English)
---------------------------------------------------------------
Italy had no prior binding regulation specifically governing tracking pixels
in emails. The Garante's Provision 284 fills that gap and classifies tracking
pixels as a form of access to users' terminal devices, placing them under the
same ePrivacy / Italian Privacy Code regime that governs cookies (Art. 122 of
the Italian Privacy Code, which implements Art. 5(3) ePrivacy Directive).

Engineering-relevant delta: where tracking pixels are embedded in ANY email
sent to Italian residents, prior consent from the recipient is now required
UNLESS one of the enumerated exceptions applies. The only material exception
for commercial email is the "audience measurement for deliverability" exemption
which requires: (a) use of a single shared (non-individualized) pixel -- meaning
the same pixel URL for all recipients, not a unique per-user URL, AND (b) full
anonymization of all technical data captured (IP address, device type, open
timestamp, etc.). If either condition is not met, consent is required.

The prior rule: tracking pixels in emails were in a regulatory grey zone in
Italy -- no binding guidance existed, though general GDPR consent principles
applied to the personal data collected. In practice, most email marketing
platforms embedded unique per-user tracking pixels without explicit pixel-
specific consent.

The new rule: unique per-user tracking pixels in marketing emails require
prior, free, specific, and informed consent from the recipient. Consent can be
bundled into the marketing-communications consent (i.e., a combined "I agree
to receive marketing emails and to open-rate tracking") IF the request is
neutral and non-coercive. Withdrawal of tracking consent must be granular:
users must be able to stop pixel tracking while continuing to receive emails.

Transitional provision: for email lists collected BEFORE publication in the
Official Gazette (i.e., collected before 29 April 2026), a six-month window
is available -- until 28 October 2026 -- during which organisations must
(a) notify recipients of the pixel tracking at the earliest opportunity and
(b) implement a withdrawal mechanism.

WHO IS AFFECTED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Applicability test:  Any organisation sending emails to Italian residents
  that embed tracking pixels. Territorial scope follows the ePrivacy model:
  it applies where the device of the Italian recipient is accessed. Establishment
  in Italy is not required.
DataSafeguard customers likely affected:  Any tenant using ID-PRIVACY for
  web consent who also sends marketing or transactional emails to Italian
  users, AND whose email-sending infrastructure embeds tracking pixels (open-
  rate pixels). This is standard practice in all major ESPs (Mailchimp,
  Salesforce Marketing Cloud, HubSpot, Klaviyo, SendGrid, etc.). Affected
  segment likely includes a majority of tenants with EU/Italian traffic.
Customer notice required:  YES -- tenants must be advised of the new Italian
  email-pixel consent requirement and the 28 October 2026 deadline for
  bringing existing email lists into compliance. By: 2026-06-15.

ID-PRIVACY IMPACT
---------------------------------------------------------------
Note: email consent is not currently a core ID-PRIVACY subsystem -- ID-PRIVACY
manages web consent (cookie banners) primarily. However, the Garante's
classification of tracking pixels as equivalent to cookies has TWO direct
platform implications and one indirect customer-facing implication.

Affected subsystems:

  [X] Consent Banner
      If a tenant uses ID-PRIVACY to capture consent for email marketing
      (which is a supported use case in some configurations), the banner or
      preference centre must now include a separate, granular toggle for
      "email open-rate tracking (tracking pixels)" as a distinct consent
      purpose for Italian users. This cannot be bundled silently into
      "marketing emails" if it has not been. Gated by jurisdiction == IT.
      Specifically: the consent purpose taxonomy for Italy must be extended
      to include purpose ID "email-pixel-tracking-IT" with Garante-compliant
      disclosure language.

  [X] Cookie Classification ML
      The Garante's ruling treats email tracking pixels as devices-access
      technologies, conceptually equivalent to cookies. If ID-PRIVACY's
      cookie scanner/classifier ever scans email templates or ESP integrations,
      the "tracking pixel" classifier must flag unique-per-user image URLs
      as requiring consent in Italian context. More broadly, the classifier's
      definition of "tracking technology" must now explicitly include email
      pixels, not just web cookies and scripts.
      PRACTICAL NOTE: most email pixels are out-of-band from web cookies and
      the web scanner will not see them -- this is primarily a documentation
      and taxonomy update unless ID-PRIVACY adds email-template scanning.

  [X] Consent Storage & Proof
      For tenants who use ID-PRIVACY to record email-related consent, a new
      consent purpose record type "email-pixel-tracking-IT" must be storable,
      with: disclosure text shown, timestamp, withdrawal events. The withdrawal
      must be granular (pixel tracking off, email marketing on) -- a single
      "unsubscribe" signal that stops all email is not sufficient.

  [ ] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP) -- no change (email domain)
  [ ] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing
      IT jurisdiction rules already exist. The addition is to route "email-
      pixel-tracking" as a consent-required purpose within the IT ruleset.
      This is a configuration addition, not a new routing branch.
  [ ] Vendor / SDK Governance
      No direct web-vendor change, but tenants should be advised to audit
      their ESP integrations for unique per-user tracking pixel use.
  [ ] DSR Engine
      No new right created by the guidelines; the withdrawal-of-consent
      mechanism must integrate with the existing preference-center flow
      (already required under GDPR Art. 7(3)).
  [ ] Audit / Logging -- no new evidence requirements beyond existing consent
      records for the email-pixel purpose
  [ ] Cross-border transfer -- no change
  [X] Tenant Config / Admin UI
      Add "email pixel tracking (Italy)" as a configurable consent purpose
      in the IT jurisdiction section of the admin console. Allow tenants
      to enable/disable this purpose independently of the web cookie banner.
  [X] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA
      Update Italy-specific compliance guidance, CMP configuration guide,
      and sample DPIA template to reference Provvedimento n. 284. Add a
      knowledge-base article explaining the distinction between the
      "deliverability measurement" exemption (non-individualized pixel,
      anonymized data) and the consent-required case (unique per-user pixel).

RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Italy's Garante is one of the most active cookie/tracking enforcement
authorities in the EU -- it issued a EUR 12.5 million fine against Poste
Italiane in April 2026 (one week before these guidelines) for unlawful app
tracking. The guidelines are legally binding; non-compliance after 28 October
2026 exposes tenants (and potentially DataSafeguard as a CMP under GDPR
Art. 28 chain) to GDPR fines up to EUR 20 million or 4% of global turnover.
Italy has also demonstrated willingness to issue simultaneous fines and
operational orders (cease-and-desist). Customer contracts typically warrant
applicable-law compliance; tenants who rely on ID-PRIVACY for their consent
infrastructure will expect DataSafeguard to surface this requirement. Failure
to do so is a direct service-level and positioning risk.

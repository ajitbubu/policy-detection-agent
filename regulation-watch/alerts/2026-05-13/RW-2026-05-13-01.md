WHAT CHANGED (<=4 sentences, plain English)
---------------------------------------------------------------
The ICO published its final "Guidance on the use of storage and access technologies" on 29 April 2026, replacing the previous detailed cookies guidance and broadening scope to cover all tracking technologies by function -- cookies, pixels, device fingerprinting, web storage, and tag-based scripts. The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (DUAA), which took effect on 5 February 2026, introduced five new categories of storage and access technology that are now PECR-consent-exempt (analytics/statistical purposes, appearance/functionality, strictly necessary, emergency geolocation, and communication-enabling) -- but the analytics and appearance exemptions require a "simple and free" opt-out mechanism rather than prior consent, which is a new affirmative obligation for the CMP. The DUAA also extended PECR liability to "instigators" -- organisations that cause cookies to be set by a third party (e.g., an advertiser whose script fires on a publisher's site) are now directly within PECR scope, which means DataSafeguard customers' vendor governance obligations have expanded. The maximum PECR fine ceiling rose from GBP 500,000 to GBP 17.5 million or 4% of global annual turnover (whichever is higher), aligning PECR penalties with UK GDPR.

---------------------------------------------------------------
WHO IS AFFECTED
---------------------------------------------------------------
- Applicability test:  Any organisation that sets or "instigates" the setting of cookies or similar technologies on devices of users in the UK; territorial scope follows the user's device location, not corporate establishment.
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: All UK-active tenants (consent banners deployed under a UK ruleset); also any EU/global tenant that has UK traffic routed through the same banner configuration. The instigator expansion particularly affects tenants who rely on ID-PRIVACY to gate vendor/SDK firing -- they are now co-liable under PECR for unconsented third-party tracking.
- Customer notice required:  YES -- within 14 days. Customers must understand (a) the five new exemptions and the opt-out obligation they carry, (b) the instigator liability expansion, and (c) the fine increase.

ID-PRIVACY IMPACT
---------------------------------------------------------------
Affected subsystems:
  [X] Consent Banner               -- Two changes: (1) For cookies now relying on the analytics and appearance/functionality DUAA exemptions, the banner must still surface a "simple means of objecting" (opt-out link/toggle) -- this is NOT a full consent-gated flow but it is not silent either. ID-PRIVACY must support a new UK "exempt-with-opt-out" state distinct from "consent required" and "strictly necessary". (2) The expanded scope to all storage/access technologies means the banner's technology categorisation layer must cover pixels, fingerprinting, and web storage -- not just cookies -- under UK rules.
  [X] Cookie Classification ML     -- The classifier must now label technologies by their *function* (analytics, appearance, strictly necessary, emergency, communication, advertising) for UK purposes, not merely by cookie name/domain. The five new DUAA exempt categories must be discrete classifier outputs. Existing "analytics" category must be sub-divided: analytics exempt from consent but requiring opt-out vs analytics that remain consent-required (e.g., cross-site profiling disguised as analytics does not qualify for the exemption).
  [X] Consent Storage & Proof      -- For the new DUAA exempt categories, the proof-of-consent schema must store the opt-out state (not just consent-granted), the technology's stated purpose, and the exemption relied upon. This is a schema delta: `duaaExemptionApplied: string | null`, `optOutOffered: boolean`, `optOutExercised: boolean`.
  [X] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP)-- The IAB TCF string does not have a first-class signal for "exempt with opt-out"; propagation of user opt-outs for DUAA-exempt analytics must be handled out-of-band via vendor SDK gating until TCF adds support. Review whether the TCF "Special Feature" opt-out or a custom purpose is the appropriate mapping for UK analytics opt-out.
  [X] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing   -- UK ruleset must be updated to support three consent states for a given technology: (a) consent required, (b) DUAA-exempt with opt-out offered, (c) strictly necessary / no consent or opt-out required. Current routing likely only supports (a) and (c). This requires a new `duaa_exempt_with_optout` state in the UK jurisdiction configuration.
  [X] Vendor / SDK Governance      -- Instigator liability means ID-PRIVACY's vendor governance module must now flag which vendors are "instigated" vs "directly set" and confirm that DUAA-exempt status (if claimed) is legitimate. Tenants can no longer rely on "the vendor sets it, not us" as a PECR defence. Vendor categorisation metadata must include a `pecr_instigator_flag` field.
  [ ] DSR Engine                   -- No new rights created.
  [X] Audit / Logging              -- Log the exemption category relied upon for each technology fire under UK rules; log whether opt-out was offered, and if so, whether it was exercised. Retention unchanged.
  [ ] Cross-border transfer        -- No change.
  [X] Tenant Config / Admin UI     -- New UK-specific toggle: "DUAA analytics exemption" and "DUAA appearance exemption" on/off per tenant, with mandatory opt-out mechanism configuration. Admin must be able to set the opt-out surface (banner layer vs. preference centre link vs. dedicated settings page). UI must warn if a technology is marked DUAA-exempt but no opt-out mechanism is configured.
  [X] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA  -- Update UK DPIA template to reference the DUAA PECR amendments and the five new exemption categories; update UK RoPA template to include `pecr_legal_basis` field with new values.

RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Under the DUAA, the maximum PECR fine is now GBP 17.5 million or 4% of global annual turnover -- a 35x increase from the prior GBP 500,000 cap. The ICO has signalled continued active enforcement against UK websites' online tracking practices. The instigator liability expansion means ID-PRIVACY, as the platform that injects vendor scripts on behalf of customers, is now potentially in scope as an instigator under PECR -- this is a direct platform-level exposure, not merely a customer advisory. Failing to support the DUAA analytics opt-out mechanism means customers who rely on ID-PRIVACY for UK compliance are misconfigured even if they believed they were exempt from consent requirements under the new rules. The ICO has stated that 99% of the UK's top 1,000 websites now meet cookie banner standards -- enforcement attention will shift to the remaining 1% and to more complex tracking (fingerprinting, pixels, instigator chains). DataSafeguard customers in that tail are highest-risk targets.

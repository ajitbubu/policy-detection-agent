WHAT CHANGED (plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The NDPC issued the GAID on 20 March 2025, which became enforceable on 19 September 2025 after a six-month transition period. The GAID is the first binding implementation directive under Nigeria's NDPA 2023 and adds specific technical requirements that go beyond the NDPA's general consent principle. Prior to the GAID, cookie consent obligations existed under the NDPA but with no technical specificity. Article 19 of the GAID now mandates: (a) cookie banners must appear in the "first visible section" of the web page — bottom-of-screen placement is explicitly non-compliant; (b) all non-essential cookies require opt-in consent before placement (analytics cookies such as _ga and _gid are explicitly called out as non-essential); (c) pre-ticked boxes, implied consent through continued browsing, and inaction are explicitly invalid; and (d) the banner must identify which cookies are used, their purpose, who deploys them, and how users can withdraw consent. The NDPC has been aggressively enforcing since late 2025 — over 1,300 compliance notices issued in August 2025 alone, with fines reaching into the hundreds of millions of naira (Meta fined approximately $32.8 million for behavioral advertising without consent).

WHO IS AFFECTED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Applicability test:  Any organization that processes personal data of Nigerian data subjects, regardless of where the organization is established. Territorial reach mirrors GDPR's targeting test.
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: All ID-PRIVACY customers whose websites/apps receive traffic from Nigeria. Nigeria has approximately 220 million people; significant web traffic across Africa. Any customer with a continent-wide or global geo-target will be in scope.
- Customer notice required:  YES — immediately. The rule is already in force. Customers not yet compliant are exposed to enforcement today.

ID-PRIVACY IMPACT
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Affected subsystems:
  [X] Consent Banner               — NG jurisdiction banner must be repositioned to the "first visible section" of the page (top-of-screen or prominent central position). Bottom-of-screen banner renders non-compliant for Nigerian visitors. Button parity: banner must present an explicit "Accept" and "Reject" option — no implied consent on load. Must list categories of cookies, their purposes, and deploying parties. No pre-ticked boxes.
  [ ] Cookie Classification ML     — Analytics cookies (_ga, _gid, advertising pixels) already classified as non-essential — no change to classifier, but NG ruleset must enforce opt-in gate for these by default.
  [X] Consent Storage & Proof      — Proof-of-consent records for NG visitors must capture: category of cookies consented to, explicit withdrawal mechanism timestamp. Pre-existing implied-consent records are invalid — must be refreshed.
  [ ] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP)— GPC/TCF signals do not substitute for GAID opt-in — banner presentation is mandatory regardless of upstream signals for NG jurisdiction.
  [X] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing   — NG jurisdiction ruleset: create or update to enforce (a) top-of-screen banner placement, (b) no pre-ticked boxes, (c) explicit accept/reject required before non-essential cookie deployment. Confirm that NG is in the routing table and that it receives an appropriately strict ruleset (equivalent to or stricter than GDPR-level).
  [ ] Vendor / SDK Governance      — No new vendor-specific requirement beyond existing opt-in gating.
  [ ] DSR Engine                   — No change.
  [X] Audit / Logging              — Consent logs for NG visitors must record the affirmative action taken (not just "banner shown"). Pre-existing logs from before September 2025 derived from implied consent should be flagged as potentially invalid for NG jurisdiction.
  [ ] Cross-border transfer        — Not directly affected by this instrument.
  [X] Tenant Config / Admin UI     — Verify NG is a configurable jurisdiction in Tenant Admin UI. If banner-placement is controlled by a global default, ensure NG can override to top-of-screen positioning. Customers should be able to preview the NG-specific banner layout.
  [ ] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA  — Update NG jurisdiction guidance note. No DPIA template change required.

WHAT CHANGED (plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
On 31 March 2026, the OAIC released Australia's first-ever Children's Online Privacy Code (COPC) as an exposure draft under the Privacy Act 1988. The prior state was no specific code for children — child users were covered only by the general Australian Privacy Principles (APPs). The draft COPC introduces a "best interests of the child" standard as an overlay on APP obligations for any online service likely to be accessed by children (not just social media — explicitly includes apps, games, streaming, and educational platforms). Key consent changes: (a) use of a child's personal information for targeted advertising requires prior consent AND the information must be collected directly from the child; (b) operators must apply a "best interests" test before any data collection or use involving children; (c) children must be notified when parents consent on their behalf; (d) geolocation tracking of a child by other users (including parents) requires explicit disclosure on the service. The Code is binding once registered and breach is an interference with privacy under the Privacy Act, attracting fines up to AUD 50 million, three times the benefit obtained, or 30% of adjusted turnover.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHO IS AFFECTED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Applicability test:  Providers of social media services, relevant electronic services, or designated internet services (as defined in Australia's Online Safety Act 2021) that are "likely to be accessed by children." No minimum age threshold stated — operators cannot assume adult-only status without evidence.
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: Any ID-PRIVACY customer whose website, app, or service could plausibly attract children in Australia — this is a broad category that extends well beyond dedicated kids' platforms to mainstream consumer apps, games, news sites, and ecommerce. Customers serving Australian users should self-assess scope.
- Customer notice required:  YES — by 2026-10-10 (60 days before effective date). Customers need time to assess whether their services are in scope and to update consent configurations.

ID-PRIVACY IMPACT
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Affected subsystems:
  [X] Consent Banner               — AU-jurisdiction, child-likely-access services: banner must gate targeted advertising cookies/pixels on explicit consent AND direct collection from the child. Parental consent flows (where applicable) must include a notification step informing the child that the parent is consenting on their behalf. Banner must not use dark patterns or design choices that undermine the "best interests" standard.
  [X] Cookie Classification ML     — A new consent-purpose category is needed: "targeted advertising — child subject" with a stricter consent gate than standard advertising cookies. Classifier must flag cookies/SDKs used for behavioral/retargeted advertising in AU child-scope contexts.
  [X] Consent Storage & Proof      — Proof-of-consent records for child-scope services must capture: (a) age-assessment or likelihood-of-child-access determination; (b) whether consent was given by the child directly vs. a parent/guardian; (c) parent-proxy consent notification sent to child.
  [ ] Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP)— No direct change; but child-scope services cannot honor TCF strings as sufficient for targeting consent where a child subject is involved.
  [X] Geo / Jurisdiction Routing   — AU ruleset needs a sub-layer: "child-likely-access" flag per tenant service. When flag is set, apply COPC overlay rules. Default should be off; customers must self-certify scope.
  [X] Vendor / SDK Governance      — All third-party tracking SDKs in scope of child services must be re-evaluated against "best interests" standard. Data sale/sharing of children's data is prohibited by the Code; vendor contracts that involve data sharing for advertising must be flagged.
  [ ] DSR Engine                   — Deletion rights for children already exist under APPs; no material new window change, but deletion must extend to all downstream vendors holding children's data.
  [X] Audit / Logging              — Logs must record the age-assessment basis for each session in scope. Parental consent flow must be separately logged with timestamp of child-notification step.
  [ ] Cross-border transfer        — No new transfer mechanism requirement in the Code, but child data subject to COPC may not be transferred to fulfill targeted advertising purposes.
  [X] Tenant Config / Admin UI     — New service-level toggle: "Child-likely-access service (COPC Australia)." When enabled, activates: targeted advertising consent gate, parental consent with child notification flow, "best interests" data minimisation prompt, and vendor SDK block list for non-COPC-compliant trackers.
  [X] Documentation / DPIA / RoPA  — DPIA template for AU must include COPC best-interests assessment. RoPA must record the age-assessment basis and the child-scope determination for each service.

RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Once registered, COPC breach is an interference with privacy carrying fines up to AUD 50 million, three times the benefit obtained, or 30% of adjusted annual turnover — whichever is greatest. The Privacy Act's penalty structure has been toughened by the 2025 Privacy Act reforms already in force. The OAIC has signaled a proactive enforcement posture. Customer risk is high for platforms serving children in Australia that continue to run behavioral-advertising cookie stacks without child-specific consent. Targeted advertising on children without direct collection + consent is a bright-line violation once the Code is in force. Reputational risk for ID-PRIVACY if customers are found non-compliant while using our platform.

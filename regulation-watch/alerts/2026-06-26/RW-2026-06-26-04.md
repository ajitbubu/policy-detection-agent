WHAT CHANGED (plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
On 27 January 2026, Brazil and the EU simultaneously adopted mutual adequacy decisions: the European Commission adopted an adequacy decision recognizing Brazil's LGPD as providing equivalent protection to GDPR, and the ANPD adopted Resolution CD/ANPD No. 32/2026 recognizing the EU/EEA as an adequate destination for personal data under LGPD Article 33(I). The practical effect for ID-PRIVACY customers is that personal data — including user-consent records, cookie-associated behavioral data, and consent signals — can now flow between the EU/EEA and Brazil without requiring Standard Contractual Clauses, Binding Corporate Rules, or case-by-case ANPD authorization as a transfer safeguard. Before this resolution, every EU-to-Brazil or Brazil-to-EU transfer of personal data required a supplementary mechanism. The adequacy decision does not apply to transfers for public security, national defense, state security, or criminal investigation purposes. The decision will be reassessed within four years (by January 2030).

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHO IS AFFECTED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Applicability test:  Organizations that transfer personal data between the EU/EEA and Brazil in either direction. Applies to data controllers and processors established in either jurisdiction. DataSafeguard as a CMP processing consent data on behalf of clients who operate in both jurisdictions is directly in scope.
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: Customers with operations or users in both the EU/EEA and Brazil — particularly multinational platforms, ecommerce, ad-tech, and financial services. Medium-large segment of the customer base.
- Customer notice required:  YES — informational update (not urgent). Customers currently using SCCs for EU-Brazil transfers may wish to transition to the adequacy pathway. No action is required if they prefer to keep SCCs.

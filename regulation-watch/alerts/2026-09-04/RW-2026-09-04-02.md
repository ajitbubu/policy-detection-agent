⚠ This is a CORRIGENDUM to the Chile Law 21.719 item previously alerted three times (2026-08-14, 2026-08-21, 2026-08-28 → Jira IDP-13560). Genuinely new material fact this run: on 2026-09-01 the government filed a bill to postpone the law's own entry-into-force by one full year (2026-12-01 → 2027-12-01) and to expand the Agencia de Protección de Datos Personales (APDP) Directive Council from 3 to 5 members. This follows the Senate's repeated failure (most recently a reconvened tied vote in May 2026) to reach the two-thirds majority needed to ratify the three original Council nominees. This directly affects engineering roadmap sequencing for Chile-readiness work, so it is being escalated as a corrigendum rather than folded silently into the digest.

WHAT CHANGED (≤4 sentences, plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Previously: Law 21.719 was on track to enter into force 2026-12-01, with the APDP's founding Directive Council expected to be seated by October 2026 after Senate confirmation. Now: the Senate has twice failed to ratify Council nominees (insufficient two-thirds support), and on 2026-09-01 the executive responded by submitting a bill to Congress that (a) pushes the law's full entry-into-force back one year to 2027-12-01, and (b) increases the Council from 3 to 5 members — likely to make future confirmation votes easier to pass. This is a proposed bill, not yet enacted; until Congress acts, the original 2026-12-01 date remains the legally operative one.

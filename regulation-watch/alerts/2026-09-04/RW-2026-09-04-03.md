WHAT CHANGED (≤4 sentences, plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Previously: Australia's Privacy Act reform proceeded in a first tranche (already implemented, largely definitional/enforcement changes) with no confirmed timeline for further consent-mechanic changes. Now: the government has released a formal exposure draft for a second tranche — roughly 40 proposals — that would (a) require consent specifically for collecting sensitive information and for "trading" personal information, (b) tighten the statutory definition of consent to "voluntary, informed, current, specific, and unambiguous" (moving materially closer to an opt-in/GDPR-style standard), and (c) introduce a new "fair and reasonable" test under a revised Australian Privacy Principle 3, under which a privacy notice or consent flow alone may no longer be sufficient to justify a data practice. This is the single most consequential proposed change to Australian consent law since the Privacy Act's last major overhaul and is squarely in ID-PRIVACY's core scope (Consent Banner + Consent Capture).

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

ID-PRIVACY® IMPACT
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Affected subsystems:
  ☑ Consent Banner               — If enacted as drafted, would require a distinct, separately-toggled consent flow for (i) collection of sensitive information and (ii) "trading" of personal information, under a tightened consent-validity standard (voluntary/informed/current/specific/unambiguous) — likely incompatible with a single bundled "Accept All" for these two categories.
  ☑ Cookie Classification ML     — Would likely need a "trading" purpose classification distinct from ordinary third-party sharing/targeted advertising, to support the new consent-gating requirement.
  ☐ Consent Storage & Proof      — No detailed proof-of-consent schema published yet in the exposure draft; monitor for a "current" (i.e., refresh/expiry) consent-validity requirement, which would affect consent-record TTL logic.
  ☐ Consent Signals (GPC/TCF/GPP)— No proposal yet regarding a browser-level signal for AU consent.
  ☐ Geo / Jurisdiction Routing   — No immediate action; AU ruleset changes would be scoped once the bill stabilizes post-consultation.
  ☑ Vendor / SDK Governance      — The "fair and reasonable" APP 3 test could mean vendor disclosure/consent alone is insufficient for certain AU data practices — worth an early legal read on whether this affects the vendor-governance model, not just the banner.
  ☐ DSR Engine                   — No new right identified in this draft relevant to DSR specifically.
  ☐ Audit / Logging              — No new requirement identified yet.

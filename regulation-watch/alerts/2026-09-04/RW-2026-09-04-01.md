WHAT CHANGED (≤4 sentences, plain English)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
No new substantive change since IDP-13558: the amendment still raises the PIPC's maximum administrative fine from a percentage of violation-related revenue to up to 10% of a company's TOTAL global revenue for the most severe cases (repeat intentional/grossly-negligent violations, breaches affecting ≥10 million data subjects, or breach following non-compliance with a PIPC corrective order), makes the business owner/CEO the explicitly designated "ultimate responsible person," requires board-level sign-off on CPO appointment/removal for large controllers, and shortens breach notification to "without delay" upon awareness of the *possibility* of a breach. What is new in this scan: the effective date (2026-09-11) is now inside the 7-day trigger window, and PIPC's own site plus the Enforcement Decree draft cycle (drafts announced 2026-03-16 and 2026-06-02) confirm implementation is proceeding on schedule with no indication of delay.

WHO IS AFFECTED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Applicability test:  Any personal-information controller subject to PIPA — i.e., any entity processing personal data of individuals in Korea, including foreign controllers targeting Korean users (extraterritorial reach consistent with existing PIPA scope). Cookie/device identifiers are treated as personal information under PIPA where they enable identification, so consent-management practices for Korean-facing properties fall squarely in scope.
- DataSafeguard customers likely affected: any tenant with a Korea-facing consent banner/property, or any global tenant whose worldwide revenue would be the base for a 10%-of-total-revenue fine if a Korea violation is found severe enough to trigger the punitive tier — this materially raises the stakes of ANY existing Korea consent-configuration defect, not just new ones.

---------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT CHANGED (plain English)
---------------------------------------------------------------
Prior rule: Article 82 of the French Data Protection Act (loi Informatique et Libertes) applied to cookies and trackers on websites; its application to invisible tracking pixels inside emails was debated and not explicitly operationalised by the CNIL.

New rule: The CNIL formally extended Article 82 to email tracking pixels. Consent is now required before deploying pixels in email for purposes including advertising measurement, audience profiling, and general open-rate analytics -- unless use is strictly limited to deliverability management (adjusting send frequency or suppressing inactive recipients) or authentication security, both of which are narrow exemptions. For email addresses collected before April 14 2026, controllers must supply recipients with clear pixel-use disclosure and a right to object by July 14 2026. All contacts acquired from April 14 2026 onward must have a compliant consent flow from first contact -- no transitional period.

Why this matters for CMP: Vendors and clients using ID-PRIVACY to gate email-list consent must now extend the consent purpose taxonomy to cover email pixel tracking as a distinct purpose; the vendor SDK governance layer must treat email-deployed pixel SDKs the same as on-site cookie SDKs.

---------------------------------------------------------------
WHO IS AFFECTED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Applicability test:  Any controller established in France, or targeting recipients in France, who embeds tracking pixels in outbound commercial or transactional email. Territorial scope: France (Article 82 of loi I&L); overlaps with GDPR Art. 6 for controllers in EEA.
DataSafeguard customers likely affected: All customers with French end-users who operate email marketing programmes -- estimated broad applicability to customers using ID-PRIVACY for EU consent management.
Customer notice required: YES -- by 2026-07-01 (14 days before transitional deadline) to allow implementation.

---------------------------------------------------------------
RISK IF NOT ACTIONED
---------------------------------------------------------------
Article 82 formal notices carry no fixed fine ceiling independently, but violations are enforced under the GDPR fine regime via CNIL: up to EUR 20M or 4% of global annual turnover. CNIL has demonstrated willingness to fine for cookie/tracker violations at scale (EUR 150M against Shein, EUR 325M against Google in 2025). Customers failing to update email-pixel consent will face direct enforcement exposure. DataSafeguard faces contractual risk if the platform does not support the required consent purpose and audit evidence.

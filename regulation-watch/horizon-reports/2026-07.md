### 2.1 Global Privacy Control (GPC) — state recognition consolidation (LOW, informational refresh)

- **Source:** globalprivacycontrol.org; state AG guidance (CA, CO, CT); [Didomi GPC 2026 explainer](https://www.didomi.io/blog/global-privacy-control-gpc-2026); [Nelson Mullins GPC alert](https://www.nelsonmullins.com/insights/alerts/privacy_and_data_security_alert/all/get-to-know-the-global-privacy-control-gpc-state-attorneys-general-remind-consumers-of-their-right-to-opt-out-of-the-sale-or-sharing-of-personal-information)
- **What's changing:** As of 2026-01-01, twelve US states require recognition of a universal opt-out mechanism (UOOM)/opt-out preference signal, with California, Colorado, and Connecticut explicitly confirming GPC qualifies. CA, CO, and CT have reportedly announced a **joint investigative sweep** targeting businesses that fail to honor GPC signals — this is an enforcement-posture development worth tracking, not a new rule.
- **Expected timing:** Already in force; sweep activity is ongoing through 2026.
- **ID-PRIVACY relevance:** Per `gap-analysis.md` item 8, GPC ingest is assessed as "likely covered" as a core capability. This item does not change that assessment but raises the near-term priority of a **conformance re-verification**: run the GPC-honor conformance suite specifically against CA/CO/CT rulesets given the active joint sweep, and confirm the "Opt-Out Request Honored" visible-indicator requirement (new CA regulation, effective 2026-01-01, requiring a user-visible confirmation the signal was processed) is implemented — this specific visible-indicator sub-requirement was not called out in the original gap analysis and should be verified as a possible gap.
- **Recommended engineering follow-up:** Confirm CA "Opt-Out Request Honored" UI indicator exists; if not, this should be raised as a new MEDIUM/HIGH item at the next Tier 1 scan (it is a CA-specific regulation, not purely Tier 3, and belongs on the Tier 1 radar going forward).

### 2.2 Chrome third-party cookies / Privacy Sandbox — continued wind-down (LOW, informational)

- **Source:** [privacysandbox.google.com/blog](https://privacysandbox.google.com/blog/update-on-the-plan-for-phase-out-of-third-party-cookies-on-chrome); [chromestatus.com](https://chromestatus.com/features#milestone)
- **What's changing:** Google confirmed (April 2025) it will not deprecate third-party cookies by default and will not ship the "Privacy Choice" prompt; as of October 2025, Google is retiring a large set of Privacy Sandbox APIs and retaining only a narrow set (CHIPS, FedCM, Private State Tokens) focused on partitioning, sign-in, and fraud reduction rather than ad targeting/attribution replacement.
- **Expected timing:** Already largely settled; no near-term forcing deadline for ID-PRIVACY.
- **ID-PRIVACY relevance:** Reduces urgency of any "post-third-party-cookie" consent architecture work that may have been provisionally planned. CHIPS (cookie partitioning) remains relevant to Cookie Classification ML — partitioned cookies may need distinct handling/labeling if not already supported — but this is a maintenance-level item, not a forcing function.
- **Recommended engineering follow-up:** No urgent action. Re-confirm CHIPS-partitioned cookie handling is supported in the classifier at the next scheduled ML review; do not prioritize new "cookieless" architecture work based on Chrome deprecation timelines, since Google has walked that back.

### 2.3 Safari 26 / WebKit — Advanced Fingerprinting Protection (AFP) and URL parameter filtering (LOW, awareness)

- **Source:** [webkit.org/tracking-prevention](https://webkit.org/tracking-prevention/); [taggrs.io Safari 26 tracking changes](https://taggrs.io/safari-26-tracking-changes/)
- **What's changing:** Safari 26 (released 2026-05-11) ships default-on Advanced Fingerprinting Protection, blocking known fingerprinting scripts from certain device-detail APIs and from setting long-lived identifiers, plus URL tracking-parameter filtering and referrer restriction for known tracking scripts, in both normal and Private Browsing modes.
- **Expected timing:** Already shipped and default-on as of Safari 26 (May 2026).
- **ID-PRIVACY relevance:** This is browser-side blocking behavior, not a consent-signal or CMP-code change — no direct action required in Consent Signals or Consent Banner subsystems. Relevant only insofar as any ID-PRIVACY or tenant analytics/fingerprinting-adjacent vendor SDKs may see reduced effectiveness on Safari; worth a note in tenant-facing "known browser limitations" documentation.
- **Recommended engineering follow-up:** Documentation-only. Add a line to tenant-facing docs noting Safari 26 AFP may affect fingerprint-based (non-cookie) vendor identification methods some tenants rely on as a fallback.

### 2.5 ISO/IEC 27701:2025 and ISO/IEC 27018:2025 — standalone-standard revisions (MEDIUM — documentation, folded here)

- **Source:** [iso.org/standard/27701](https://www.iso.org/standard/27701); [iso.org/standard/27018](https://www.iso.org/standard/27018)
- **What's changing:** ISO/IEC 27701 was republished 2025-10-14 as a standalone Privacy Information Management System standard (previously an extension to ISO/IEC 27001/27002), now independently certifiable, with expanded guidance for cloud, AI-related processing, IoT, biometrics, and health data. ISO/IEC 27018 was republished as Edition 3 in August 2025, realigned to ISO/IEC 27002:2022, with a new Annex B for extended cloud-PII implementation guidance.
- **Expected timing:** Already published (2025); transition/re-certification windows for organizations currently certified against the 2019 edition are typically 2-3 years but should be confirmed with the certifying body.
- **ID-PRIVACY relevance:** Matches `gap-analysis.md` item 10 pattern (documentation/certification-only impact, no CMP code change). If DataSafeguard holds or is pursuing ISO 27701/27018 certification for ID-PRIVACY, Documentation/DPIA/RoPA and Audit/Logging subsystems should be reviewed against the new standalone standard structure, particularly the new AI-related-processing and biometrics/health-data guidance given ID-PRIVACY's biometric/neural-data classification work already underway per gap-analysis item 9.
- **Recommended action:** PRODUCT/LEGAL — confirm current ISO 27701/27018 certification status and whether re-certification against the 2025 editions is planned; no engineering action required unless certification scope changes.

## 3. Cross-cutting theme for the coming 6-12 months

Three of the items above (TCF v5.0.b, MSPA Fifth Amended/GPP signaling, GPC visible-indicator requirement) point to the same underlying trend: **industry frameworks and state regulators are converging on stricter, more auditable consent-signal fidelity** — not new consent *categories*, but tighter requirements that the *signals CMPs already emit* be provably correct, versioned, and (for GPC) visibly confirmed to the end user. This favors prioritizing:

1. Signal-version tagging across TCF, GPP/MSPA, and GPC in the audit/logging layer (a pattern already recommended in both HIGH alerts this cycle) — treat as a single cross-cutting engineering initiative rather than three separate one-off tasks.
2. A conformance-testing cadence (quarterly, at minimum) against IAB Europe's TCF validator and any equivalent GPP/MSPA validator, rather than only reacting to version-bump announcements.
3. Closing the CA GPC visible-indicator gap (2.1 above) promptly, since it sits at the intersection of a Tier 1 state regulation and a Tier 3 signal-framework item and is the most concretely actionable near-term item in this report.
